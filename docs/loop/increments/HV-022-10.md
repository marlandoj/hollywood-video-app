# HV-022-10 — The first film voiced by the operator's first vendor

epic: HV-022
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: G14-202609210000 (ElevenLabs primary, Azure as the fallback), already granted
spend_usd: 0.50 — **elevenlabs** text-to-speech only. No video or image provider is used: the picture
runs on `mock` for this proof, so nothing is spent on generation. The expected actual cost is about
**$0.01** (roughly 200 characters at the plan's $5 per 90,000); the figure above is the ceiling this
increment may not cross.

## Why

Five increments built the second voice vendor and none of them has spoken a word. HV-022-05 wrote
its contract, HV-022-06 its adapter, HV-022-07 the authorized-voice catalogue, HV-022-08 its own $25
budget line and HV-022-09 the crew's vendor order — all of it proved against fixtures, which is the
right way to build it and no proof at all that the service answers.

The operator's catalogue now exists on the host: **seven premade voices** authorized from the
account's own evidence, beside the three Azure ones, ten policies currently valid. So the question
this increment settles is the one no fixture can: with both vendors authorized and a real key, does
the crew cast ElevenLabs, does a take come back, does it carry the vendor's own cost, and does the
finished film play with it.

**The picture is deliberately not part of this.** Release 1 already proved a film end to end on live
paid generation, twice. Re-proving it here would spend one to two dollars of video generation to
learn nothing new, so the picture pool is `mock` for this run and the only live vendor is the one
being proved. That also keeps the run inside a figure small enough to be uninteresting: about a cent.

## What this increment does

No behaviour changes. This is a live proof and its record:

- **A short reel is run through the studio's own front door** on the staging host with the picture on
  `mock` and ElevenLabs authorized, every crew proposal accepted, exactly as `scripts/studio-run.ts`
  drives it. The script gives three speaking parts of different stated sex, so the crew's casting is
  visible rather than incidental.
- **`docs/evidence/release-2/elevenlabs-voiced.json`** records it in the `hv-live-proof/1` shape the
  Azure proof used (HV-022-03): the release, the catalogue and its evidence, which voice each
  character was cast, every job with its duration, each take's characters and measured length, the
  vendor line before and after, and the film's own spend.
- **`test/elevenlabs-voiced.test.ts`** holds that record to the criteria below, the way
  `test/release-1-run.test.ts` holds the Release 1 run. A proof nobody can re-read is not evidence.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **The crew casts the first vendor.** With both vendors authorized, every speaking character is
   cast an ElevenLabs voice, and each voice's sex matches the part's — from the account's own
   catalogue label, never from the voice's name. No character is cast on Azure.
2. **A take is dispatched and comes back.** Each line is one reservation and one dispatch on the
   vendor's own contract, the read returns at 44.1 kHz and is delivered at the studio's 48 kHz by the
   recipe the capability declares, and the recorded take length is within a second of the line's
   temporary read it replaces.
3. **The vendor's own line carries it.** The $25 ElevenLabs line moves by the run's holds and
   nothing else does; the line stays under both its $5 and $15 alerts; and the film's own spend and
   the month's are unchanged by the picture, which spent nothing.
4. **The film plays with the vendor's reads.** The shared cut carries the ElevenLabs audio, at a
   mean level in the same range Release 1 recorded, and the review link opens.
5. **The record is true.** Every job id, character, voice, hold and total in the evidence file is
   what the staging database and the ledger actually hold, and the test reads the file rather than
   restating it.
6. **Nothing else changes.** No source file outside `docs/evidence/**` and the new test is touched;
   the audio, crew-voice and budget suites pass unchanged.

## Afterwards

The host's picture profile is returned to `mock` and the run's profile is recorded in the evidence,
so the next increment starts from a known state. The ElevenLabs holds do not settle on their own —
like the Azure ones, they clear when the operator allocates the vendor's invoice, and that is
recorded as a known gap rather than quietly reconciled.
