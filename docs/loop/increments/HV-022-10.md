# HV-022-10 — An authorized voice of any vendor reaches its own contract

epic: HV-022
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

The first live ElevenLabs film stopped before a single take was dispatched:

> `/api/projects/:id/audio-takes -> 400 Choose a supported voice and language contract.`

Nothing was wrong with the casting. The crew had done its job exactly as HV-022-09 built it — three
speaking parts, three ElevenLabs voices, each matched to the part's stated sex from the account's own
catalogue label:

| Part | Introduced as | Cast |
|---|---|---|
| MARGUERITE | an older woman | Alice (female) |
| TOMAS | a middle-aged man | Bill (male) |
| PIP | a young man | George (male) |

and the profile it stored was a valid `hv-audio-voice/4`, which the validator accepts.

The route then threw that profile away and rebuilt one from the policy:

```ts
schema: policy.provider === "azure" ? "hv-audio-voice/2" : "hv-audio-voice/1"
```

That conditional was written when there were two vendors. A third arrives and it does not fail — it
silently hands ElevenLabs the **cartesia** schema, which `audioVoiceProfile` rightly refuses, because
`hv-audio-voice/1` means cartesia and nothing else. The same line appears twice, in the cast voice
route and the take route, and each also derived that vendor's extra controls for itself: Azure's
`style` and `intensity` were listed by hand in both, and ElevenLabs' `stability`, `similarity` and
`exaggeration` in neither. So a take of an authorized ElevenLabs voice could not be admitted at all,
and no fixture caught it because every fixture built its profile the way the contract says to.

The defect is not the missing branch. It is that the mapping from a vendor to its contract was
derived at each call site instead of stated once, which is what made adding the vendor look complete
when two routes still had no idea it existed.

## What this increment does

- **`AUDIO_VOICE_SCHEMA`, `AUDIO_VOICE_CONTROL_DEFAULTS` and `AUDIO_VOICE_CONTROL_FIELDS`** are
  declared once in `packages/planner/src/audio-performances.ts`, beside the validator that enforces
  them: each vendor's schema, the controls its contract carries beyond speed/volume/emotion with
  their defaults, and the same controls as field names for reading a caller's overrides.
- **Both API sites read the table** rather than re-deriving it — the cast voice route
  (`PUT /api/projects/:id/cast/:characterId/audio-voice`) and the audio-take route
  (`POST /api/projects/:id/audio-takes`).
- **No contract moves.** Every schema, control range and refusal is exactly what HV-022-05 declared;
  this only routes an authorized voice to the contract it already had.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **An ElevenLabs voice saves and reads back on its own contract** through the real cast voice
   route: `hv-audio-voice/4`, provider `elevenlabs`, with the vendor's stability, similarity and
   exaggeration present at their declared defaults.
2. **The vendor's refusals still hold** through that route: a loudness change and any emotion are
   both refused, rather than dropped or delivered at a level the service never applied.
3. **Every vendor the profile contract admits has a table entry**, and each entry builds a profile
   its own validator accepts with the controls that vendor's contract carries — so a fourth vendor
   cannot be half-added the way the third was.
   - Tests for 1–3: `packages/api/test/audio-studio.test.ts`, driving the real routes, with a new
     synthetic `test/fixtures/elevenlabs-audio.ts` policy that belongs to no account.
4. **Nothing else changes.** The audio studio, crew voice, Azure and localized audio suites pass
   unchanged, and no schema, control range or price moves.

The live proof this defect stopped is HV-022-11, which runs once this is deployed.
