# HV-016-34 blocked — The mixed-film suites spend their time in production's held checks, not in test waste

epic: HV-016
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0
date: 2026-10-01

## What was asked

Since HV-016-30 (PR #309), CI's `quality` step (`bun test packages test`) takes about 3 h 10 min
instead of about 80 min. Four files take about 132 of its 188 minutes:
`current-film-mixed-worker.test.ts` (77 min), `current-film-mixed-artifacts.test.ts` (23 min),
`current-film-v3-worker.test.ts` (22.5 min) and `current-film-mixed-archive.test.ts` (9 min).
The increment was to cut that time by removing waste in the tests (oversized media, a slow
fixture, wall-clock waits, polling, repeated hashing of large files, sequential uploads) while each
test keeps the same assertions on the same real paths. If the time turned out to be inherent, the
instruction was to record the profile here and stop.

## What the profile shows

Measured on this 2-core host, one test at a time, with nothing else running. The probe scripts
were standalone copies of the first `current-film-v3-worker` case (Bun's `--cpu-prof` does not work
under `bun test`), plus spies that count and time the held-check validators.

**The media is small, and producing it is cheap.**

- The source fixture builds in 12 to 14 s.
- The mixed preview has 3 slots and 180 frames at 640x360.
- Its originals are 11 files of about 1.3 MB. Its proof is 19 files of about 2.1 MB.
- ffmpeg and ffprobe (`spawnSync`, `spawn`) take about 4 s of the 468 s CPU profile.
- There are no sleeps or polling waits on these paths. The only timers are the worker's 1 s
  workspace scan and the 2 s access re-check in `withEditSourceAccess`.

**The time goes to re-validating large JSON envelopes at every access check.**

- The worker run for that one preview takes about 435 s.
- During the run, the worker's access check (`assertPendingContext`, in
  `packages/queue/src/worker.ts`) runs 887 to 904 times.
- Each check costs about 0.45 s. It runs `currentFilmV3HeldJob` (about 0.15 s),
  `assertCurrentFilmGenerationCurrent` (about 0.14 s) and `assertCurrentFilmSourcePermission`
  (about 0.07 s). Until proof is prepared it also runs `assertCurrentFilmMixedCurrent` (about
  0.33 s, which includes its own generation check).
- In total these validators take about 384 s of the 435 s worker run, about 88%.
- CPU profile, by self time: `JSON.stringify` 24.4%, SHA-256 `update` 17.0%, `structuredClone`
  12.5%, `getOwnPropertyDescriptor` 7.5% and `sort` 7.1%. `contentHash`, including what it calls,
  is 58% of all samples.

**Why each check is expensive.**

- I measured one warm check in isolation. It used a real held job captured from a worker run,
  after proof preparation. The check took 447 ms:
  - 164 ms is `editValidationKey` of the two separate inputs: the re-read current job and the
    claimed job. Each is about 2.5 MB of JSON, because it carries a 1.2 MB proof.
  - About 113 ms is the same content validated or hashed again within the one check:
    - 47 ms: the plan key and its clone in the generation check.
    - 36 ms: the project's screenplay library, validated three times.
    - 15 ms: the source job's key.
    - About 25 ms: origin, proposal and version hashes that the generation check and the
      source-permission check both compute.
  - The rest is rendering, materializing and clones.
- For scale, the 1,318 KB plan costs about 25 ms to `contentHash` and 34 ms to `editValidationKey`.
- That is by design. The validators recheck descriptors and complete content on every call (see
  `checkedCurrentFilmMixedJob`). `currentFilmAccess` says to "never retain a result, expiry window,
  project snapshot or cross-operation cache".

**Why there are so many checks.**

Each one is a deliberate fresh-authority check, made for every file, every 1 MB block, every
row, or on the 2 s timer. Counted by call site for one preview:

- 2 s timer in `withEditSourceAccess`, or a call with no synchronous caller: about 273 (30%).
- Per file in `current-film-copy-publication.ts`: about 250.
- Per file in `verifyCurrentFilmMixedMedia`: 113.
- The source verifier inside proof verification: about 120.
- Local proof measurement, per file and per block: about 38.

**The other suites have the same cause.**

- `current-film-mixed-artifacts.test.ts` uses no PostgreSQL or S3; it has a transactional
  transport double. Its first three cases took 215 s, of which about 144 s is the same validators
  (366 held checks).
- The double's per-transaction `structuredClone` of its state cost 8 s over 369 transactions,
  about 4%.
- On PostgreSQL every access is `PostgresArtifactStore.held`, which is the same validators plus a
  locked read. Every proof, origins and slot publication verifies the whole film twice: once before
  upload and once inside the held transaction.
- A mixed final's proof holds the preview and the preview's own proof, so its envelope and its
  verification tree are both larger. That is the 46-minute "admits and completes the genuine
  approved final" phase. HV-016-30 recorded it as a gap ("Caching a verified proof per job ... is a
  design change, not a port").

## What was tried and measured

- **Smaller media.** No help is available: media work is about 1% of the profile.
- **A smaller film.** A fixture variant without the inserted scene has 2 slots, 120 frames,
  9 original files and 17 proof files. Its worker run took 364 s instead of 435 s (16% faster), with
  785 checks instead of about 900. Most checks come from preparing and verifying the proof and the
  originals, and those barely shrink. This variant would also drop the inserted-scene film that
  the fixture exists to provide, so it was not adopted.
- **Sharing work between tests.** Each worker case proves a different interruption, resume or
  refusal on its own run, so no case's worker can be reused for another without changing what it
  proves.
- **The transport double's state copies.** They are 4% of that suite and are needed for its
  rollback isolation.

No change to the tests alone removes a substantial part of the time without weakening a test. The
time is production's per-access validation cost, paid faithfully by real worker runs.

## Decision needed (production design, not test setup)

Each option changes production code paths in `packages/planner` (validators), `packages/queue`
(worker access) or `packages/storage` (held checks). Each also goes against a rule stated in the
code, so a human should choose.

1. **Hash each envelope once per access check.** Compute each envelope's `editValidationKey` once
   per check, and let the nested validators use it instead of re-serializing the same plan, job
   and project.
   - This is still a fresh check every time, and nothing is kept between checks.
   - **Measured gain: about 25% per check, not 3 to 4 times.** An earlier draft of this doc
     estimated 3 to 4 times faster, on the wrong assumption that every hash was plan-sized.
   - Each check must still key both separate 2.5 MB jobs (164 ms), and the render work stays.
     Only the about 113 ms of repeated work can go, roughly 447 ms down to 335 ms per check. CI
     would drop from about 3 h 10 min to about 2 h 25 min.
   - An identity memo cannot achieve even this. Each validator layer clones its input with
     `structuredClone`, so the same content arrives as different objects. The duplicates would
     have to be passed down explicitly, changing `assertCurrentFilmGenerationCurrent`,
     `assertCurrentFilmSourcePermission`, `assertCurrentFilmMixedCurrent`,
     `validateCurrentFilmMixedJobPlan` and `validateProjectCurrentScreenplay`.
   - A faster `canonical` that produces identical bytes measured only 1.5 times faster on its own
     (62 ms down to 42 ms per 2.5 MB job).
   - It is a refactor across the validator chain (`currentFilmV3HeldJob`,
     `assertCurrentFilmGenerationCurrent`, `assertCurrentFilmSourcePermission`,
     `assertCurrentFilmMixedLockedTransaction`), and those are security-relevant.
2. **Verify proof once, then pin it.** Verify the full proof once at preparation. After that,
   check it against the artifact index digests instead of re-running full proof verification at
   every publication. HV-016-30 suggested this. It would remove most of the final's 46 minutes.
   It changes what each publication re-proves.
3. **A test-only access cadence.** Add a knob for the 2 s access timer in `withEditSourceAccess`,
   defaulting to 2 s, and have these suites set a long interval.
   - At most about 30% of the checks go away, roughly 25% of the time.
   - The real worker would then run at a cadence production never uses, and a case that relies on
     the timer to notice a mid-operation revocation would silently stop proving it. Not
     recommended.

`.github/workflows/**` is frozen, so splitting or sharding CI's `quality` step was not an option
here.

## Reproduce

Probe scripts and logs are in `/tmp/claude-0/probe-HV-016-34/` on the build host:

- `scripts/v3-preview.ts` with `bun --cpu-prof --cpu-prof-md`
- `scripts/v3-count.ts` and `v3-sites.ts` (validator and call-site counts)
- `scripts/count-preload.ts`, with `bun test --preload`, for the artifacts suite
- `scripts/fixture2.ts` (the 2-slot variant)

None of them is committed. To rebuild them, copy the first `current-film-v3-worker` case into a
plain `bun run` script, and wrap the four validators named above with `spyOn` timers.
