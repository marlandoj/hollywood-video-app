# HV-022-03 — The studio's films speak with Azure voices

epic: HV-022 (performance: voice and dialogue)
release: 1 "Studio" (build order step 6, third part)
gate: G11-202609191100 (Azure Speech, East US, the operator's key), G13-202609192200
spend_usd: 1
providers: Azure AI Speech, standard neural voices (`en-US-GuyNeural`, `en-US-DavisNeural`, `en-US-JaneNeural`)

## Why

HV-022-02 casts a production voice for each character, but nothing renders them. G11 says the build session writes the voice policy file.

## What this increment does

- **The operator's catalogue.**
  - `packages/generator/src/azure-policy-catalogue.ts` builds the three `hv-audio-policy/2` policies from saved evidence only:
    - the account: resource ID, tier and region;
    - Azure's own voice list for the region, which must list every authorized voice;
    - the licence and price pages that were reviewed.
  - The policies are valid for a year. Each take holds $0.03 and is capped at 1,500 characters.
  - `scripts/audio-policy.ts` fetches and saves that evidence on the host and writes the catalogue at mode 600. `--check` runs the application's validator.
- **The switch.** `scripts/staging-providers.py --voice azure|off`:
  - `azure` needs the operator's `HV_AZURE_SPEECH_KEY` and a private, valid catalogue;
  - it writes only `HV_AUDIO_POLICY_FILE`, and provider profiles keep it;
  - every deploy leaves it out.
- **The studio.** `approveRoughCut` → `voiceFinal`:
  - one take per voiced line, with fixed idempotency keys;
  - a wait for the takes;
  - a dialogue replacement of the final with this pass's usable takes.

  The voiced cut becomes `state.final`, which is the cut that is shared.
- **Spend.** A take's hold now counts toward the film's own limit, inside PostgreSQL admission (`PostgresAudioLedger.admitAudio`).
- **Docs:** `docs/STAGING-LOCAL.md` ("Production voices") and `docs/CREW.md`.
- **The live run.** The lighthouse reel with `live-film-anchored` and Azure voices. Evidence goes in `docs/evidence/release-1/live-reel-voiced.json`, and staging then returns to mock with voices off.

## What this increment does not do

- **The rough cut still speaks with the temporary voice.** Re-voicing the rough cut is a later step.
- **No new voices.** Adding voices changes the Azure capability revision, which is later work.
- **Invoices are not reconciled here.** The operator allocates them with `scripts/reconcile-audio.ts`.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **The catalogue.**
   - Three valid policies for a year, checked by the application's validator.
   - A new voice elsewhere in Azure's list changes nothing, while new licence or price pages do.
   - Missing or wrong evidence authorizes nothing.
   - Test: `packages/generator/test/azure-policy-catalogue.test.ts`.
2. **The switch.**
   - It needs the key and a private catalogue, and touches only its own line.
   - Profiles keep it, `off` removes it, and deploys leave it out.
   - Test: `scripts/test_storage_deployment.py` `test_azure_voices_need_the_key_and_a_private_catalogue_and_touch_only_their_line`.
3. **The studio.**
   - One take per voiced line, skipping characters without a voice and unavailable lines.
   - Fixed idempotency keys.
   - Only this pass's usable takes are laid.
   - The voiced cut is the one shared.
   - With no catalogue, nothing is recorded.
   - Test: `packages/frontend/test/studio.test.js`.
4. **The film limit covers takes.**
   - A take past it is refused and holds nothing.
   - Test: `packages/storage/test/audio-jobs.test.ts` (CI's PostgreSQL job).
5. **The final is a valid dialogue source.** It is carried and silent-captioned by HV-022-01.
   - Test: `packages/api/test/final-dialogue.test.ts`.
