# HV-025-09 — An editorial render gets time from its cut, and says where the time went

epic: HV-025
release: 1 "Studio" (closing gap), carried into 2
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

After HV-025-07 and HV-025-08 the Release 1 short's title pass got all the way to its last step and
then failed differently: the titled picture edit ran for **30 minutes**, hit the job's processing
deadline, re-queued itself, and started again. Left alone it would have done that all night, so the
operator's build session stopped it.

Two things were wrong, and only one of them is the render's speed.

- **The deadline was a constant.** Every editorial job was admitted with
  `HV_JOB_TIMEOUT_MS ?? 30 minutes`, whatever it was rendering. Thirty minutes is generous for the
  fifteen-second reel (its titled edit took 412 s) and impossible for the fifty-second short: the
  render is the whole conform, and `verifyEditMedia` reproduces that conform before the output is
  accepted, so the work grows with the cut and the deadline did not.
- **The trace could not say which half was slow.** The job's whole 976 s came back as one
  `job.process` span. Everything below it — preparing the sources, conforming, sealing, verifying —
  was invisible, so there was nothing to profile from.

## What this increment does

- **`editRenderTimeoutMs(frames)`** (`packages/planner/src/edit-resources.ts`): 120 s of start-up
  plus 2 s per frame, never below the old 30 minutes and never above 4 hours. Measured on staging, a
  titled cut costs on the order of a second per frame end to end, so this is about double the
  observed cost — a deadline, not a target. Picture edits and assembly edits are admitted with it,
  and `HV_JOB_TIMEOUT_MS` still overrides it for a host that needs a different bound.
- **Phases in the trace**: `processEditJob` records the render, the seal and the verification as
  their own `media.assemble` spans, carrying `hv.edit.phase` and the cut's `hv.edit.frames`. The
  telemetry attribute allow-list gains those two keys and the two editorial stages, so they survive
  into the exported span rather than being dropped.

This does not make the render faster. It lets a 50-second film finish, and it makes the next
increment — why a 50-second titled edit costs half an hour — a measurement instead of a guess.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **The deadline comes from the cut.** Zero frames and a short reel get the 30-minute floor; a
   fifty-second short with titles gets base plus two seconds a frame; a feature-length cut stops at
   the four-hour ceiling; a fractional or negative frame count is refused.
   - Test: `packages/generator/test/edit-resources.test.ts`.
2. **An admitted editorial job carries it**, and an operator override still wins.
   - Test: `packages/api/test/editorial-render-deadline.test.ts`.
3. **Each phase is its own span**, in order, with the stage, the job and the cut's frames on it.
   - Same test.
4. **Nothing else changes.** The editorial and observability suites pass unchanged.
