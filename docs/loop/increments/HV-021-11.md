# HV-021-11 — Continuity across a feature's sequences (Release 3 step 5)

epic: HV-021
release: 3 "Features"
gate: G20-202610031349 (Release 3's build order, row 5: "The Supervisor compares each sequence's last scene with the next one's first, as it does across a `CONTINUOUS` heading")
spend_usd: 0

## Why

Release 3 makes a feature as sequences, each rendered and approved on its own (HV-030-29), then joined
(HV-030-30). Exit criterion 2 asks that "the Continuity Supervisor's report covers the whole feature,
including across each sequence boundary", and the part `HV-021.cross-sequence-continuity` is exercised
when "the Supervisor's report compares across every sequence boundary, and its repair is reviewed and
applied". Before this increment:

- **The desk didn't read a feature.** `GET /direction`, the repair review and the accept read the 24-
  or 60-shot desk plan. A feature's directions are saved against its own shots (`featureShots`), so a
  feature past that plan reported its later scenes as missing or stale. Only the plan step's Supervisor
  read the feature's shots, so the desk's report and the Supervisor's notes disagreed for a feature,
  although HV-021-09 requires them to agree.
- **No boundary was named.** A wardrobe change at a `CONTINUOUS` heading that happened to open a
  sequence was reported as any `CONTINUOUS` finding. Nothing said it sat where two separately approved
  renders meet, and the report had no account of the boundaries it compared nothing across.
- **Nothing carried the light across a split moment.** A `CONTINUOUS` scene in the same place as the
  scene before is one moment in one place. Split between two sequences, the halves are rendered and
  approved apart, and their light was never compared.
- **A repair said nothing about the made sequences.** Any direction save leaves every sequence's rough
  cut and final unusable for the next approval, and the repair's answer didn't say which ones.

## What this increment does

- **The report covers every sequence boundary** (`continuityReport(..., sequences)` in
  `packages/planner/src/continuity.ts`). With a feature's current plan, it adds `boundaries`, one
  `{from, to, lastScene, firstScene, continuous, sameLocation, comparisons, findings}` per boundary.
  The opening scene of each sequence already gets the `CONTINUOUS` check every scene gets. Its
  `time-contradicts-previous` and `wardrobe-contradicts-previous` findings now carry
  `sequenceBoundary: {from, to}`. When the opening scene isn't `CONTINUOUS`, the boundary records
  `continuous: false` and zero comparisons: story time may pass, so nothing is held across it.
- **One check is the boundary's own: `boundary-look-changed`** (warning). It applies when the opening
  scene is `CONTINUOUS` and names the same place as the scene before (`continuityHeadingLocation`: the
  heading's first segment, without its time, a `CONTINUOUS` marker or a scene number). Each light field
  both scenes declare (key, fill, back, motivated sources) is then held to the first shot that states
  it, the rule a scene's own look is held to. `continuityBoundaryHolds` computes that hold, and the
  report and the repair both use it. A chain of such boundaries holds every scene in it to that one
  shot. Its comparisons are counted in the scene's `lookComparisons`. Time of day stays with the
  `CONTINUOUS` check.
- **The repair** (`continuity-repair.ts`) proposes an edit for every differing shot in the opening
  scene, held to the shot in the sequence before. That gives one edit per shot and field, marked
  `sequenceBoundary`, and it replaces that scene's own hold for that field. The edits go through the
  same review, the same exact-match accept and the same `directionSettings` validation as every repair.
  The summary adds "Sequence 3 opens in the place and moment the sequence before it closes, so its light
  is held to that sequence's." A `CONTINUOUS` contradiction on a scene that opens a sequence gets a note
  naming the boundary ("Scene 3 opens sequence 2 and is CONTINUOUS from Scene 2, the last scene of
  sequence 1, …"). Wardrobe and time are still not repaired.
- **Applying names the sequences to make again** (`continuityRepairRemakes`). This covers every rough
  cut or final of the current split that was made, or is being made, under the direction the repair
  replaces. A failed or cancelled render isn't listed. Each entry is
  `{sequence, touched, stages, jobIds}`, where `touched` marks the sequences the repair's own edits are
  in. `POST /direction/continuity/repair` answers it as `remake` before anything is applied, and
  `…/accept` answers it after. This matches how a direction save already affects approvals: the final
  and the join refuse renders made under another direction.
- **A feature's own shots everywhere** (`continuityShotPlan` in `sequences.ts`). `GET /direction`'s
  `continuity`, the repair's review and accept, and the plan step's Supervisor read `featureShots` for a
  feature, plus its plan while it is current (`stalePlanReason`). A reel and a short read the desk's
  plan exactly as before.
- **The Supervisor says it** (`crew/continuity-supervisor.ts`). A scene whose finding of a kind is
  across a boundary is named as "scene 5 (opening sequence 3)", and `boundary-look-changed` has its own
  sentence pointing at "Review continuity repair". These notes reach the front door's look approval.
- **The desk shows it** (`packages/frontend/src/continuity.js`):
  - a boundary finding reads "Warning: … (where sequence 2 meets sequence 3)";
  - its scene group reads "· opens sequence 3";
  - a "Sequence boundaries (N)" group lists one line per boundary (`boundaryLine`), including "not
    compared" and "nothing declared on both sides";
  - a boundary edit says where it holds;
  - the review and the apply status name the sequences that need a new rough cut (`remakeSentence`).
- `docs/CONTINUITY.md`: "Across a feature's sequence boundaries".

No provider or model is called for anything this adds. No key, vendor, cap or default changes. Nothing
the Supervisor does renders or spends.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Every boundary is covered.** A six-scene feature in three sequences reports two boundaries, each
   with whether it is `CONTINUOUS`, whether it stays in one place, and what was compared and found.
   A boundary without `CONTINUOUS` compares nothing across it and says so.
2. **Contradicting boundaries give findings at both.** Take a coat on at the end of sequence 1 and off
   at the start of sequence 2 (`CONTINUOUS`). Then take the stairwell lit by lamp at the end of
   sequence 2 and by daylight at the start of sequence 3 (`CONTINUOUS`, same place). They give a
   wardrobe finding labelled `{from: 1, to: 2}` and a `boundary-look-changed` labelled `{from: 2, to: 3}`.
   The second names the shot it is held to and the shot that differs. A time of day directed against
   the scene before is the `CONTINUOUS` check's finding, labelled.
3. **A consistent boundary gives none.** The same wardrobe, and the same light typed in another case
   or spacing, give no boundary finding, and the comparisons are still counted.
4. **The repair is proposed, reviewed and applied for a boundary finding.** The proposal holds the
   opening scene's differing shots to the sequence before, with one edit per shot and field when the
   scene also drifts within itself. It names the wardrobe it leaves, and says both in its summary.
   Through the real routes, an altered edit is refused (409) and the reviewed edits are applied as one
   direction version. The light finding is then gone, the wardrobe finding stays, and a second review
   holds nothing more across the boundary. A chain of same-place boundaries holds to the first shot.
5. **Applying flags the made sequences.** With rough cuts of sequences 1 and 3 admitted under the
   current direction, the review and the accept both answer
   `[{sequence: 1, touched: false}, {sequence: 3, touched: true}]` with their stages and job ids, and
   each sequence's next rough cut is a new render. Failed renders, renders under an older direction,
   other splits and non-sequence jobs are not listed. With no edits or no feature, nothing is listed.
6. **Reels and shorts are unchanged.** Without a plan the report has no `boundaries`, no labels and no
   boundary finding. With a plan, it only adds: remove the labels, the light finding and its comparisons
   and it equals the report without one. A short is planned at the desk's tier. A plan for another
   screenplay version draws no boundaries. The existing continuity, `CONTINUOUS`, repair, Supervisor and
   desk suites pass unchanged.
7. **The front door and the desk show boundary findings labelled.** The plan route's Supervisor note
   names "scene 5 (opening sequence 3)", and the look approval shows it under "Continuity Supervisor".
   The desk labels each boundary finding and its scene, lists each boundary in words, shows where a
   boundary edit holds, and names the sequences that need a new rough cut before and after applying.
   A report without sequences draws none of it.
8. **The desk reads a feature's own shots.** `GET /direction` for the 54-shot feature reports all six
   scenes and 54 shots, not its 24-shot plan.

## Known gaps

- **Only `CONTINUOUS` means "no time cut".** That matches HV-021-08: "SAME", "CONT'D", "MOMENTS LATER"
  and an unmarked cut let time pass, so a coat that comes off across them isn't reported.
- **The light is held only across a sequence boundary.** A same-place `CONTINUOUS` pair inside one
  sequence isn't compared for light, which leaves the existing `CONTINUOUS` check and a short's report
  exactly as they were. Doing it everywhere would be one rule in `continuityBoundaryHolds`, but it
  would change shorts.
- **Places are matched by text.** "INT. STAIRWELL" and "INT. STAIRS" are different places, and a
  location is split only at a spaced dash.
- **Wardrobe and time across a boundary are still not repaired.** They are the creator's call
  (HV-021-08). Exit criterion 2's "explicitly kept" has no keep action yet: such a finding is settled
  only by changing the cast, the heading or the direction.
- **The remake is feature-wide.** Direction revisions are whole-project (`directionMatches`), so a
  repair in sequence 3 also sends sequence 1's rough cut back. The answer says so and marks which
  sequences the repair touched; making approvals per sequence is not in scope.
- **`needsRoughCut` (HV-034-02) knows only the style bible.** `GET /style-bible` reports a sequence
  `needsRoughCut` when it has no rough cut of the current bible revision. A continuity repair changes the
  direction, not the bible, so that flag stays false after a repair, even though the final's admission
  and the join will refuse a rough cut made under the replaced direction. The repair's own `remake`
  answer says this. The two flags are kept separate, because making `needsRoughCut` also track the
  direction would change the bible route's meaning and its tests. One "made under the current film"
  check per sequence, covering the bible and the direction, is a follow-up.
- **The stand-in crew lights a `CONTINUOUS` scene as day.** It reads only a heading's own time, so a
  feature planned on the stand-in reports time contradictions at its `CONTINUOUS` headings, and the
  light at a same-place one. The route test shows this. The report is right; the stand-in needs fixing
  (HV-030).
- **The desk still edits a feature through its 24- or 60-shot plan** (HV-030-29's gap). The report and
  the repair now read the feature's shots, but a shot's direction can't be saved at the desk past that
  plan.
- **The Supervisor's notes for the `CONTINUOUS` kinds** still quote the first scene's message (the
  existing fallback), now with each boundary scene labelled.
- **`rulesVersion` stays 1**, as in HV-021-08. The revision already moves with the new fields.
- **Not run against PostgreSQL.** The repair routes read jobs through `projectJobs`, as every route
  does. The tests use the JSON stores.
- **Still declarations, not pictures.** Nothing here looks at a frame.
