# HV-024-14 — The crew's films carry the studio's ambience, and the credits say so

epic: HV-024
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

HV-024-12 taught the studio to make its own ambience beds, at no cost, and gave them a route:
`POST /api/projects/<id>/ambience/<cutId>` answers cues ready for a sound-mix session. Its Known gaps
said two things were left:

- **The crew flow doesn't ask for ambience yet.** The front door's Composer mixed only its score.
- **The closing credits don't name the ambience yet.** The credit was in each bed's rights record and
  in the cue sheet, but not on screen.

Both waited on HV-024-11, which was changing the same two files. It has merged. So a film made
through the front door still had a silent ambience stem, though the studio could fill it for free.

## What this increment does

- **The Composer asks for the cut's ambience** (`scoreFinal` in `packages/frontend/src/studio.js`).
  - Once the score is in the library (its own loop, or HV-024-11's generated cue), it asks
    `POST /ambience/<cut id>` with `{}`. Each scene gets the bed its heading picks.
  - Every cue the route answers goes into the same sound-mix session as the music cue, unchanged.
    The route sets each bed's level, fades and ducking; the studio invents none.
- **The session with ambience has its own key**, `crew-score-ambience-<cut id>`. A score alone keeps
  `crew-score-<cut id>`.
  - The sound-mix route refuses a known key with a different session (*"This key belongs to a
    different sound session."*). One shared key would have turned a film first mixed without ambience
    into a failed score when it was mixed again with it.
  - The ambience route takes no request key; the route refuses any field but `overrides`. Asking
    again for the same cut answers the same cues and reuses the beds, so the cut in its URL makes a
    retry safe.
- **A refusal costs a note, not the film.** If the route refuses (an expired cut, a busy sound slot,
  a full library), the film is mixed with its score alone. The last approval says *"Composer: the
  studio's ambience was not added (<reason>); the film is shared with its score and no ambience."*
- **The credits name it only when it is there** (`creditRows` in `packages/frontend/src/titles.js`).
  "Ambience: Generated by the studio", the beds' own words, follows the music row. It appears only
  when the mix that carried the beds finished.
- **HV-024-11's music is unchanged.** The generated cue is still asked for under
  `crew-music-<cut id>`, mixed first, and credited as Music. With a vendor, the one session holds the
  generated cue and every ambience cue.
- **Docs.** `docs/SOUND-AMBIENCE.md` has a new section, "The crew flow (HV-024-14)", and its Known
  gaps. `docs/CREW.md` describes the ambience under the score, and the new credit row.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **The final cut is given the studio's ambience.** The studio asks the ambience route once, for the
   final cut, with `{}`. The mix's cues are the score's cue followed by exactly the cues the route
   answered: a kitchen's room tone and a beach's surf, each at −6 dB, ducked 6 dB, with the route's
   fades.
2. **A refusal still finishes the film.** For an expired cut, a busy slot and a full library:
   - the scored cut is the film;
   - the note names the route's reason;
   - the mix holds the score alone under `crew-score-<cut id>`;
   - the route is asked once.
3. **The credits name the ambience only when it was mixed.**
   - With the beds mixed, the last two rows are the score's and "Ambience: Generated by the studio".
   - There is no ambience row when the route refuses, when the mix fails, or when the creator asks
     for no music. With no music the route is never asked.
   - `creditRows` without `ambience` is what it was before.
4. **Music and ambience together.** With a music vendor, the generated cue is still keyed
   `crew-music-<cut id>` and comes first in the session, with every ambience cue after it. The
   credits end with Music, then Ambience.
5. **Both requests are keyed by the cut.** Two passes over one cut ask the same route and send the same
   mix under `crew-score-ambience-<cut id>`. A different cut gets a different key. The source guard
   still counts six fixed request keys, and names both mix keys.
6. Reverting `studio.js` and `titles.js` fails the five new tests in `studio.test.js`, the new test
   in `titles.test.js`, the source guard in `studio-retry.test.js`, and two of the updated tests in
   `studio.test.js` (the score's mix, and the titled cut's credits).

- Tests:
  - `packages/frontend/test/studio.test.js`: five new tests (1–5). Four existing tests now expect
    the ambience: the request in the route, the cues and key in the mix, and the Ambience row in the
    credits. The fake's ambience answer is made by the planner's own `ambienceScenes` and
    `ambienceCues`.
  - `packages/frontend/test/titles.test.js` (3).
  - `packages/frontend/test/studio-retry.test.js` (5): still six fixed keys; the guard now names the
    two mix keys.

## Known gaps

- **Closes HV-024-12's first two gaps.** The crew flow now asks for ambience, and the closing
  credits name it.
- **A film with no music has no ambience.** "No music" skips the whole mix, so a creator who asks
  for silence gets the film's own sound only. Ambience on its own would need a choice about what
  "silent" means.
- **A busy sound slot costs the ambience.** The score's own upload waits up to half an hour for the
  slot. The ambience request asks once, as the increment asked.
- **The creator can't choose a scene's bed from the front door.** The crew flow sends no overrides;
  only the API takes them.
- **A bed withdrawn between two passes changes the session.** Its fresh copy has a new asset id, so
  the second pass's session differs under the same `crew-score-ambience-<cut id>` key and is refused
  as a different session. That pass shares the film without its score, and the note says the score
  could not be mixed. HV-024-11's generated cue has the same exposure.
- **No one has heard a crew film with its beds.** The fake answers the route's shapes; the real
  route's render and mix are proven by HV-024-12's server test, not by a front-door run.
