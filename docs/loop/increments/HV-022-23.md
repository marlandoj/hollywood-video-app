# HV-022-23 — The studio can lip-sync a voiced cut's on-camera lines, behind a switch that is off

epic: HV-022
release: 3 "Features"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

Kevin's review of "The Tide Clock" (`docs/evidence/release-3/release-run.json`, `feature.review.comments`)
said the faces did not move while their lines played. HV-030-43 fixed the coverage: each feature line now sits
on its own speaker's shot (`dialoguePlacement: "speaker"`), and that shot's prompt asks for the speaker's face
and moving mouth. The picture is still silent, though. Kling renders no audio, the rough cut's voices are laid
under the final (`packages/queue/src/final-dialogue.ts`), and the studio then lays in the cast's takes
(`voiceFinal` in `packages/frontend/src/studio.js`). Whatever mouth the model draws is not timed to the line.

A lip-sync job already exists (`docs/LIP-SYNC.md`, P7). It covers:

- the Sync `sync-3` adapter, `packages/generator/src/sync-lipsync.ts`;
- the plan and policy, `packages/planner/src/lipsync.ts` and `lipsync-policy.ts`;
- the ledger, `packages/storage/src/lipsync-ledger.ts`;
- the worker, `packages/queue/src/lipsync-worker.ts`;
- the owner routes, `packages/api/src/lipsync-api.ts`.

It is an owner job only. For each pass the owner chooses a voiced cut, a line, a source frame and a face point
on that frame. Nothing connects it to the studio's film run.

## How a pass is admitted today (what this increment builds on)

- **Source.** A completed dialogue-replacement job (a voiced cut), or a completed lip-sync pass, that still
  carries an applied cast take on the line (`retainLipSyncSource`). The line's window is its measured samples,
  rounded out to whole frames (`lipSyncWindow`).
- **Frame and face point.** The owner previews a frame of the window. The server decodes it and returns its
  native size and the SHA-256 of its RGB pixels. The owner then submits that frame, the same size and hash,
  and an `x, y` in native pixels (`LipSyncSelection`). Admission decodes the frame again and refuses a
  mismatch. The worker checks it once more before it dispatches.
- **Policy.** `HV_LIPSYNC_POLICY_FILE` (`hv-lipsync-policy/1`). It gives the account, licence and price
  evidence, the output hosts, the validity window, a flat `heldUsd` per pass and `maxFrames`. No policy ships.
- **Hold.** `PostgresLipSyncLedger.admitLipSync` reserves `heldUsd` in `hv_reservations`, after the film's limit
  (`assertFilmWithin`) and inside the monthly cap. That needs PostgreSQL. The file store refuses lip-sync with a
  503.
- **Permissions.** Current project, cast and retained-voice permission (`assertLipSyncPermission`) at
  admission, dispatch, completion and playback.
- **Dispatch.** The worker's lip-sync lane exists only with PostgreSQL, `HV_SYNC_API_KEY` and a policy file
  (`packages/queue/src/worker.ts`). Without them a pass fails before any provider call.

## The design choice

The hard part is the face point. The existing job binds every pass to one the owner chose on a verified frame.
This increment detects no faces and guesses no points. It also adds no vendor that could. The two options
offered were:

- (a) a run option that plans passes only for on-camera lines and reuses a saved face point;
- (b) a planner-only work list.

What fits the code is (b) as the core, with the narrow part of (a) on top:

1. **A work list, computed by the planner** (`packages/planner/src/lipsync-worklist.ts`, `lipSyncWorkList`).
   For every line of a voiced cut it gives:
   - the shot, line, speaker and cast character;
   - the window (start frame, frames, samples);
   - the held cost from the current policy;
   - the reason the line is in or out.

   Exactly one reason applies, checked in this order:

   | reason | when |
   |---|---|
   | `no-applied-voice` | the line keeps its temporary voice |
   | `unplaced` | the sequence plan is not marked `dialoguePlacement: "speaker"` (any short, reel or older feature) |
   | `off-camera` | `Scene.offCameraDialogue` flags the speech (V.O., O.S., a device or door voice), or it speaks only parentheticals |
   | `not-framed` | the shot's prompt does not carry the speaking sentence naming this speaker. This covers accepted coverage, a fourth or later name past the three-name cap, and a shot whose speeches are not its scene's in order |
   | `already-synced` | this cut's own pass history already has the line |
   | `pass-limit` | the cut has 32 passes |
   | `too-long` | the window exceeds the policy's `maxFrames` |
   | `no-face-point` | the owner has chosen no point for this shot and speaker on this picture |
   | `ready` | none of the above |

2. **Saved face points are the owner's, on the same picture** (`savedLipSyncFacePoints`, `lipSyncPicture`).
   - A point comes from an owner's own lip-sync pass in the project. It is kept as the frame number in the
     whole film, plus the native size, `x, y` and RGB hash.
   - It is reused only for the same shot, the same cast character and the same picture, and only when its
     frame lies inside the new line's window.
   - "Same picture" is exact. Dialogue replacement copies the final's H.264 packets, so every voiced cut of one
     final decodes to the same frames, named by `videoStreamSha256`. A lip-sync output re-encodes the whole
     film, so it is its own picture (`lipsync-export:<sha>`), and no point carries onto it.
   - These passes offer no point:
     - a failed or cancelled pass, because the point may be why it failed;
     - a pass whose plan no longer validates;
     - a pass the studio queued, because it copied a point and chose nothing.
   - At queue time the route decodes the frame again and compares its hash. A mismatch is skipped and said, and
     never guessed.

3. **The studio queues the ready lines** (`POST /api/projects/:id/lip-sync/:voicedCutId/work-list`). Each
   ready line becomes one ordinary `hv-lipsync-plan/1` pass of the voiced cut (`studioLipSyncPlan`, which is
   `createLipSyncPlan` with the owner's point), admitted by the same `admitLipSync`.
   - Every pass reads from the same voiced cut. Each is its own version for the owner to review under **Direct
     lip-sync for applied voices**.
   - The studio never chooses a pass as the export. The film it shares stays the voiced cut.
   - The request key is fixed by the cut, the line and the policy (`studioLipSyncKey`), so a retry never pays
     twice.

**Why independent passes, and not a chain.** The existing job does allow one pass to be the source of the next.
But each pass re-encodes the whole film, so the next pass's frame no longer matches any point the owner chose.
Chaining would mean re-binding the owner's point to a lossy re-encode of the frame without the owner seeing it.
This increment does not do that.

The ready passes are therefore independent windows on one picture. The provider clip of each is retained
(`provider.mp4`), and that is what a later increment needs to assemble several windows into one export.

## What this increment does

- **The switch.** `HV_STUDIO_LIPSYNC=1` (`STUDIO_LIPSYNC_ENV`, `studioLipSyncEnabled`), read at request time as
  `HV_NARRATION` and `HV_ANIMATIC_CAPTIONS` are. It is off unless set to exactly `1`. Off by default
  everywhere:
  - **Environment:** unset is off.
  - **Policy:** `hv-lipsync-policy/1` is unchanged, has no switch, and no policy ships.
  - **Project:** no project field.

  The switch never enters a plan, policy, project, job, ledger row or snapshot.
- **Off, nothing changes.**
  - `/lip-sync/:id/work-list` falls through to the existing "unknown lip-sync route" 404.
  - The job view is exactly as before.
  - The studio asks nothing about lip-sync.
  - The lip-sync capability (`13785712…`), the fixture policy (`23b1104e…`), every existing plan and the
    budget defaults are unchanged.
- **On, without a policy or without PostgreSQL**, `GET …/work-list` returns the list with
  `enabled: false` and the reason. With no policy, the list carries no hold and no duration check. `POST` answers
  503 ("Nothing is queued or held"). No job, reservation or ledger entry is written.
- **On, with a policy and PostgreSQL**, `POST` takes `{generationApproved: true, worklistRevision}`. A stale or
  unapproved list is refused (409). Then, in this order:
  1. **Holds before any spend.** The sum of every new pass's hold is computed in integer micro-dollars
     (`studioLipSyncHoldUsd`). It is checked against the film's limit (`assertFilmBudget` over
     `filmSpend`, i.e. the feature's $150 or the film's $40) and the monthly cap ($500), before the first pass
     is admitted. A list that doesn't fit is refused whole (429, `budget_exhausted`).
  2. **Frame check.** Each ready line's saved frame is decoded again.
  3. **Admission.** Each pass is admitted by `admitLipSync`. That checks the film's limit and the month again,
     and reserves `heldUsd` in `hv_reservations`.

  Lip-sync has no vendor line of its own. It is held to the film's limit and the monthly cap, as an owner pass
  is. A line that already holds a live pass on this cut, the owner's or the studio's, is listed under `queued`
  and not admitted again. Admission is not spend. Nothing is dispatched without the worker's key.
- **The job view.** A finished voiced cut (`stage: "dialogue-replacement"`) carries `studioLipSync: true`, only
  while the switch is on (`GET /api/jobs/:id`).
- **The studio run** (`lipSyncFinal` in `studio.js`). Right after the cast's voices are laid in, a voiced cut
  marked `studioLipSync` has its work list read. The studio then:
  - queues the new ready lines, if there are any;
  - adds a finishing note saying how many were queued, the dollars held, and how many on-camera lines need a
    face point;
  - says when nothing was queued because no policy or provider service is configured;
  - keeps the voiced cut if the queue request is refused, and says so.

  It does not wait for the passes, which are separate versions.
- **One helper** (`onCameraSpeakers` in `dialogue-placement.ts`): the names `speakingDirection` already
  computed, now exported. `speakingDirection`'s output is unchanged.
- **One route word** (`work-list` in `PROJECT_ROUTE_WORDS`, `packages/observability/src/index.ts`), so the
  request log names the new route by its pattern (HV-030-32's closed word list; `route-labels.test.ts`).

## What is automated, and what is not

| | automated here | still the owner's |
|---|---|---|
| which lines are lip-sync work | yes: placement, off-camera flags, speaking sentence, history, duration | — |
| the face point | reuse of the owner's own point on the same picture, verified by frame hash | choosing it for every shot that has none, under Direct lip-sync |
| holds | every pass summed and checked before any admission, then each reserved | — |
| queueing | yes, with the switch on, a policy and PostgreSQL | — |
| dispatch | the existing worker lane (key, policy, PostgreSQL) | the operator's key and policy |
| choosing a pass as the export | no | review and select under Direct lip-sync |
| one export with every window | no (see below) | — |

On a fresh run, no face point is saved yet, so every on-camera line is listed as `no-face-point` and nothing is
queued or held. That is the honest state of the pipeline: the studio says how many need one. A pass is queued
only where the owner has already chosen a point on that picture, for example when the cast is re-voiced and a
new voiced cut of the same final is made.

### What a later increment needs for full automation

1. **Choosing the face without the owner.** Either a face or landmark detector, which is a new vendor or a
   bundled model, its licence and an evaluation on authorized footage (profiles, occlusion, two-shots). Or
   Sync's own speaker detection, which the capability declines today (`selection.mode: "manual"`). Either way
   it is a new `hv-lipsync-capability` revision and a new pin.
2. **One export with every window.** A multi-window assembly that lays each completed pass's retained
   `provider.mp4` into the voiced cut's picture in one encode. It would come with a new result schema, history
   and review rules. Until then each pass is its own version.
3. **A face point saved without a pass.** An owner route to save a point per shot, so the first studio run can
   be ready without a manual pass first.
4. **The creator's approval of the lip-sync hold.** Today the operator's switch is the opt-in, and the studio
   queues on the creator's earlier approval of the final, as `voiceFinal` does for takes. The rough-cut
   approval's quote does not include lip-sync holds.

## What this increment does not do

- No face detection, no new vendor, key, account or provider, no network call. `spend_usd` is 0. Paid providers
  stay on mock.
- No change to the lip-sync capability, policy schema, plan schema, ledger, worker, adapter, holds arithmetic,
  the $500 monthly cap, the $40 and $150 film limits, or any budget default.
- No chained passes, no pass chosen as an export, no re-render.
- No change to `packages/safety`, `docs/legal/**`, `docs/adr/**`, `.github/workflows/**`, the capability pins
  or any evidence.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Off is unchanged.** `HV_STUDIO_LIPSYNC` unset, `0` or `true` is off; only `1` is on. Off, `GET` and `POST
   …/work-list` answer exactly the unknown-route 404. The job views of a voiced cut, a film and a lip-sync pass,
   and the lip-sync catalogue, hash the same off and on (signed media links aside). On, only `studioLipSync:
   true` is added, and only to a finished voiced cut. The capability, the fixture policy and its price revision
   keep their pinned revisions. An owner plan revalidates and rehashes the same with the switch on. The budget
   defaults stay $40 and $150. With the switch off, the studio makes no lip-sync request.
2. **The work list.** On a placed feature with two voiced cuts of one final:
   - MARLA's line on the shot naming her, with the owner's point from a pass on the first cut, is `ready` on
     the second, with the owner's exact selection.
   - KEVIN (V.O.) is `off-camera`.
   - MARLA's second line is `no-face-point`.

   Reuse is refused, and the line reads `no-face-point`, when the point belongs to another character, lies
   outside the window, or is on another picture. A failed or cancelled pass offers no point. On an unmarked
   plan every line with a take is `unplaced`. A policy with fewer frames than the window gives `too-long`.
   After a pass completes, its output is a new picture, its line is `already-synced`, and the other on-camera
   line needs a point.
3. **Holds.** The list holds passes × `heldUsd`. A $0.333333 policy holds $0.999999 for three passes, in integer
   micro-dollars. Through PostgreSQL:
   - a list past a $4 film limit is refused whole (429), with no job and no reservation;
   - a stale or unapproved list is refused (409), holding nothing;
   - the ready line is admitted once, with a $5 reservation in `hv_reservations` and the job's cap and
     reservation at $5;
   - asking again admits nothing, and the film's spend shows $5 held under the unchanged $40 limit.
4. **No policy.** With no policy the list has no hold, no line can be planned, and `POST` answers 503 with no
   job or ledger change. Without PostgreSQL the same holds with a policy, and the list is priced.
5. **Queueing.** A ready line's plan is `createLipSyncPlan` with the owner's selection and the list's request
   hash, under a key fixed by the cut, line and policy. It is prepared from the frame the owner verified,
   dispatched once through the closed Sync transport, rendered and completed as an owner pass is. The studio
   asks for the list once and queues once, and its notes say what was queued, held and still needs a point,
   that nothing was queued without a policy, and that a refused queue keeps the voiced cut.

## Known gaps

- **A fresh run queues nothing.** It lists every on-camera line as needing a face point (see above). The first
  live use is the owner choosing points on the voiced cut's shots.
- **Each pass is its own version of the film.** Watching all of them at once needs the multi-window assembly.
- **"Framed" means the plan asked for it, not that the picture shows it.** A speech on a scene's first shot (a
  one-shot scene) carries the speaking sentence but is framed wide by the stand-in crew. A live crew may frame
  any shot otherwise. Whether Kling drew a face where the owner's point is, is the owner's check when they
  choose it.
- **No price for a pass is known.** The policy's `heldUsd` is flat per pass and bounded by `maxFrames`; the
  fixture holds $5. A feature with 40 on-camera lines would hold 40 × `heldUsd` against the $150 feature limit,
  which already carries the feature's picture holds.
- **A partly admitted list.** Admission is per pass after the whole-list check. If the budget moves between
  them, the passes admitted so far stay held (the answer lists them), and a retry admits the rest under the same
  keys.

## Open questions for the owner

1. The held cost per pass. Sync bills by duration. The existing policy shape holds one flat `heldUsd` per pass
   with `maxFrames` as the bound. Should a studio pass hold by its window's seconds instead? That would be a new
   policy schema.
2. Which provider option for face selection in a later increment: Sync's own speaker detection (no new vendor,
   less control), or a bundled or vendor face detector (new vendor and licence).
3. Whether the creator should approve the lip-sync hold as its own step, or whether the operator's switch plus
   the final's approval is enough, as it is for the cast's takes.
4. Whether a point from a pass the owner reviewed as `revise` or `cutaway` should still be reused.
