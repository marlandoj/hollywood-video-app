# HV-031-04 — Real people by consent; public figures refused by name

epic: HV-031
release: 1 (build order step 2)
gate: G12-202609191900 (resolved: the operator approved this design)
spend_usd: 0

## Goal

Let a filmmaker cast themselves, or someone who gave them permission, from their own photos. Refuse named public figures everywhere. Both have to be in place before any stage leaves mock.

## Why now

Build step 2 was planned as "refuse identifiable real people", because the prompt gate let "A portrait of Taylor Swift" through (`docs/CASTING.md`). The operator wants the opposite for himself: to appear in his own film.

The two needs don't conflict:

- **Consent is the dividing line.** A cast member is a person who consented, and a public figure can't consent through an anonymous studio.
- **Before this change, no real person could be cast at all.** The only permission declaration was "this is my original fictional character", and `characterRecord` refused any other `kind`.

## What this increment does

**Casting (`packages/planner/src/casting.ts`)**

- `kind` is `"original-fictional" | "consented-real-person"`.
- Permitting a real person needs two things:
  - the attestation, worded for a real person;
  - `permission.consent`, either `"self"` or `"permission"`.
- A pending real-person draft may omit the consent.
- A fictional record may not carry `consent`. Its permission object is exactly its earlier shape, so every saved cast keeps its revision. That's pinned against `origin/main`.
- Revoking or restoring an old cast version drops the consent, as it already drops the permission.
- Saving any record whose name, aliases or appearance names a public figure is refused. Saved records are not re-judged on read, so a longer list never makes an old cast unreadable. The gate still refuses the name at generation.

**The prompt gate (`packages/safety`)**

- A new `named_public_figure` category: `public-figures.ts`, 185 names.
- Matching is:
  - on whole words only, on both sides;
  - case- and accent-insensitive;
  - with spaces, dots and hyphens interchangeable.
- Mononyms are listed only where the word has no everyday meaning. For example, "Beyoncé" is listed, but "Madonna", "Prince", "Drake" and "Adele" are not.
- `checkPrompt` now tests every rule against the text as written **and** folded (lower-case, accents stripped). This can only add refusals: "Pokemon" now meets the same rule as "Pokémon".
- The refusal for either real-person category explains the consented-cast route. It still says "content policy" and still makes zero provider calls.

**Consent stays in its project (`actor-library.ts`)**

- A real person can't be minted as an actor-library share.
- `validateActorShare` refuses one on every read. That covers a well-formed share with intact hashes, which the test constructs.

**Surfaces**

- **Character sheets:** a real person's sheet prompt says "the named cast member, as shown in the reference images". Saying "a real person" would trip the existing `identifiable_real_person` rule. A fictional sheet's prompt is unchanged.
- **Cast editor (`frontend/src/casting.js`):**
  - a "Who is this?" choice;
  - a "Whose consent" choice;
  - attestation text worded for each kind;
  - photo guidance and a photo attestation for real people;
  - the card shows "Real person (you)" or "(with permission)".
- **Reference upload refusal message:** no longer says fictional-only.
- **Docs:**
  - `docs/CASTING.md` gains "Real people: by consent only".
  - `docs/ROADMAP.md` step 2 and exit criterion 3 are reworded.
  - `docs/loop/HUMAN-GATES.md` records G12.

## What this increment does not do

- **It does not verify identity.** The consent is a declaration: the studio has no accounts (ADR-0018). That's accepted on private staging. Before public launch it belongs to the counsel and moderation review under G7.
- **It does not detect public figures in images.** A celebrity photo uploaded as a "consented" reference is stopped only by the uploader's attestation. Image moderation remains an open HV-031 item.
- **It is a keyword list.** A figure not on the list, or a paraphrase ("the singer of Shake It Off"), is not caught. Growing the list is always allowed. Removing a name is a refusal shrink and needs a gate.
- **Historical figures are not listed.**
- **It does not relax any existing rule.** "A real person, my coworker, portrayed …" is still refused. A real cast member is described by appearance, and their likeness comes from the reference photos.
- **It does not add a voice clone.**
- **It does not touch provider routing.** Everything stays mock.

## Acceptance criteria

All in `packages/planner/test/consented-casting.test.ts` unless noted.

1. **A consented real person is accepted and renders.**
   - Accepted with `self` or `permission`, and survives snapshot validation.
   - Directs every shot with gate-clean prompts.
   - Survives the service's save and reload.
   - Restoring an old version returns it to pending without consent.
   - Revoking stops rendering and drops the consent.
2. **Consent is declared, and only where it applies.**
   - Permitting without the attestation, without whose consent, or with an unknown consent value is refused.
   - A pending draft may omit it.
   - A fictional record carrying `consent` is refused.
   - Unknown kinds are refused.
3. **Saved fictional casts are unchanged.** The pinned revision `3eded0d7…` equals `origin/main`'s for the same input and instant.
4. **Public figures are refused by name in prompts.** This holds in any case, with or without accents, in possessive form, and with dotted or hyphenated names. Every listed name is refused on its own, and each is listed once. Ordinary words that share a name, and names embedded in longer words on either side, pass. Also covered by `packages/safety/test/safety.test.ts`, which runs the three new battery prompts.
5. **Public figures are refused in cast records.** A record naming one is refused on save, for either kind. A saved one is still readable, and still cannot render.
6. **A real person cannot leave its project as an actor share,** at mint or at read.
7. **A real person's character sheet prompt passes the gate,** and a fictional one is worded as before.
