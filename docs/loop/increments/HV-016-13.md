# HV-016-13 — A numbered shooting script was refused as "not laid out as a screenplay"

epic: HV-016
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

Production and shooting drafts print each scene's number beside its heading, in the left margin,
the right margin or both. Every screenwriting program can do this, and any script that has been
through a production office has the numbers. The numbers are runs of their own on the heading's
baseline. The PDF importer's header and docs/SCRIPT-IMPORT.md both say scene numbers are "not
carried" and are reported as a note. That isn't what happened:

- **A number in the left margin** became the page's leftmost text. Margins are measured from the
  leftmost text, so every action line landed half an inch right of the action margin, and the
  script was refused with *"This PDF's text is not laid out as a screenplay — 3 of 10 lines sit
  outside the margins a screenplay uses. Export the script as Fountain or Final Draft."* The script
  was laid out as a screenplay, and it had just been exported from one of those programs.
- **A number in the right margin** was glued onto the heading. `INT. LIGHTHOUSE - NIGHT 12A`
  imported with no note, and "NIGHT 12A" became the scene's time of day.

## What this increment does

- **Scene numbers are taken off the heading's baseline before the margins are measured.** A run
  counts as a scene number only if:
  - it sits beside a scene heading;
  - it holds nothing but a number (`12`, `12A`, `A12`, `12.`);
  - on the right, a clear gap separates it from the heading, meaning two characters where a normal
    space is one.
- **The writer is told**: *"Scene numbers were not imported; the studio numbers scenes in the order
  they appear."*
- `pdfLines` is now built from two steps, grouping runs onto baselines and joining each baseline, so
  the importer can drop runs between them. What `pdfLines` returns hasn't changed.
- The test fixture can draw more runs on a line's baseline, which is how a real draft prints its
  numbers.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Numbers in the left margin, the right margin or both** import as exactly the same screenplay
   as the unnumbered draft, with the same two headings.
2. **The `scene-numbers` note** is given when numbers were removed, and only then.
3. **Nothing that isn't a scene number is touched**:
   - `EXT. HIGHWAY 101`, with the number drawn a single space after the words, keeps its highway;
   - an action line starting with a numeric run keeps it;
   - a number in the right margin beside action stays;
   - `(12)` beside a heading stays.
4. Reverting `pdf.ts` fails criteria 1 and 2.

- Tests: `packages/parser/test/pdf-scene-numbers.test.ts`.

## Known gaps this leaves

- **A number drawn in the same run as the heading**, for example `12   INT. HOUSE - DAY` as one
  string, is not recognized. The programs this was checked against position the numbers as separate
  runs. A producer that pads with spaces instead would still be refused.
- **Numbers aren't kept.** Fountain's `#12A#` scene-number syntax isn't something `parseFountain`
  reads, so keeping the numbers would put them back into the heading.
- **Other shooting-draft marks**, such as `CONTINUED:` at the top of a page, `(CONTINUED)` at the
  bottom and revision asterisks in the right margin, are still read as action, as a parenthetical, or
  as part of the line. Each needs its own rule and its own test.
