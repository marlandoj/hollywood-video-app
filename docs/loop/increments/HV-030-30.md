# HV-030-30 — Feature assembly, titles and review (Release 3 step 7)

epic: HV-030
release: 3 "Features"
gate: G20-202610031349 (Release 3's build order: the sequences join into one film with an opening title and end credits; one review link)
spend_usd: 0

## Why

Build-order row 7 of Release 3: "Sequences join into one film with an opening title and end credits;
one review link." HV-030-29 made the sequence the render unit and left the first gap open, "Not
joined": each sequence's final was its own film, shared and downloaded as "sequence k", with no title or
credits, and the last sequence said so ("joining them into one feature … isn't built yet").

A short gets its title and credits as a picture edit (HV-025-03). That path can't carry a feature: a
picture edit conforms every frame into lossless masters and admits an edit only within its retained-media
estimate (two raw RGB copies of every frame, three times over for the workspace, at most 48 GiB of
output and 128 GiB of workspace), which at 1280x720 is about 4.5 minutes. A feature is 15 to 20. So the
join is the assembler's own concat and crossfade, applied to whole films, streamed through one ffmpeg run.

## What this increment does

- **A job of its own stage, `feature-film`** (`packages/planner/src/feature-film.ts`). Its plan
  (`hv-feature-film/1`) names each sequence's final and finished film under the current split
  (`sequences: [{number, firstScene, lastScene, finalJobId, filmJobId}]`), keeps each film's own copy
  (whose cast the feature carries), the Editor's title and credits graphics, the size and the 12-frame
  dissolve. A film is traced to its final through the dialogue replacement, lip-sync version or sound mix
  that finished it (`finalOf`).
- **Admission refuses the join (409, `FeatureFilmConflict`)** for: a sequence with no film named; a film
  that isn't this project's, isn't finished, or isn't made from a sequence's final; a film of another
  sequence or of an older split; a stale final (the screenplay, the cast or the shot directions changed
  after it was made, or its rough cut is no longer approved); a sequence that has a newer final; a film
  named twice; a title or credits that isn't this project's finished graphic of that kind; and anything
  but a feature with a split. It costs $0, under the month and the feature's own film limit.
- **The assembler joins the films** (`assembleFeatureFilm`, `packages/assembler/src/feature-film.ts`):
  `xfade` and `acrossfade` at each join (0.4 s; a straight cut for a film under 1.6 s), so the sound runs
  on unbroken; the RGBA title overlaid on the first sequence's start; the credits card appended after the
  last frame; each film's captions moved to its place. The export is encoded with the assembler's
  settings, passes `validateExport` (the ffprobe gate), is signed beside its record with the host's key
  (`exportCredentials`, `provenance.c2pa`), and is cut into HLS. Its `provenance.json`
  (`hv-feature-film-result/1`) records the join, the graphics, each film's duration, sha256 and start.
- **The worker** (`packages/queue/src/feature-film-worker.ts`) checks before the join and before it
  completes that each film and graphic is still the one admitted and unexpired, and on the JSON store
  that each film's cast still permits it; it reads sources from disk or the object store and publishes the
  export like a final's. The output validator (`validateFeatureFilmOutput`) checks the paths, the file
  inventory, the MP4's sha256 and the sidecar (`exportSidecarProblem`).
- **The route** `GET/POST /api/projects/:projectId/feature-film` (`packages/api/src/feature-film-api.ts`).
  `GET` answers the feature's size, the dissolve, each sequence's newest final and the joins made; `POST`
  admits the join, and a repeated request key is the same job (`admitted: false`). The job view
  summarises the join and never carries the films' own copies. A feature film is shown, and a review link
  bound to it served, only while every film's cast permits it (`assertOutputPermission`); a reviewer's
  decision on it approves the final.
- **The stores.** Migration `0021_feature_film` adds the stage to the `hv_jobs` stage check (additive:
  the check gains one value). The PostgreSQL ledger admits a feature film only while its films and
  graphics are the ones named and their casts permit it, and the job store checks the same when it
  completes. A state snapshot carries the plan and the export and refuses either changed.
- **The front door** (`packages/frontend/src/studio.js`). After the last sequence's film is finished, the
  Editor renders one opening title and one end credits with the short's own path (`renderTitles`,
  `titlePlans` at the feature's size), the credits naming the Showrunner ("Sequences by: Showrunner (AI
  crew)", `creditRows({showrunner: true})`) and the voices, music and ambience any sequence carried, and
  asks for the join with a key fixed by the films and graphics (`featureJoinKey`). The joined film is the
  last approval ("Approval 7 of 7: the whole feature, its 3 sequences joined into one film"), the one film
  downloaded ("Download the feature (MP4)") and shared, with one review link. A join that stops keeps the
  last sequence's film with the Editor's note and offers "Ask the Editor to join the sequences again".
  Without a graphics renderer the films are joined untitled, and the Editor says so. The "not joined yet"
  wording is gone.
- **Docs.** `docs/CREW.md` (the feature's film, the front door, Not yet) and `docs/PICTURE-EDITORIAL.md`
  (why the joined feature isn't a picture edit).

No provider is called, no key or vendor is added, and no cap or default is raised.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Three sequences join into one film.** On the mock providers, through the real studio, API and
   worker, a three-sequence feature's films join into one `feature-film` export whose duration is the
   three films' less two 0.4 s dissolves (±0.15 s), whose sound runs its whole length, whose films start
   where the record says, and which carries no picture edit.
2. **One title and one credits.** With the pinned graphics renderer, the joined film carries the one
   `crew-title` over its start and the one `crew-credits` after its last frame, its length the films'
   less the dissolves plus 6 s; the credits name the writer and the Showrunner. The kernel shows the title
   over the start only, each film in its place and the credits card last.
3. **Refused when missing or stale.** The join is refused (409, nothing admitted) for a missing sequence,
   crossed sequences, a rough cut named as a film, an unknown or another project's film, a stale final
   (its rough cut sent back), a non-graphic named as the title, and a reel; and, in the validators, for a
   superseded final, an older split, an unfinished film and a film named twice.
4. **Provenance.** The plan, the export's `provenance.json` and the job view record each sequence's
   final and film under the current split.
5. **Signed.** With a throwaway key configured, the export has a `provenance.c2pa` sidecar named in its
   record, read as `Trusted` against the issuing root and bound to the MP4 the reviewer is served;
   without a key it is unsigned and says so.
6. **One review link.** The creator's share makes one `approve` link bound to the joined film; the
   reviewer opens it (stage `feature-film`, its sidecar URL), fetches the film and approves it as the
   final. The studio's own request key repeated is the same join.
7. **Kept and refused when changed.** A plan, a job or an export changed after admission is refused by
   the validators, a state snapshot and the PostgreSQL job store; PostgreSQL admits the join at $0 under
   its own stage and refuses it, and its completion, while a film is not the one named.
8. **The front door.** After the last sequence the studio joins, titles and shares the one film with the
   new words; a stopped join keeps the last sequence's film and is asked for again with the same key and
   the same graphics; nothing is joined or titled before the last sequence's film.
9. **Reels and shorts unchanged.** A reel's and a short's requests and words (`studio.test.js`), their
   titled picture edit (`graphics.test.ts`), the credits (`titles.test.js`) and HV-030-29's sequence
   flow are unchanged; a reel can't be joined.

## Known gaps this leaves

- **The joined feature isn't a picture edit.** It can't be opened or re-cut at the editorial desk, isn't
  an editorial source, and has no OTIO or EDL export or deliverable yet; step 8 (interchange) reads the
  feature's cut and will need the join's record or its own cut list.
- **The credits are silent.** A short's credits carry a stretch of its score; the feature's credits card
  is appended with silence, because the join reads each film's mixed sound, not its stems.
- **A reopened link doesn't join.** HV-030-29's resume gap stands: a reopened feature resumes to the
  latest film, without the earlier sequences' films, so it says the sequences aren't joined from a
  reopened link. The route itself takes any films; only the studio's flow is missing.
- **The join is made again, not resumed.** A worker that loses its lease joins the films again from the
  start (no checkpoint). It costs nothing, but a 20-minute feature takes minutes of encoding each time.
- **The PostgreSQL worker checks the casts at completion, not before the join.** The JSON worker checks
  them before and after; the PostgreSQL job store's completion transaction checks them, as for the other
  stages without a ledger-side worker check.
- **Older releases don't know the stage.** A rollback reads a snapshot or a database with a
  `feature-film` job as invalid (no `hv-state` bump), which fails closed; the migration only widens the
  stage check.
- **Not run against S3 here.** The worker's object-store reads and publish use the existing
  `response`/`publishExport` paths; no test runs the join on RustFS.
- **Sharing stays the creator's step.** The flow joins on its own after the last sequence's film, but the
  review link is made when the creator presses "Share the feature with a reviewer", as for a short.
- **The tests run on the mock providers.** No paid render was made; at feature length (about 240 shots,
  ten sequences) the join has not been timed.
