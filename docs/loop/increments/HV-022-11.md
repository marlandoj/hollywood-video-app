# HV-022-11 — The first film voiced by the operator's first vendor

epic: HV-022
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: G14-202609210000 (ElevenLabs primary, Azure as the fallback), already granted
spend_usd: 0.50 — **elevenlabs** text-to-speech only. No video or image provider is used: the picture
runs on `mock` for this proof, so nothing is spent on generation. The expected actual cost is about
**$0.01** (94 characters of dialogue against a plan of $5 per 90,000); the figure above is the
ceiling this increment may not cross.

## Why

Six increments built the second voice vendor and none of them has spoken a word. HV-022-05 wrote its
contract, HV-022-06 its adapter, HV-022-07 the authorized-voice catalogue, HV-022-08 its own $25
budget line, HV-022-09 the crew's vendor order, and HV-022-10 fixed the defect the first attempt at
this proof uncovered — two routes that still mapped a vendor to its contract with a conditional
written when there were two of them.

That last one is the argument for this increment. Everything was proved against fixtures, which is
the right way to build it, and the fixtures all built their profiles the way the contract says to —
so none of them could see that the routes did not. The first live run found it in ninety seconds,
before a cent was spent.

So the question this settles is the one no fixture can: with both vendors authorized and a real key,
does the crew cast ElevenLabs, does a take come back, does it carry the vendor's own cost, and does
the finished film play with it.

**The picture is deliberately not part of this.** Release 1 already proved a film end to end on live
paid generation, twice. Re-proving it here would spend a dollar or two of video generation to learn
nothing new, so the picture pool is `mock` and the only live vendor is the one being proved. The run
costs about a cent.

## What this increment does

No behaviour changes. This is a live proof and its record:

- **A short reel is run through the studio's own front door** on the staging host with the picture on
  `mock` and ElevenLabs authorized, every crew proposal accepted, exactly as `scripts/studio-run.ts`
  drives it. The script gives three speaking parts of different stated sex and age, so the crew's
  casting is visible rather than incidental.
- **`docs/evidence/release-2/elevenlabs-voiced.json`** records it in the `hv-live-proof/1` shape the
  Azure proof used (HV-022-03): the release, the catalogue and its evidence, which voice each
  character was cast and why, every job with its duration, each take's characters and measured
  length, the vendor line before and after, and the film's own spend.
- **`test/elevenlabs-voiced.test.ts`** holds that record to the criteria below, the way
  `test/release-1-run.test.ts` holds the Release 1 run. A proof nobody can re-read is not evidence.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **The crew casts the first vendor.** With both vendors authorized, every speaking character is
   cast an ElevenLabs voice on `hv-audio-voice/4`, and each voice's sex matches the part's stated
   sex — from the account's own catalogue label, never from the voice's name. No character is cast
   on Azure.
2. **A take is dispatched and comes back.** Each line is one reservation and one dispatch, the read
   is delivered at the studio's 48 kHz by the recipe the capability declares, and each recorded take
   has audible, non-silent audio of a plausible length for its line.
3. **The vendor's own line carries it.** The ElevenLabs line moves by the run's holds and no other
   line does; it stays under both its $5 and $15 alerts and far under its $25 cap; and the month's
   generation spend is unchanged, because the picture spent nothing.
4. **The film plays with the vendor's reads.** The shared cut carries the ElevenLabs audio at a mean
   level in the range Release 1 recorded, and the review link opens.
5. **The record is true.** Every job id, character, voice, hold and total in the evidence file is
   what the staging database and the ledger actually hold, and the test reads the file rather than
   restating it.
6. **Nothing else changes.** No source file outside `docs/evidence/**` and the new test is touched.

## Afterwards

The host's picture profile stays on `mock`, and the run's profile is recorded in the evidence so the
next increment starts from a known state. The ElevenLabs holds do not settle on their own — like the
Azure ones they clear when the operator allocates the vendor's invoice, and that is recorded as a
known gap rather than quietly reconciled.
