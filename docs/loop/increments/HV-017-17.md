# HV-017-17 — Identity across sequences (Release 3 step 4)

epic: HV-017
release: 3 "Features"
gate: G20-202610031349 (Release 3's build order, row 4: "Each character's lock is carried into every sequence's renders, and the record can show it per shot")
spend_usd: 0

## Why

Release 3 makes a feature as a series of sequences (HV-030-29), each its own rough cut and final, joined
into one film (HV-030-30). Exit criterion 2 asks for one cast and one look across every sequence: "Each
recurring character is locked once and every shot that character appears in, in every sequence, is
rendered from that lock". The parts table names `HV-017.feature-identity`: "Every shot of each locked
character, in every sequence, is rendered from that character's lock", and the Release 3 record has to
show it with real ids.

A sequence render already cast its shots through `directCast`, the same path as a short, so each render
read whatever lock the cast held when it was admitted. Three things were missing:

- **Nothing recorded which lock a shot used.** The provenance carried the whole cast snapshot, and the
  render record bound the shot's reference images inside a hash. Neither said, per shot, "MARA, lock
  revision X, these two images in this order", so the record could not show criterion 2 per shot.
- **No rule for a lock changed mid-feature.** A feature is made over many sittings. A creator who changes
  a lock after sequence 1 gets later sequences from the new lock and sequence 1 from the old, and nothing
  said so. The join already refused a final whose cast had changed, but only as "stale", and nothing
  before the join told the creator which sequence was made from which lock.
- **No test** that a sequence after the first is conditioned on the lock at all.

## What this increment does

- **The lock record of a shot** (`packages/planner/src/identity-locks.ts`). `shotIdentityLocks(shot,
  casting)` names, for each locked character the shot was cast with, its id and name, the lock's name,
  revision and `lockedAt`, and the lock's images (`assets`: id and SHA-256, in render order). It states
  only what the render was given: each lock's images must be in the shot's own reference set, in the
  lock's order and with the lock's bytes, or it refuses (`IdentityLockError`) rather than record a lock
  the render was not conditioned on. An unlocked character has no entry.
- **Each shot's provenance names it** (`packages/assembler/src/index.ts`). The export's `provenance.json`
  gains `identityLocks` on each shot with a locked character (an optional key on `ProvenanceManifest`'s
  shot). It is computed before the encode, so a shot whose references don't hold its locks refuses before
  any media is written. A shot with no locked character, and so a film with no lock, has the record it had
  before. The JSON is not signed into the C2PA sidecar (which binds the MP4), so the sidecar is unchanged.
- **The rule after a lock changes** (the style bible's, HV-034-02). A render uses the locks current at its
  admission. Sequences rendered after a change use the new revision. A sequence already rendered keeps the
  revision it used and is reported `needsRoughCut` until it has a rough cut made from the current locks.
  Its final can't follow the old rough cut (409, the cast changed after the approved preview), and the
  feature's join refuses its old final as stale, so a joined feature is made from one revision of every
  lock. `lockDrift` names, per character a render showed, the revision it used and the cast's current one:
  a lock changed, removed, or added since (a character rendered unlocked and locked later) is drift; an
  unchanged lock, or a character still unlocked, is not.
- **The desk reads it** (`packages/api/src/identity-locks-api.ts`): `GET
  /api/projects/:projectId/identity-locks`, owner only (401 otherwise, including a review link's token),
  `private, no-store`, 405 for anything but GET. It answers the cast's current `locks` and, per sequence of
  the current split (one entry with `number: null` for a reel or a short), `needsRoughCut`, `drift` (its
  newest rough cut's) and each finished rough cut and final with `current`, `drift` and, per shot, its
  scene and the locks it used. The read is derived from each render's own recorded cast snapshot and shot
  list with the same lock record the provenance writes; the test holds the two equal shot by shot.
- **Docs.** `docs/CASTING.md`: "A lock across a feature's sequences".

No provider is called, no key or vendor is added, no cap or default is raised, and no stored state
changes shape: the only new field is the optional `identityLocks` on a shot in a new export's
`provenance.json`.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Every sequence renders each locked character from its lock.** A three-sequence feature with MARA
   locked to two images (in the order the creator chose) and JUNO to one, and TOM holding one image but no
   lock: in every sequence's rough cut and final, every shot showing MARA or JUNO is sent to the vendor
   with the lock's images, in the lock's order and with the lock's bytes; a shot of TOM alone carries only
   TOM's own image. Real API and worker; the reference vendors are closed HTTP fixtures.
2. **Each shot's provenance names the locks it used.** Every such shot's `identityLocks` names exactly the
   locked characters in it, with the lock's revision and its images' ids and SHA-256 in order; a shot of
   TOM alone (unlocked) carries none. A shot whose references are out of the lock's order, miss one of its
   images, or carry other bytes is refused rather than recorded.
3. **A lock changed after sequence 1.** MARA's lock is changed after sequence 1's rough cut and final.
   Sequences 2 and 3's rough cuts and finals use the new revision, in the request and in the provenance;
   sequence 1 is reported `needsRoughCut` with `drift` naming MARA's old and new revisions; a final from
   sequence 1's old rough cut is refused (409); the join is refused, naming sequence 1's final as stale.
   Sequence 1's rough cut made again uses the new revision and clears the flag, and with its new final the
   feature's join is admitted. Its earlier renders stay listed, with `current: false`.
4. **The desk read.** Owner only: no token, another project's token and a review link's token are 401, and
   POST is 405. Per sequence and shot it lists exactly the locks each render's provenance records.
5. **Drift.** A changed lock, an unlocked look, a look locked after the render and a character who left
   the cast are drift; a sequence that doesn't show the character, an unchanged lock and a character still
   unlocked are not.
6. **Reels and shorts unchanged.** A short with a locked character sends the lock's images as before, names
   the lock per shot, and the desk lists it as one entry (`number: null`); a short with no lock has no
   `identityLocks` in its provenance. The existing reference, lock, sheet, anchor, showrunner and feature
   tests pass unchanged.

- Tests: `packages/api/test/feature-identity-locks.test.ts` (1, 2, 3, 4, 6),
  `packages/planner/test/identity-locks.test.ts` (1 and 2 at the planner, across the stand-in split's
  three sequences; 2's refusals; 5). No existing test changed.

## Known gaps this leaves

- **A feature's storyboard stills still aren't pinned as first frames** (HV-030-29's gap): the desk's
  `GET /direction` and anchors still plan a feature as one render, so a sequence's final starts from the
  script, not from its approved still, and says so. Identity here is the locked reference set.
- **The front door doesn't show the flag.** A flagged sequence is visible at the desk API only; the studio
  doesn't offer to make it again. The join still refuses its old final.
- **The lock is carried, not measured.** Nothing compares the pictures with the lock's images; how closely
  a provider keeps to them is untested (closed vendor fixtures and the mock only; no paid render). That is
  build-order step 9 (identity similarity).
- **A storyboard-mode anchor doesn't reapply references.** When a shot's frame anchor is honoured by a
  storyboard provider, the cast references aren't reapplied (`frameAnchorControl` says so in the same shot
  record); `identityLocks` still names the lock the request carried.
- **Take groups, character sheets, screenplay previews and current-film renders** carry no per-shot
  `identityLocks` (a sheet's and a current film's renders are still conditioned on the lock), and the desk
  read lists film renders only.
- **The desk read is derived**, from each render's recorded cast snapshot and shot list, rather than read
  back from each export's `provenance.json`; the test holds the two equal.
- **A render admitted before a lock change and dispatched after it** uses the lock it was admitted with
  (the rule) and is then flagged; it isn't refused at dispatch.
- **PostgreSQL.** Nothing in storage changed; the read uses the project's jobs on either store, but the
  end-to-end test runs on the JSON stores only.
- **The end-to-end test takes about two and a half minutes** (eight renders with finals, on ffmpeg).
