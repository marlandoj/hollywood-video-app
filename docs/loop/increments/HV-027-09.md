# HV-027-09 — The admission check that the film is still the film compared the plan with itself

epic: HV-027
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

`POST /projects/:id/deliveries/:job` reads the project's jobs once, at the top of the handler, finds
the source film in that snapshot and derives the delivery binding from it. Then, after planning,
idempotency, pricing and the capacity decision, it asks:

```ts
assertDeliverySourceAvailable(binding,source);
```

Every value that check compares — the job id, project, stage, status, the sealed revision, and the
master's path, digest and length — was read out of **that same `source` object** by
`deliveryBindingFor` a few lines earlier. The call could not fail.

Its purpose, in its own test's words, is that *"a film rendered again since the deliverable was
planned is refused by name, not delivered from the old bytes under the new film's name."* At the
route that was not served. The PostgreSQL ledger re-reads the source inside its own transaction, so
that backend was protected further down; the local store had nothing, and a re-render landing between
the snapshot and the admission produced a job that was admitted and then failed at render time, when
the worker opened files that were no longer the ones it was bound to.

## What this increment does

- **The film is read again at admission**, from the store, rather than taken from the snapshot the
  binding was made from.
- **On the local store it is read once more at the last moment**, next to the permission re-read,
  immediately before the job is enqueued — the local backend has no transaction to hold the source
  still, so this is the narrowest window it can offer.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **The snapshot the binding was made from always agrees with it** — shown, because it is why the old
   check proved nothing.
2. **The film read again tells a re-render apart**, and a film that is gone is refused by name.
3. **The route asks the store, not the snapshot, on both backends**: never
   `assertDeliverySourceAvailable(binding,source)`, twice with a fresh read, and on the local path
   before the enqueue.
4. Reverting `packages/api/src/delivery-api.ts` fails criterion 3.

- Tests: `packages/api/test/delivery-admission-source.test.ts`. Criteria 1 and 2 hold on either source
  — they show why the reading matters rather than which reading the route takes; criterion 3 is the
  guard on the route.

## Known gaps this leaves

- **No test drives the route through a re-render.** Reaching this line needs a rendered, sealed
  picture edit, and making the source change between the offer and the POST needs a second render;
  `delivery-route.test.ts` renders one film and never re-renders it.
- **The local store is still not atomic.** The last re-read narrows the window to the time between a
  `get` and an `enqueue`; it does not close it. PostgreSQL closes it, and nothing tests the two
  backends against each other here.
- **The offer list still uses the snapshot**, which is right for an offer — it is a read — and means
  an offer made just before a re-render names the old revision until the page is reloaded.
