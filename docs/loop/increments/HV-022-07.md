## Why

The contract (HV-022-05) and the adapter (HV-022-06) can speak to the service. Neither authorizes a
voice: in this application a voice is castable only when the operator's own evidence says so, the
way the Azure catalogue works. This increment is that catalogue for the second vendor.

The vendor's billing does not look like Azure's. Azure bills per character, so a take's hold is a
rate times the line's ceiling. ElevenLabs sells a **monthly character allowance** — the operator's
account answers as Starter with 90,000 characters a month — so a take's cost is a share of that
allowance, and the honest hold is the plan's price for one period over the characters it buys, times
the line's ceiling. The catalogue carries that arithmetic with the page the price came from.

One refusal is new and deliberate: a **free** ElevenLabs plan may not use the output commercially,
and a film shared from this studio is used. A catalogue built from a free account is refused rather
than quietly produced.

## What this increment does

- **`packages/generator/src/elevenlabs-policy-catalogue.ts`**: builds `hv-audio-policy/4` records
  from four saved snapshots — the account's subscription, the account's voice list, the licence
  pages reviewed, and the price page — plus the operator's chosen voice ids and the plan price they
  read. Every authorized voice must appear in the account's own list. The permission revision binds
  the account, the authorized voices' entries and the licence; the price revision binds the plan's
  rate and the line ceiling. A voice appearing elsewhere in the account moves neither.
- **`elevenLabsTakeHoldUsd(planUsdPerPeriod, characterLimit, maxCharacters)`**: the hold, to six
  decimal places, refusing a missing price or allowance rather than assuming one.
- **`audioPolicy`** accepts the vendor: its own schema (`hv-audio-policy/4`), its own voice-id shape,
  its pinned model, and its line ceiling of 10,000 characters.
- **`scripts/audio-policy.ts --vendor elevenlabs`** writes the catalogue on the host beside the
  Azure one — other vendors' authorized voices are kept, not replaced — saving the subscription, the
  voice list, the licence and price pages and a manifest as evidence, mode 600. The key is sent in
  its own header and never printed.

Casting still uses Azure: which vendor the crew casts from, and the vendor's own budget line, are
the next increment.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **The catalogue carries the account's own rate**, each policy passes the application's own
   validator, and the manifest records the account without a credential in it.
   - Test: `packages/generator/test/elevenlabs-policy-catalogue.test.ts`.
2. **A free plan is refused**, as are a voice the account no longer lists, a voice id the service
   would never issue, an empty or oversized authorization list, missing licence or price pages, and
   unreadable account or voice records.
   - Same test.
3. **The revisions bind what the evidence says**: another voice in the account moves nothing; new
   terms move the permission; a new price page or plan price moves the price, not the permission.
   - Same test.
4. **Nothing else changes.** The Azure catalogue, audio job and adapter suites pass unchanged.
