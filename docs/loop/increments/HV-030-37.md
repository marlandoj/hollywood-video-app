# HV-030-37 — `studio-run.ts --resume` carries on a stopped feature without paying twice

epic: HV-030
release: 3 "Features"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

Release 3's live run (G23, $135 declared) stopped on "The Tide Clock". It got through the pitch, the
read-through, the desk steps before the look (WREN and OSWIN locked, the continuity repair), the look,
and sequence 1's storyboard and rough cut (23 paid stills), which was approved. Sequence 1's final then
rendered 8 of its 21 shots, at about $0.42 each. fal refused shot 9 (422: the prompt was over 2,500
characters). The job retried and failed, and `studio-run.ts` stopped with `outcome: stopped`. About
$6.50 was spent.

HV-019-19 (#379) fits the prompt. It keeps every prompt that was within the limit byte for byte, so the
8 rendered shots keep their input hashes. But nothing could carry the run on:

- `studio-run.ts` could only pitch a new project. That pays again for the read-through, the look, the 23
  stills and the 8 shots.
- Asking for sequence 1's final again with the studio's default key answers with the failed job.
- Shot reuse read only finished films. A failed final's checkpointed shots could not be reused. And a
  feature's sequence refused reuse outright ("Selective reuse applies to a whole film").
- `release-3-run.ts` counts a final's providers from that final's own route decisions. A reused shot
  has none. It would count as `unknown`, and the contract would read that as a second vendor.

## What this increment does

- **`--resume`** in `scripts/studio-run.ts`. It reads the earlier record (`--out`) and the token file
  beside it, and carries on the same project. It refuses before any request if the record isn't a
  planned feature, if the script's sha256 differs from the record's, or if the token names another
  project.
- **The studio rebuilds the feature from its own state** (`resumeFeature` in
  `packages/frontend/src/studio.js`). It reads the project's jobs and approvals, the split and each
  sequence's newest finished final (`GET /feature-film`), and the cast. It takes the sequences in order:
  - A sequence with a finished final is finished again. Every finishing pass asks with the key fixed by
    that film, so the studio answers with what it made. Nothing is rendered or paid twice.
  - The first sequence without one is where the run stopped. With a rough cut, the studio stands on it.
    An approval already given is not given again. A final still rendering is waited on. A final that
    failed is asked for again under `crew-final-<rough cut id>-retry-<n>`, where n counts the finals
    of that rough cut that failed, with `reuseUnchanged: true`.
  - Without a rough cut: the look for sequence 1, or the next sequence after the one before.
- **The tone and the plan's facts come from the record.** The studio doesn't keep what the crew said
  (HV-016-09). The Composer reads the tone. The credits read whether the Continuity Supervisor compared
  anything. The read-through and the plan are never asked for again.
- **A failed final's shots are reusable** (`packages/planner/src/shot-reuse.ts`). A failed job keeps
  the shots it rendered in its checkpoint, and its execution inventory pins each one's record by
  revision. The API reads the records back from the job's clip manifest (`checkpointedShots`), and a
  record is reused only if the inventory names its revision. The worker checks the same before it copies
  a shot, and copies its files by sha256.
- **A sequence's final may reuse shots.** The plan is made for that sequence and its style bible.
  Choosing shots to render fresh, and reuse in a sequence's rough cut, are still refused.
- **The record keeps every earlier step.** The resumed record starts from the earlier one whole:
  `readThrough`, `plan`, `deskBeforeLook`, `roughCut`, `secondsAt`. It adds an entry to `resumes`:
  - what it replaced (the outcome, error, feature, final and review it found);
  - each step `kept` or `made`, and each sequence's rough cut and final `kept`, `made`, `retried` or
    `waited`;
  - for a retried final, its job, the failed one, and how many shots were reused and rendered.

  A link already shared for the same film is kept, not made again.
- **`release-3-run.ts` counts a reused shot under the provider that rendered it**, read from the job
  its `reusedFrom` names (`reusedSources`). The same goes for quality routing.
- **The runbook** (`docs/loop/increments/HV-030-31.md`, step 2) gains the resume command.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **The stop, reproduced.** A two-sequence feature runs through the real API and worker on mock. A
   stand-in for fal's 422 refuses one marked shot. Sequence 1's final fails with its first 2 shots
   checkpointed, and the run stops.
2. **The same project, nothing asked for again.** With the refusal lifted, `--resume` finishes the
   feature in the same project. It sends no new project, script, rights, read-through, plan or cast
   approval. Sequence 1 has one rough cut, and only sequence 2's rough cut is approved.
3. **The rendered shots are reused at $0.** Sequence 1's final is asked for again under
   `crew-final-<rough cut id>-retry-1`. Its first 2 shots come back from the failed final with the same
   shot ids and video sha256s, and `reusedFrom` names it. No provider call is made for them. The ledger
   reads $0.
4. **An honest record.** The resumed record keeps the earlier `readThrough`, `plan`, `roughCut`,
   `secondsAt`, `startedAt`, `script` and `tone`. Its `resumes` entry says what was kept, retried and
   made, the shots reused and rendered, and what it replaced. It holds the joined, shared feature as
   `release-3-run.ts` reads it. `release-3-run.ts` counts every reused shot under the provider that
   rendered it, never `unknown`. No token or link is in the record or on the terminal.
5. **Run again, nothing is admitted.** A second `--resume` of the finished feature admits no job, makes
   no provider call and keeps the shared link. A sequence's final still can't choose shots to render
   fresh.
6. **A wrong record is refused first.** `--resume` with another script exits non-zero before any
   request.
7. **Reverting the change fails the new tests** (checked). Restoring `scripts/studio-run.ts`,
   `packages/frontend/src/studio.js`, `packages/planner/src/shot-reuse.ts`,
   `packages/planner/src/sequences.ts` and `packages/api/src/server.ts` from `main` fails tests 2, 3 and
   4 of `test/release-3-resume.test.ts`: main's script ignores `--resume` and pitches a new project.
   Restoring `scripts/release-3-run.ts` too fails the file on the export it imports, and
   `packages/frontend/test/studio-retry.test.js` on its count of keys. Restoring only the API and
   planner fails tests 2 and 3 on the studio's 400. Restoring only `shot-reuse.ts` fails test 2: no
   shot is reused.

- Tests: `test/release-3-resume.test.ts` (1 to 6; 4 pass), `packages/frontend/test/studio-retry.test.js`
  (its key count, updated; 5 pass). Unchanged and passing: `test/release-3-driver.test.ts` (17 pass),
  the frontend suite, and the API, planner and queue suites on shot reuse, feature sequences and
  execution capture.

## Known gaps

- **Only a failed final's shots are reused.** A final that was cancelled (a budget stop) keeps its
  checkpoint too, but isn't read. A failed rough cut isn't asked for again under a fresh key; its
  default key answers with it. The live run needs neither.
- **Reuse needs the same inputs.** A shot is reused only if its input hash and the provider plan's
  revision are unchanged. On staging that needs HV-019-19 deployed and the pool and quality results file
  as they were. If not, the shots are rendered again and paid for, and `reusedShots` says so. Check it
  after sequence 1.
- **The crew's words come from the record.** The tone, whether stills are pinned and the Supervisor's
  comparison count are the earlier record's. The plan's notes aren't recorded; the credits read only
  whether the Supervisor compared anything.
- **A failed final's media has no expiry of its own.** It is kept while its project is.
- **Overlap with HV-019-19.** Both change `packages/planner/src/shot-reuse.ts`, in different functions.
- Nothing has run on staging. The live run's resume is the check.
