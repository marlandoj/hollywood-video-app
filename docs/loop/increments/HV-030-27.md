## Why

Release 2 is done and acknowledged (G6-202610030339). G19-202610030430 set Release 3's scope: a
feature made as a series of sequences, proven on one 15–20 minute feature of about 200–240 shots,
rendered live. The run declares about $120. English only. No voice meetings.

The roadmap has Release 3's slice table and that scope, and nothing more. Nothing says when Release 3
is done, and the loop has no build order to take work from. HV-030-22 wrote Release 2's criteria
before any of its run was built. This increment does the same for Release 3. It is a proposal for
Kevin. It writes no code and spends nothing.

## What this increment does

It adds three things under "## Release 3" in `docs/ROADMAP.md`:

- **"Release 3 exit criteria (proposed; agreed at G6)"**, nine numbered criteria:
  1. One feature, from pasted script to one shared film, with live picture. 15–20 minutes, about
     200–240 shots, pitched as a `feature`, split into sequences by the Showrunner, and joined into
     one film with one review link. No mock shot in the shared film.
  2. One cast and one look across every sequence. Every shot of a locked character is rendered from
     its lock, and the Continuity Supervisor's report covers every sequence boundary.
  3. Reviewed on a second device, with comments in at least three sequences and a decision that names
     its stage.
  4. Every Release 3 slice exercised with real ids, or deferred to a gate entry that exists. The
     second video vendor can be exercised only after a G3 entry approves it.
  5. Within the declared spend (about $120), within each line's limit, and within the feature's own
     film limit. The run raises no cap or default.
  6. Consented casting and the public-figure refusal hold for everything the crew and the Showrunner
     write. English only.
  7. A signed C2PA sidecar on the shared feature that verifies.
  8. Every step names the surface it used.
  9. The operator's acknowledgement.
- **A parts table** in Release 2's row format, 15 parts across the nine epics in the Release 3
  table, each with one of Release 2's surfaces.
- **A spend-lines table**: generation $450 (the feature declares about $120, the paid benchmark about
  $20), voice $25, music $10, crew $25.
- **A build order** of 16 increments: the feature format and its film limit first, the live run last.

Release 2's section is unchanged byte for byte. `test/release-2-contract.ts` reads Release 2 as the
text between "## Release 2" and the first "## Release 3", so nothing added under Release 3 reaches it.

## Constraints found

These shape the build order. Each is from the code or from a release record.

1. **Formats.** A reel is at most 90 s and a short at most 600 s. A 15–20 minute feature (900–1,200 s)
   needs a new `feature` format. Today `readThroughInput`, the plan step and `styleCardInput` refuse
   anything but `reel` and `short`.
2. **Shots per render.** One render admits at most 24 shots on the free tier and 60 with an operator
   grant (`TIERS` in `packages/queue/src/index.ts`). A 200–240-shot feature can't be one render. At 24
   shots a sequence, it is 9 or 10 sequences of about 2 minutes each.
3. **The per-film limit.** `HV_FILM_SPEND_CAP_USD` is $40 per project, checked on what a film has
   spent plus what it holds. A render holds three attempts at the dearest eligible estimate
   (HV-019-06). On the anchored profile one 24-shot sequence holds 24 × $0.42 × 3 = $30.24. A whole
   feature in one project spends $84–101 of video and holds up to $30 more at its last sequence. So
   either:
   - **the feature is one project with its own film limit** (proposed: $150, about $101 of spend
     plus one sequence's hold, with room for retakes), and reel and short keep $40; or
   - **each sequence is its own project** under today's $40. Then nothing bounds the feature as a
     whole, and identity, continuity, review and assembly have to cross projects in a studio with no
     accounts (ADR-0018).

   This increment proposes the first. Setting any film limit above $40 is a budget decision, so it
   is Kevin's at G6.
4. **Price.** G19's $70–85 is Kling 2.5 at $0.35 per 5 s. The anchored profile (`live-film-anchored`),
   which Release 1 used to keep characters close, bills Kling O3 at $0.084 a second: $0.42 per 5 s,
   so 200–240 shots cost $84–101. That leaves $19–36 of the $120 for retakes, about 45–85 shots.
   Stills cost about $0.003 each, under $1 for the feature. The read-through quotes Kling 2.5's price
   whatever the profile is (`ESTIMATE_VIDEO_SPEC`), so it would understate an anchored feature by
   a fifth. Build step 1 fixes the quote.
5. **Voice holds.** An ElevenLabs take holds the price of 10,000 characters (`elevenLabsTakeHoldUsd`
   with `ELEVENLABS_MAX_LINE_CHARACTERS`), about $0.56 a take. The voice line already holds $14.44 of
   its $25, so about 18 more takes fit. A 15–20 minute script has roughly 150–250 lines. Holds also
   count toward the film limit. Build step 6 sizes a take's hold to its own line, which holds less
   and raises nothing. The operator can also settle the existing holds against the invoice with
   `scripts/reconcile-audio.ts`.
6. **Music.** A generated cue is 10–300 s (`MUSIC_CUE_MAX_SEC`), so a 20-minute score is at least four
   cues. At $0.15 a minute that is about $3, inside the $10 line. A sound library asset is at most
   600 s (`MAX_SOUND_SECONDS`), so the mix is built per sequence or the limit is revisited.
7. **Time.** Release 1's ten-shot short took about 32 minutes from its rough cut to its final, about
   3.2 minutes a shot, one job at a time per project on the free tier. At that rate 200–240 shots take about 11–13 hours. Project tokens live
   72 hours (`PROJECT_TOKEN_TTL_MS`), so the run fits, but only just with retakes and review. HTTP
   requests are cut at 255 s (`idleTimeout`); Release 1 recorded a route that passed it on a 50 s film.
   Anything that grows with film length must be measured at 20 minutes. That is what the mock
   rehearsal (build step 15) is for.
8. **The second vendor.** It can't spend before a G3 entry approves it. Its part is exercised after
   G3 or deferred to the entry recording the decision.
9. **Native camera paths** need a provider that takes camera control. None in today's pool is wired
   for it. If the approved pool has none, `HV-020.native-camera` is deferred to a gate entry.

## What G6 asks of Kevin

1. **Agree the criteria, parts and build order**, or amend them.
2. **Choose the feature's film limit.** Proposed: one project per feature with a $150 limit for the
   `feature` format, while reel and short keep $40. The run still declares about $120 of spend; the
   limit also covers what is held while a sequence renders.
3. **Confirm the approvals.** Proposed: the look is approved once for the whole feature, then each
   sequence gets its rough cut and final. That is 1 + 2 × 10 = 21 approvals for a 10-sequence
   feature, against 30 if every sequence also approved its own look.
4. **Confirm the picture profile.** Proposed: `live-film-anchored` at $0.42 a shot, since it keeps
   characters closer. Kling 2.5 alone ($0.35 a shot) is cheaper and matches G19's figure.
5. **The second video vendor.** Name one for a G3 decision, or defer `HV-019.second-vendor`.

## Known gaps this leaves

- **Nothing is built.** Every part in the table is new work, except the titles, credits and masks
  that `HV-025` reuses.
- **The contract test doesn't exist yet.** Build step 14 writes it, as HV-030-22 did for Release 2.
  Until then these tables are prose that no test reads.
- **The 21 approvals are many for one creator.** The Showrunner may need to offer "approve the rest
  like this one". That is a product decision for step 2.
- **"Measured quality" depends on the benchmark.** Until HV-037 measures something, quality routing
  has nothing honest to rank by, so step 10 waits for step 9.
- **The operator view is a rolling 30-day window.** The feature run, the paid benchmark and what is
  already recorded (about $17) share it, all under the $450 alert.
