# HV-034-02 — The style bible: written once, read by every sequence (Release 3 step 3)

epic: HV-034
release: 3 "Features"
gate: G20-202610031349 (Release 3's build order, row 3: "Written once by the Showrunner from the creator's answers and style card; every sequence's plan reads it")
spend_usd: 0

## Why

Release 3 makes a feature as a series of sequences (HV-030-29), each its own rough cut and final. Every
sequence's render read only its own shots: the cast kept a character's description the same, but
nothing stated the feature's look, so sequence 7 had no reason to look like sequence 1. The parts table
names `HV-034.style-bible`: "The Showrunner writes the feature's style bible once, and every sequence's
plan reads it". Release 3's exit criterion 2 asks for one cast and one look across every sequence, and
criterion 6 for the crew's rules (consented casting, no public figures) to hold for everything the
Showrunner writes.

## What this increment does

- **The record** (`packages/planner/src/style-bible.ts`, `hv-style-bible/1`): six bounded lines (look,
  palette, lighting, lens and framing, tone, sound), each principal character and each recurring
  location, with its version, the script version it was written for, its source and a revision hash.
  The studio names the locations (from the scene headings) and the characters (the cast's own records:
  a character's entry is its cast appearance and default wardrobe; a consented real person is never
  re-described). Every string passes the gate and the public-figure check alone, and the whole bible
  passes the gate together. A stored bible is checked for shape, limits and revision.
- **The Showrunner writes it** (`packages/planner/src/crew/style-bible.ts`) at the plan step of a
  `feature`, after the split and before the crew's plan, from the creator's answers, the style card
  the creator attached (now accepted by `POST /crew/plan`, validated and gated whole, stored nowhere)
  and the screenplay. With a crew model it is asked for the six lines and each named location's
  description (`crew-style-bible` on the crew line, up to 1,500 output tokens; a flagged script is never
  sent). Its answer is used field by field: a missing, non-text, oversized, gate-refused or
  public-figure line, and an oversized, misshapen or unnamed location, is dropped with its reason
  (`dropped`) and the stand-in's line stands in. No JSON, nothing usable, or a whole that fails the gate
  is unusable (`unusableReason`, logged with `step: "style-bible"`), and the stand-in writes it all.
  Without a model the stand-in writes it deterministically, at $0.
- **Written once.** Planning the feature again keeps the bible: nothing is asked or spent, and it is
  carried forward to the current cast and locations (a new version only if that changed it).
- **The crew's plan reads it.** A feature's plan prompt includes the bible's lines and locations; a
  reel's and a short's prompt is unchanged.
- **Kept on the project** as `styleBible`, in the same write as the format, sequences, cast and
  direction (additive; no `hv-state` bump). A project load, the PostgreSQL project service and a state
  snapshot carry it, and refuse one that isn't the studio's, changed after it was written, sits on
  anything but a feature, or names a screenplay version the project doesn't have. Planning a feature
  again as a reel or a short removes it.
- **Every sequence's render reads it.** A sequence render carries the bible and names its revision in
  `sequence.bibleRevision` (the job record shows which bible a sequence's render used). Each shot's
  prompt gains one block after its cast and shot direction: the six lines and the shot's location.
  Admission, the worker, re-renders and snapshots compute it the same way (`bibleShots`), and the request
  key names the revision. The JSON and PostgreSQL admissions refuse a render of a bible the project no
  longer has. A feature planned before this increment has no bible and renders as it did.
- **The rule after a change.** A sequence render reads the bible current at its admission. A creator's
  edit makes a new revision: sequences not yet made read it; a sequence already made keeps the revision
  it read and is reported `needsRoughCut` until it has a rough cut of the current one, and its final must
  read the same revision as its approved rough cut (409 "The style bible changed after this sequence's
  rough cut. Make its rough cut again, so its final keeps the bible's look.").
- **The join keeps one look (rebased onto HV-030-30).** The feature's join reads each sequence's newest
  final that read the current bible, refuses one made with an older bible or none, naming the sequence,
  and records `bibleRevision` in its plan (`hv-feature-film/1`, an optional key inside its revision).
- **At the desk.** `GET /api/projects/:projectId/style-bible` (owner only, as every desk read) answers the
  bible and, per sequence, `madeWith` and `needsRoughCut`. `PUT` the same path edits the six lines and
  any location's description with the same validation, refused whole with its reason (400), or 409 on a
  stale revision. A reel or a short has none (404).
- **The front door** shows a short summary at a feature's Approval 1 (look, palette, lighting, lens, and
  how many characters and locations) and sends the attached style card with a feature's plan. A reel's
  and a short's requests and words are unchanged.
- **Docs.** `docs/CREW.md`: the persona's new job, the bible, the rule after an edit and the front door.

No provider is called, no key or vendor is added, no cap or default is raised. A paid bible answer is a
crew-line call at the creator's "Plan the film", like the split's and the plan's; nothing renders or
spends on generation on its own.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Written once per feature.** A feature's plan writes a bible (version 1) with the cast's characters
   and the script's locations; the Showrunner's second note says so; it is stored on the project,
   reloads unchanged and is served at the desk to the owner only (401 otherwise). Planning the feature
   again keeps it (`kept: true`, same revision, "Kept the feature's style bible") and never asks the
   model for it again, while the split and the plan are asked again.
2. **The stand-in is deterministic.** The same answers, card and script give the same bible and
   revision at $0; the look, tone and sound come from this film's answers, then the card, then the
   stand-in's conventions; creator words that together fail the gate leave only the conventions.
3. **The model's words, field by field.** A usable answer is used with the studio's location names. An
   oversized, gate-refused, public-figure, non-text or missing line, and an oversized, unknown,
   misshapen or repeated location, is dropped with its reason and replaced by the stand-in's; nothing is
   cut short. No JSON, nothing usable, or a whole that fails the gate is unusable, and the paid answer
   stays on the crew line as `crew-style-bible`. A flagged script, or no model, asks nothing.
4. **The cast speaks for the characters.** A character's entry is its cast record's appearance and
   default wardrobe; a consented real person is never re-described; a character the script doesn't
   mention is left out; an appearance naming a public figure is left out with its reason.
5. **Every sequence reads the same revision.** Each sequence in the plan answer names the bible's
   revision; renders of sequence 1 and the last carry the bible and name it, and every shot prompt of
   both holds the same style block, its own location, and the same cast description.
6. **The creator's edit and the rule after it.** An oversized, public-figure or gate-refused line, an
   unknown location, a characters field or a stale revision is refused with its reason (400 or 409) and
   nothing saved; a valid edit is version 2 by the creator; the sequence made before it is reported
   `needsRoughCut`; its final from the old rough cut is refused with 409; a new rough cut is a new job
   that reads the new revision and clears the flag.
7. **Kept and carried.** A state snapshot reads the bible back unchanged and refuses a tampered,
   misshapen, misplaced (not a feature) or other-screenplay bible; a render's bible rides in the
   snapshot and a mismatched, unnamed, unsequenced or tampered one is refused. In PostgreSQL the bible
   is kept and edited, and admission refuses a render of an edited-away bible or of none.
8. **Reels and shorts unchanged.** A reel and a short get no bible and no Showrunner note, have none at
   the desk (404 to read and edit), render with no bible and no `Style bible` in any prompt; planning a
   feature again as a short removes its bible; the crew's plan prompt for one is unchanged; the front
   door sends a reel's and a short's plan exactly as before and shows no bible.
9. **The joined feature keeps one look.** The join is made of finals that read the current bible; a final
   made with an older bible, or none, is refused naming its sequence; a feature with no bible joins as
   before; the join's plan records the revision, and a changed or malformed one is refused. End to end,
   the front door's join of three sequences records the bible every final read.
10. Reverting the change fails the named tests (checked): `bibleShots` returning the shots unchanged
   fails 4 tests in the two style-bible files; writing the bible afresh on every plan fails 2 route
   tests.

## Known gaps this leaves

- **The bible is read, not enforced.** It is text in every sequence's shot prompts; no picture is
  compared against it, and how closely a provider keeps to it is untested (mock and FLUX Schnell
  storyboard renders only, no paid render).
- **Characters are the cast's, as at the plan step.** A cast edit at the desk after the plan isn't in
  the bible until the feature is planned again. Renders take each character's look from the cast
  itself, so the picture follows the cast either way. Carrying each character's lock into every
  sequence's renders is step 4 (HV-017).
- **Nothing re-renders after an edit.** A sequence made with an older bible is flagged `needsRoughCut`
  and its final refused until its rough cut is made again; the front door has no edit control yet (the
  desk API has), and doesn't show the flag.
- **The plan's model answer still won't fit a feature** (HV-030-29's gap): its prompt now carries the
  bible, but a live model's plan of ~240 shots is cut off and the stand-in directs it. The bible reaches
  every render through its own prompt block regardless.
- **The style card is read at the plan step only when the studio still holds it.** A resumed project
  can't send it again, and the plan step's card is only read for a feature.
- **The desk shows `madeWith` from the jobs of the current sequence plan.** A replan that changes the
  split starts the per-sequence record afresh, as `GET /spend` does.
- **A rollback refuses a sequence render that read a bible.** An older release's `validateSequenceRef`
  takes exact keys, so a state snapshot holding a job with `bibleRevision` is refused (fail closed), and
  the older release forgets the project's bible on its next write (no `hv-state` bump, as in HV-030-28
  and HV-030-29).
