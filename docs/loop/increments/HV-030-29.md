# HV-030-29 — The Showrunner: a feature made as sequences (Release 3 step 2)

epic: HV-030
release: 3 "Features"
gate: G20-202610031349 (Release 3's build order; the look approved once for the whole feature, then the rough cut and final per sequence)
spend_usd: 0

## Why

Release 3's promise is a feature, produced as a series of sequences. Step 1 (HV-030-28) added the
`feature` format and its $150 film limit, and left its first gap open: "No sequences yet". A feature's
plan step directed one render's shots, and rendering a feature of more than 24 scenes was refused at
admission ("This tier supports screenplays with up to 24 scenes"). One render is at most 24 shots on
the free tier (`TIERS.free.maxShots`); a 15–20 minute feature is about 200–240.

Build-order row 2 asks for a Showrunner that splits a feature into sequences of at most 24 shots, each
with its own rough cut and final, with the look approved once. At G20 Kevin decided the approvals: the
look once for the whole feature, then the rough cut and the final per sequence, about 21 approvals for
about ten sequences.

## What this increment does

- **The feature's shots** (`packages/planner/src/sequences.ts`, `featureShots`). Each scene is planned
  on its own in a 24-shot render: one shot per beat; a scene of more than 24 beats grouped into 24 shots;
  a scene with accepted coverage keeps its coverage. A sequence of at most 24 such shots plans exactly
  those shots in its own render, so the feature's shot ids, prompts and seeds don't depend on where the
  sequences break. The plan step directs all of them once.
- **The Showrunner** (`packages/planner/src/crew/showrunner.ts`; `SHOWRUNNER` on the crew roster in
  `personas.ts`, `speaks: "sequence-plan"`, not in `PERSONAS`: it asks no questions). Its one tool is a
  list of sequence boundaries by scene number. It runs on the crew model path: shown each scene's
  heading and shot count (gated before it is sent), asked for `{"sequences": [{"firstScene",
  "lastScene"}]}`, metered on the crew line as `crew-showrunner`. The studio counts the shots and
  validates the split (`splitProblem`): every scene exactly once, in order, no gap, no overlap, at least
  one scene and at most 24 shots each. An unusable answer falls back to the stand-in's deterministic
  greedy split with `unusableReason` (`no_json`, `bad_shape`, or the vendor's), and the API logs it with
  `step: "showrunner"`. With no model, or a feature that fits one render, the stand-in splits and nothing
  is spent.
- **The plan step** for a `feature` runs the Showrunner first, then the crew's plan over the feature's
  shots, and stores the plan (`hv-sequence-plan/1`) on the project as `sequences` in the same write as
  the format, cast and direction. A reel or a short has none, and planning a feature again as one removes
  it. The answer adds `sequences` for a feature, and the Showrunner's note goes first. A scene whose
  accepted coverage is past 24 shots, or a feature past 240 shots, is refused with 409 before any model
  is asked. The Continuity Supervisor reads the feature's own shots. A project's direction holds up to
  240 shots (`DIRECTION_ENTRY_LIMIT`, was 60), because the crew directs every sequence's shots at once.
- **The sequence is the render unit.** `POST /jobs` for a feature with a plan takes `sequence` (1 to N)
  and refuses a rough cut or final without one (400); a reel or a short naming one is refused too. The
  job carries `sequence: {number, of, firstScene, lastScene, planRevision}`. Every place a film render's
  shots are derived reads the feature's shots and keeps the sequence's (`filmPlan` and `inSequence`):
  admission, the worker, the PostgreSQL ledger's admission and dispatch checks, state snapshots,
  `renderShots` (re-renders, dialogue and lip-sync sources) and the desk's storyboard sources. A render
  of a reel or a short goes through `sourcePlan` exactly as before. A stale plan (older screenplay, or
  coverage that no longer fits) is refused with 409. A final must follow its own sequence's approved
  rough cut, checked at admission, in the PostgreSQL ledger and in the worker. The request key names the
  sequence. Selective reuse is refused for a sequence. A job's sequence is validated on enqueue and in a
  snapshot, and only a film's rough cut or final may carry one.
- **Spend.** Each sequence render is its own admission under the feature's one film limit ($150).
  `GET /spend` adds per-sequence `spentUsd` and `heldUsd` for a feature; a reel's and a short's answer
  is unchanged.
- **The front door** (`packages/frontend/src/studio.js`). For a feature, Approval 1 (the plan, the look
  and the cast) is asked once and renders the first sequence's storyboard and rough cut: an animatic
  admits at most one render's 24 shots, so a storyboard of the whole feature isn't possible and each later
  sequence's storyboard comes with its own rough cut. Each sequence's final follows its rough cut; its
  film offers "Approve sequence k and make sequence k+1's rough cut". The headings count 1 + 2 × N
  approvals. Each approval shows the sequence's and the feature's running cost. A sequence is voiced
  (its own scenes' lines only) and scored like a short, but carries no title or credits, and the last
  sequence says the sequences aren't joined yet. A reel and a short send and show exactly what they did.
- **Docs.** `docs/CREW.md`: the persona, the split, the render unit and the front door's feature flow.

No provider is called, no key or vendor is added, and no cap or default is raised. Nothing the
Showrunner does renders or spends on generation; a paid split answer is a crew-line call at the
creator's "Plan the film", like the plan step's own.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **The split.** A 34-scene feature (one scene of 30 beats) splits into sequences of at most 24 shots
   that cover every scene exactly once, in order; the 30-beat scene is one sequence of 24 grouped shots.
   Each sequence's shots are exactly a 24-shot render of its scenes, and the feature's shots are the
   same under another valid split.
2. **The stand-in is deterministic.** The same script gives the same plan and revision, with no spend.
3. **The model proposes, the studio validates.** A usable split is the model's, with the studio's shot
   counts. A gap, an overlap, a sequence past 24 shots, sequences out of order, an empty sequence, a
   missing last scene and an unknown scene are each refused: the greedy split is used, `unusableReason`
   is `bad_shape`, and the paid answer stays on the crew line. Non-JSON is `no_json`. A feature that
   fits one render asks no model.
4. **Kept and carried.** The plan is stored on the project beside `feature`, reloads unchanged, survives
   a state snapshot round-trip, and is removed when the film is planned again as a short. A tampered,
   misplaced (not a feature) or stale-version plan is refused by `validateSnapshot`; a malformed sequence
   on a job, or one on a job that can't render it, is refused.
5. **The render unit.** A feature renders only by naming a sequence (400 otherwise, and for 0, N+1,
   strings and fractions); a repeat is the same job and another sequence another job; the job renders
   only its sequence's shots; a plan for an older screenplay is refused with 409; a final of another
   sequence than its approved rough cut is refused with 409.
6. **The film limit per admission.** Three 13-shot sequences under a feature limit of 2.5 holds: the
   first two are admitted, the third refused (429), and `GET /spend` reports each sequence's hold.
7. **The front door, end to end on mock providers.** Real API and worker: the cast is approved once;
   sequence 1's rough cut, its final, then sequence 2's rough cut and final, in that order, each render
   naming its sequence and rendering only its shots; no title, credits, editorial sequence or joined film
   is made; the headings count 5 approvals; asking for a third sequence is refused.
8. **Reels and shorts unchanged.** A reel and a short get no sequences, no Showrunner note, the same
   `GET /spend` answer, and render whole; a reel's studio requests and words are the same
   (`studio.test.js` holds the whole route), and its step titles are `STEP_TITLES`.

## Known gaps this leaves

- **The sequences aren't joined (step 7).** Each sequence's final is its own film, shared and downloaded
  as "sequence k". No title, credits or joined review link exist yet, and nothing says otherwise.
- **The desk still plans a feature as one render.** `GET /direction`, a shot's direction save and its
  anchors use the 24- or 60-shot plan, so a feature's shots past that can't be edited at the desk, and
  the studio doesn't pin a sequence's storyboard stills on a profile that starts from a still (the
  anchored profile G20 chose). It says so on the rough cut. Step 4 (identity across sequences) needs this.
- **The plan step's model answer won't fit a feature.** Directing about 240 shots needs far more than
  the plan step's 6,000 output tokens, so a live model's plan for a feature will be cut off and the
  stand-in directs it (with `unusableReason: "cut_off"`). The split is a separate, small call.
- **The read-through and the plan can still disagree** when a scene has more than 24 beats (the
  read-through spreads 240 shots over the feature; the plan groups that scene into 24) or the feature is
  past 240 shots.
- **A feature isn't resumed sequence by sequence.** A reopened link resumes to the latest rough cut or
  final; a resumed rough cut's final names its own sequence, but there is no "next sequence" from a
  resumed final.
- **Direction history is heavier for a feature.** A direction version of 240 shots is kept up to 100
  times per project; nothing trims it beyond that.
- **A rollback refuses a feature directed past 60 shots.** An older release reads the larger direction as
  invalid state, and forgets the plan and a job's sequence (no `hv-state` bump, as in HV-030-28).
- **The tests run on the mock and FLUX Schnell storyboard pools.** No paid render was made.
