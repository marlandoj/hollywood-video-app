# HV-019-07 — A film that was interrupted said less about itself

epic: HV-019
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

A critic pass over `packages/queue` found that the evidence a film carries about its own quality
depends on whether its render happened to be interrupted.

`degradedShots` and `shotReviews` are declared empty at `worker.ts:361`, after the checkpoint's
clips are restored. The shot loop opens with `if (index < resumed) continue;`, so nothing can
re-add a shot an earlier incarnation rendered. And the review flush sat **below** the loop, so an
incarnation that died mid-film flagged nothing at all — not even the shots it had already rendered,
degraded and checkpointed.

Both halves fail on the ordinary retry path, which is the path the checkpoint exists for: a lapsed
lease, a dead worker, a provider that went away. Measured on a three-shot film whose second shot is
discontinuous against its first:

```
CONTROL (one uninterrupted pass)
  delivered mp4 metadata: "degraded_shots=shot-1-2"
  operator review queue:  [ "shot-1-2" ]

INTERRUPTED (the host for shot 3 goes away; the job is requeued at checkpointShots=2, then resumed)
  after incarnation 1, review queue: []          <- nothing flagged, ever
  delivered mp4 metadata: "degraded_shots=none"  <- same clips, same fingerprints, same bytes
  operator review queue after completion: []
```

The two runs deliver byte-identical clips. Only the interruption differs.

AC-012 asks for the degraded note in the final MP4 and for the below-threshold shots to reach the
operator review queue; FR-036 is the queue. A report that undercounts in silence is the worst
failure available to a report, and this repository refuses that shape everywhere else — the picture
quality check refuses an unpairable detector list rather than reporting fewer defects than it found
(HV-026-04), and the continuity report carries `notChecked` beside its findings.

## What this increment does

- **`degradedShots` is rebuilt for the restored prefix**, and rebuilt *exactly* rather than
  estimated. `repairLoop` marks a shot degraded when `checkContinuity(id, previous, the clip it
  kept)` does not pass, and the checkpoint retains every clip with its fingerprint — so the same
  check over the restored clips returns the same verdict. It is the same function, over the same
  inputs, and the test proves the two runs agree rather than asserting a string.
- **A checkpoint that holds more clips than the film has shots is refused** rather than indexed
  past, because the rebuild indexes `shots` by clip position and that had been an invariant nobody
  stated.
- **Review flags are emitted as they are established**, immediately before the shot's checkpoint,
  instead of after the whole film. An incarnation that dies now reports what it established.
  *Before* the checkpoint is the safe order, not the eager one: die before the flag and the shot is
  not checkpointed, so it is rendered again and flagged then; die after the flag and before the
  checkpoint and the shot is flagged twice — which the queue now answers with one entry either way.
- **The two stores of the operator's review queue agree that a shot is one item.**
  `PostgresReviewQueue.flag` has always keyed on `sha256(projectId + "\0" + shotId)` with
  `on conflict do update … resolved_at = null`; `OperatorReviewQueue.flag` pushed unconditionally.
  `resolve(shotId)` clears *one* unresolved item and does not even take a project, so it agrees with
  PostgreSQL — and a duplicate in the JSON store was an entry no `resolve` call would ever reach.
  The JSON store now replaces in place and reopens a resolved shot, exactly as PostgreSQL does.
- The post-loop drain stays, and says why: every shot drains at its own checkpoint, so it is empty
  today, and removing it would let a future branch that pushes without checkpointing lose its flags
  in silence — which is this increment's own defect one level down.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **An interrupted render and an uninterrupted one deliver the same degraded note**, asserted
   against each other rather than against a literal, with the clips proved identical first (name,
   fingerprint and SHA-256 from the job's own manifest).
2. **The incarnation that dies has already flagged what it established**, before it is resumed.
3. **The shot is queued once, not twice**, across the interruption.
4. **A film with nothing wrong with it still says so** — `degraded_shots=none`, an empty queue —
   interrupted at the same point, so the rebuild is a measurement and not a way of always answering
   "degraded".
5. **The JSON review queue keys on (project, shot)**: a second flag replaces rather than repeats,
   the same shot id in another project is another item, and a flag after a resolve reopens it.
6. Reverting the two source changes makes criteria 1–3 and 5 fail (run by hand; recorded here).

- Tests: `packages/queue/test/degraded-resume.test.ts` (1–5).

## Known gaps this leaves

- **"Passed only after repairs" is not recoverable across an interruption.** `repairLoop` also
  queues a shot that needed one or two repairs and then passed, and no fingerprint distinguishes
  that clip from a first-attempt one. Going forward it is flagged by the incarnation that rendered
  it, so nothing is lost; a job that was interrupted *before* this increment and resumes after it
  will not carry those. Persisting `shotReviews` alongside `checkpointShots` is what would make it
  exact, and it needs a checkpoint payload change.
- **The clip manifest is still written before the fenced checkpoint.** A worker whose lease lapsed
  writes `clips/manifest.json` in the shared job directory before the call that would refuse it.
  I could not exhibit a clobber — the fenced cost record between the provider returning and the
  manifest write throws `LeaseError` first — so nothing is claimed here, but the ordering is
  structural and the reuse branch has a longer unfenced stretch.
- **Nothing here bounds `DurableJobStore.claimNext`**, which the same critic pass measured at
  Θ(queued × total) with no job ever removed from the JSON store: 1,241 ms per claim at 8,000
  queued jobs, and a single 3-shot film issues about 30 store transactions, each a full reload and
  rewrite under the interprocess lock. That is a separate increment and a larger one.
- **Nor the fair-share defect**: a `budget_throttle` admission records `queuedBehind` as every
  active job in the studio, so one project's film blocks an unrelated project's, before
  `fairShareOrder` is consulted at all. Also separate, and it touches admission rather than the
  worker.
