# HV-030-12 — The crew was paid for a plan the direction then refused

epic: HV-030
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

`validateCrewPlan` gates the model's plan before the studio uses it. It gated every free-text shot
field at one number:

```ts
const LIMIT = {…, intent: 300, timeOfDay: 80};
keyLight: gated(shot.keyLight ?? "", LIMIT.intent, "light"), …
transitionIntent: gated(shot.transitionIntent ?? "", LIMIT.intent, "transition")
```

`crewChanges` then runs the same values through `directionSettings`, whose own table allows
`keyLight` and `transitionIntent` **240**. So a plan whose light or transition ran 241–300
characters:

1. passed `validateCrewPlan` and came back as `source: "anthropic"`;
2. had already been recorded against the crew's budget by `runPlan`;
3. threw `keyLight must be text of at most 240 characters.` from `crewChanges`, which the plan route
   does not catch.

The creator got a 400 at **Plan the film** about a field they never saw, and each retry paid for
another model call. docs/CREW.md says *"Anything else falls back to the stand-in plan"* — it did not,
because the refusal came after the gate instead of at it. Reproduced with a 287-character key light.

## What this increment does

- **`DIRECTION_TEXT_LIMITS` is exported** from `direction.ts` — the table `directionSettings` already
  enforced, now named — and the crew gates each shot field against it. `performance` and
  `soundIntent` move from 300 to the direction's 600 and 400: the crew was stricter than the
  validator there, which was harmless but was the same second description.
- An over-long field is refused inside `validateCrewPlan`, where `runPlan` already falls back to the
  stand-in plan; the model call is paid once and the step completes.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Every shot field the crew writes is gated at the direction's own limit**: at the limit the
   model's plan is used and `crewChanges` accepts it; one character over, `validateCrewPlan` refuses.
2. **A paid plan with an over-long light falls back to the stand-in plan** with
   `fallbackReason: "model_unusable"`, the call's cost recorded once, and a stand-in plan the
   direction accepts.
3. Reverting `production-plan.ts` fails both.

- Tests: `packages/planner/test/crew-plan-limits.test.ts`.

## Known gaps this leaves

- **One over-long field throws away the whole plan.** Falling back is what the docs promise, but a
  plan with one 241-character light and forty good shots becomes the stand-in plan. Truncating or
  dropping just that field would keep the rest; it would also be the studio editing the model's words
  without saying so.
- **The character fields still carry their own limits.** They are all stricter than or equal to
  `characterRecord`'s, so nothing is refused late today, but it is the same shape with no shared table.
- **The prompt does not tell the model the limits.** It is told what to write, not how long it may be,
  so it will keep producing plans that fall back.
