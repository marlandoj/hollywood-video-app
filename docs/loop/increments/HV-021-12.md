# HV-021-12 — The cast wears what the script says, in every shot

epic: HV-021
release: after Release 3 (a follow-up from its review; no release criterion)
gate: none new (Kevin's "Start wave 2", 2026-11-10: fix the wardrobe continuity from The Tide Clock's review)
spend_usd: 0

## Why

"The Tide Clock" was approved with seven timecoded comments. Two were wardrobe:

- sequence 1, 00:00:16: "Character has different colored pants" (scene 1, WREN);
- sequence 5, 00:08:54: "Actor has a hat in one scene and no hat in the next" (scene 15 into 16: WREN, DEV and MARISOL).

The run's crew plan was the stand-in (`crew.plan: "stand-in"`, `model_unusable`), and the stand-in gave every
character the same wardrobe, "Everyday clothes that suit the scene." That is the line fal received for every
shot (`packages/queue/test/fixtures/fal-received-shot9.txt`). It names no clothes and tells each shot to dress
the character for the scene, so each shot did.

The script had dressed them. WREN is "in a mustard scarf and a leather apron", DEV "in a blue knitted cap" and
HOLLIS "in a grey suit with a chain of office". Those words reached the prompt only inside the appearance's
quoted introduction, beside a wardrobe line that said otherwise. MARISOL's "She wears a yellow oilskin and a
captain's cap." is the sentence after her introduction, and it reached no prompt at all.

The continuity report then said "Nothing in this film's declared look contradicts itself." and raised no
wardrobe finding (its 52 findings were `handoff-absent` and `identity-unanchored`), because the placeholder
counted as a stated wardrobe.

Reproduced at $0 with the rehearsal's own setup (`packages/api/test/feature-prompt-limits.test.ts`'s chain on
the mock): every character's wardrobe was the placeholder, and the prompt fit never cut a wardrobe (22 shots
were fitted, by their style and direction lines only), so the fit is not the cause.

## What this increment does

- **The stand-in reads the costume the script states** (`introductionWardrobe`,
  `packages/planner/src/crew/introductions.ts`), and invents none. Its answer goes into every shot, so a wrong
  costume is worse than none, and when in doubt it reads nothing:
  - only the sentence that introduces the name, and only when the name is its subject (in its first few
    words, and not "Nora's brother");
  - in it, the comma-set parts after the name: "in ...", "wearing ..." or "dressed in ..."; the character's
    own description with a costume ("an apprentice clockmaker in a mustard scarf and a leather apron"); or
    "wears ..." right after the name or its description. A full name in capitals, "WREN ALDER", and an age
    in brackets are part of the name;
  - the walk stops at the first part that names another character or person ("watches TOM", "helps a
    child", "who wears"), so nobody else's clothes are read as theirs;
  - the next sentence counts only when it opens with the name, or with the pronoun the introduction gives
    them (none when their sex is unknown), goes straight to "wears", and neither sentence names anyone else.
  A costume is a list of items ("a coat, a hat and boots"), each ending in a garment from a fixed table
  (`GARMENTS`). It ends at the first item that isn't one, so "a red coat and stirs the pot" is "a red coat",
  and "a hat shop", "the pockets of her coat", "a nurse in a hurry" and "wears a smile" are not costumes. It
  passes the same gates as the appearance.
- **No costume stated is no wardrobe.** The placeholder is gone. The character's wardrobe is empty, the
  report says `wardrobe-unstated`, and the creator states it at the Director's desk before the look.
- **The old placeholder reads as no wardrobe** (`PLACEHOLDER_WARDROBE`, `packages/planner/src/continuity.ts`).
  A film already cast with it, The Tide Clock included, gets a `wardrobe-unstated` finding that names the
  placeholder, and it is not counted as a wardrobe comparison.

For The Tide Clock the stand-in now gives WREN "A mustard scarf and a leather apron.", DEV "A blue knitted
cap.", MARISOL "A yellow oilskin and a captain's cap." and HOLLIS "A grey suit with a chain of office.".
OSWIN's script states no costume, so his is unstated and the desk says so.

## What it does not do

- It reads no pictures, so it can't show the drift is gone. The script never says what WREN wears on their
  legs, so the trousers are still the creator's to state. Seeing the change on screen needs a paid re-render
  of the shots the comments name, which is Kevin's call.
- It doesn't change a cast record already saved. The Tide Clock's cast keeps its placeholder until it is
  edited, and the desk now flags it. Until then its renders and wardrobe sheets still carry the placeholder
  line, because dropping it from saved prompts would change what earlier approvals were made on.
- It misses some costumes on purpose, which then read as unstated: a costume before the name ("Wearing a
  red coat, NORA enters"), a costume in a sentence that also names someone else, and "she wears" after an
  introduction that doesn't say who "she" is.
- A live crew model writes its own wardrobe. This changes only the stand-in.

   test now checks that some go over once the reference note is counted, which is what the fit handles. All
