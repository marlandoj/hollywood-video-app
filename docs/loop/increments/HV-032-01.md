# HV-032-01 — Give the queue a dead-letter terminus, and bound the two lists that had none

epic: HV-032
spend_usd: 0

## Goal

Stop a job that is going nowhere from occupying a project's only concurrency slot for ever, and put a bound on the notification list inside a stored job body.

**The defect.** `requeueExpired` returned every lapsed-lease job to `queued`, incremented `resumedCount`, appended a notification, and bounded none of it. The retry budget beside it — `retriesUsed` against `retryPolicy.maxRetries` — is consumed **only** by `fail()`, which needs a worker alive enough to report a failure. A job whose worker is OOM-killed, whose host is lost, or which crashes the process reports nothing at all: it stays `running` with a lapsed lease, is recovered, is claimed again, dies again. `resumedCount` was incremented and compared against nothing.

`TIERS.free.maxConcurrent` is **1**. So the consequence is not untidiness: a single stuck job holds a free-tier project's only slot, and every honest job behind it waits for the life of the deployment. No dead-letter state existed anywhere in the codebase, though FULL-SCOPE §P15 names one.

**How this increment was found.** The program-wide re-verification of the pre-loop credit ran one auditor per epic across all 22 epics that had never had the pass `docs/loop/STATUS.md` says the first planner is supposed to run. HV-032 came back `partially-delivered`, with a correction to the board in the *understating* direction — the three-slot fleet, leases with version fences, enqueue-time idempotency and abandoned-lease recovery are all real and tested — and with this as its recommended first increment: *"bound `requeueExpired` so a job whose lease has lapsed more than N times stops at a terminal `failed` state with an explicit dead-letter reason instead of being re-claimed for ever, and bound the `notifications` array the way `recordRouteDecision` already bounds `routeDecisions`."* Verified against the source before any code was written.

## The shape of the repair

**A lapsed lease does not prove a worker died, and an earlier draft of this increment claimed it did.** `leaseExpired` reads a status and a timestamp. It cannot distinguish a dead worker from a live one whose heartbeat was late: the worker heartbeats on a five-second timer whose failures are logged and swallowed, against a five-minute default lease, so a database stall, a blocked event loop or a partition longer than the lease all look identical from here. A budget that counted *lapses* would therefore have stopped healthy work on a flaky host — and worse, a long assembly or current-film job that checkpoints forward across six forced terminations would have been killed on the sixth while minutes from done, which is exactly the shape `docs/WORKER-FLEET.md` describes when it says forced termination relies on lease expiry.

So the budget counts **lapses without progress**. Each lapse fingerprints the job's progress — `checkpointFrame`, `checkpointShots`, accrued cost and every per-kind checkpoint and output field — and compares it with the fingerprint recorded at the previous lapse. Any forward progress resets the streak to one. What remains, and is stated rather than claimed away: a job that makes no checkpoint between lapses — a single long provider call, or a short job with no checkpoint at all by design — can still reach the terminus on a flaky host with no worker having died.

**Two budgets, kept separate on purpose.** `MAX_LEASE_RECOVERIES = 5` counts stalled lapses. `retryPolicy.maxRetries` counts failures a worker *reported*. Conflating them would mean a job admitted with `maxRetries: 0` — every audio take and every retained audition, by construction — could not survive a single host restart, which perturbation P3 demonstrates by taking three tests down.

**The terminus is a real terminal state, not a skip.** A job past the budget becomes `failed` with `failureKind: "dead_letter"` — an additive second member of a closed set whose only prior value was `"policy_refusal"`, and which nothing outside `packages/queue` enumerates — an explicit `failureReason` naming the count, `completedAt` set, `nextEligibleAt` cleared, and no lease or claim. `TERMINAL` already contains `failed`, so `eligibleToStart` refuses it, `requeueExpired` no longer sees it, and `complete()` cannot be called on it. Skipping the job instead would have left it `running` for ever and *still* counted against its project's concurrency.

**`recoverAbandoned` now returns what it touched, not what it resumed.** The PostgreSQL store persists whatever that call returns, so a dead-lettered job that was not returned would have been computed and thrown away, leaving the row `running` in the database and the terminus purely local. The return value is every touched job and the caller reads the status: `job.dead_lettered` or `job.resumed` on the outbox. Nothing drains either event yet, which is also true of the `job.resumed` it mirrors.

**One notify, eight writers.** `notifications` lives inside the stored job body and had eight append sites across two packages, none bounded, while `routeDecisions` beside it has been bounded at 8192 since it was written. There is now one `notify(job, message)` that appends and trims to `MAX_JOB_NOTIFICATIONS = 256`. The oldest entries are dropped rather than the write refused, because these are messages to a person and not evidence — `routeDecisions` throws because it *is* evidence, and that asymmetry is deliberate.

**The eighth writer arrived while this increment was open, and the guard caught it on the merge.** This branch was cut at `facd660`, where the list had seven writers — six in `packages/queue` and one in `packages/storage/src/ledger.ts`. `HV-031-01` then merged to `main` and added an eighth, `DurableJobStore.revokeProject`, which appends the takedown notice directly. The branch was green locally and the guard passed, because the guard reads the working tree and the eighth writer was not in it; CI builds the *merge* of branch and `main`, and there `no source file outside the queue appends to a job's notifications` failed with `Expected: 2 / Received: 3` on run `35212175751`. The branch was rebased onto `ac0de18` and that call converted to `notify`. This is the intended behaviour of a source-level scan rather than a hole in it: a per-call-site test would have passed on the merge too, because `revokeProject`'s own suite asserts the notice is delivered and says nothing about the bound. The residual limit is stated plainly — the guard runs on whatever tree it is given, so a writer added to `main` after a branch's last CI run is caught by that branch's next CI run and not before.

**One of the writers was a second copy of a whole rule.** `packages/storage/src/ledger.ts` cancels a job for exceeding its cost cap inside its own transaction, and spelled the user-facing sentence out a second time — identical text, in a second file, with nothing holding them equal. `costCapCancelNotice()` is now declared once beside `notify`, and a test scans `packages/*/src/**/*.ts` for the sentence and requires exactly one file to contain it. This is the same shape the last five increments in this program each found somewhere else; see `HV-019-03`, `HV-024-01`, `HV-025-01`, `HV-029-01` and `HV-039-01` for the others, which is a comparison a reader can check rather than a running total they cannot.

## Declared changes beyond the defect

- **Two new optional `Job` fields**, `lapsesWithoutProgress?: number` and `lapseProgressMark?: string`, both in `AutoFields` so no caller can supply them. Optional on purpose: every body written before them lacks them, a required field would have forced either a migration or a type change on every fixture in the repository, and each read goes through `?? 0`. No migration: both live inside the existing `body` jsonb.
- **`resumedCount` no longer increments on the terminal pass.** It is served to the project owner — `publicJob` in `packages/api/src/server.ts` spreads the whole job minus a fixed private set, so `resumedCount`, `failureKind`, `failureReason` and the notification list all reach `/api/jobs/:jobId` and five other views, and `packages/api/src/graphic-api.ts` names it explicitly. A dead-lettered job therefore reports the number of resumes that actually happened, and the streak lives in `lapsesWithoutProgress` beside it. An earlier draft of this doc claimed `resumedCount` was read by exactly one place; it is read by seven.
- **`reload()` now defaults `notifications` to `[]`.** It previously did not, so a legacy body missing the field would have thrown inside `notify` — the increment centralises the append, and centralising a crash without hardening it would have been worse than leaving it spread out.
- **The bound is applied at write time.** A job that is never written again keeps whatever list it has, and the public view still serves it. Trimming every stored body would be a migration.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **A job that makes no progress stops at a dead letter.** Five stalled lapses return it to `queued` with `resumedCount` incrementing, a notification each time, no `failureKind`, and `retriesUsed` untouched. The sixth makes it `failed` with `failureKind: "dead_letter"`, the reason naming the count, `completedAt` set, and `nextEligibleAt`, `claimedBy` and `leaseExpiresAt` all null, with `resumedCount` stopped at five and `lapsesWithoutProgress` at six. A later `claimNext` returns nothing and a later `recoverAbandoned` returns nothing. Satisfies FULL-SCOPE §P15's dead-letter clause and ADR-0020 fail-closed.

2. **A stuck job holds the project's only slot until the terminus releases it.** While it holds a live lease the project's other queued job is refused the slot — asserted directly, not inferred from claim ordering — and that refusal repeats after every re-claim until the terminus, after which the waiting job runs. Which of the two the fair-share order picks first is a property of a deterministic sort over their identities and is read rather than asserted.

3. **A job that makes progress between lapses is never dead-lettered.** Ten lapses — twice the budget — each with one checkpoint in between, leave the job `queued` with its streak at one and its resume count at ten; the streak then fires once progress stops.

4. **The two budgets are independent.** A job admitted with `maxRetries: 0` still receives the full recovery budget, and a *reported* failure consumes `retriesUsed` while leaving `resumedCount`, `lapsesWithoutProgress` and `failureKind` alone.

5. **The notification list is bounded, in one place, for every writer.** A job carrying 300 notifications is trimmed to 256 by its next write, keeping the newest and dropping the oldest contiguously; and a scan of every `packages/*/src/**/*.ts` finds a direct write to `.notifications` — `push`, `unshift`, `splice`, `concat`, `fill`, `copyWithin` or an index assignment — in exactly one file, on lines inside `notify` itself.

6. **The cost-cap cancellation notice is declared once.** A scan finds the sentence in exactly one source file, and `packages/storage/src/ledger.ts` reaches it through the module and no longer appends to `notifications` directly.

7. **On PostgreSQL the row stops, and the outbox says why.** Driven through six real recovery passes against a database, the row reaches `status = 'failed'` with `failureKind: "dead_letter"` in its body, no claim and no lease; a second job on its own first lapse in the same call is resumed; and the outbox carries exactly one `job.dead_lettered` for the first, five `job.resumed` for its earlier passes, and one `job.resumed` for the second. The dead letter is not re-offered by `claimNext`.

8. **Nothing frozen is touched, and no new dependency, vendor, key, route, account, tier, payment path, budget default or migration appears.** `docs/loop/STATUS.md` and `docs/PROGRAM-EXECUTION.md` are not in the diff; RECORD owns both.

## Demonstrated, not asserted

Ten perturbations, each applied on this branch, measured against the final test files, and reverted. The PostgreSQL rows ran against a throwaway PostgreSQL 16 cluster created for this increment — never the staging database.

| perturbation | result |
|---|---|
| P1 — unbounded requeue, exactly as it shipped | `4 pass / 3 fail` |
| P2 — the terminus sets `failureKind` but not `failed`, so it skips rather than terminates | `6 pass / 1 fail` |
| P3 — the budget is taken from `retryPolicy.maxRetries` | `4 pass / 3 fail` |
| P4 — the budget counts lapses rather than *stalled* lapses | `6 pass / 1 fail` |
| P5 — `notify` appends without trimming | `5 pass / 2 fail` |
| P6 — the PostgreSQL ledger's line reverted exactly as it shipped: a direct append of the spelled-out notice | `5 pass / 2 fail` |
| P7 — `resumedCount` is incremented on the terminal pass too | `6 pass / 1 fail` |
| P8 — the PostgreSQL recovery event is always `job.resumed` | `0 pass / 1 fail` |
| P9 — the PostgreSQL recovery event arms are swapped | `0 pass / 1 fail` |
| P10 — `requeueExpired` returns only resumed jobs, so nothing persists the dead letter | `0 pass / 1 fail` |
| P11 — `revokeProject` appends the takedown notice directly, exactly as `HV-031-01` shipped it | `6 pass / 1 fail` |
| P12 — the ledger keeps `notify` but spells the sentence out inline, so only the duplication returns | `6 pass / 1 fail` |

P6 and P12 are the same line perturbed two ways on purpose: P6 reverts it whole and takes both guards down, P12 reverts only the sentence and isolates the declared-once guard from the bound guard. Measuring only P6 would have left it unclear which of the two failures the sentence scan was responsible for.

P4 is the correction an earlier draft needed: it *passed* that draft, because the draft counted lapses. P8, P9 and P10 exist because the one changed line outside `packages/queue` had no guard at all — the in-memory suite stays green if the outbox event is rewritten to lie.

## What this increment does **not** do

- **It does not fix HV-032's other MAJOR: fair-share weight never decays.** `packages/operator/src/index.ts:89` weights a project by lifetime-cumulative GPU seconds, so an established project is starved indefinitely — the exact opposite of AC-025. That needs a decay window and a decision about its length, and it is HV-032-02.
- **It does not give HV-032 the gap register its two sibling epics have.** HV-038 and HV-040 both wrote down what their pillar does not cover and pointed the remainder at HV-032; HV-032 has never written its own.
- **Nothing drains the outbox.** `job.dead_lettered` joins `job.resumed`, `job.claimed`, `job.checkpoint` and the rest, and the only consumer of `hv_outbox` anywhere is the retention sweep's `storage.project.delete`. An operator cannot yet see a dead letter without reading the database.
- **It delivers no kill switch, no operator lever, no GPU worker, no real job classes, no webhook or polling reconciliation, no network- or session-level fairness, no graded reduced-capacity mode and no public capacity page.** P15 remains largely absent under this epic's own name, and the three clauses that *are* substantially delivered are delivered by HV-038 and HV-040.
- **It does not make `hv_workers.classes` mean anything.** Three advertised job classes are honoured by no worker and consumed by no reader while thirteen job stages are actually claimed.
- **It chooses 5 rather than deriving the budget from anything.** There is no operational evidence in this repository about how often a worker dies, so 5 is a judgement, stated as one, and exported so a later increment with evidence can move it in one place.
