# HV-016-06 — An all-caps action line was a character with nothing to say

epic: HV-016
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

The character-cue test was the shape of the line and nothing else:

```ts
if (CHARACTER.test(t) && t.length <= 40 && !SCENE_HEADING.test(t))
```

`CHARACTER` is `/^[A-Z][A-Z0-9 '().-]*$/`, so `SHE SLAMS THE DOOR.` passed it. Measured on this
repo's own parser:

```
INT. BAR - DAY / SHE SLAMS THE DOOR. / He flinches.

action:    ["He flinches."]
dialogue:  [{character: "SHE SLAMS THE DOOR.", lines: []}]
beats:     ["dialogue", "action"]
warnings 0, unparseable 0
```

Two things at once, both silent. **The writer's action line left the film** — it is in neither
`action` nor the beats, so it never reaches a prompt, is never rendered and is never in the picture.
And **a speaker who does not exist entered it**. Nothing in `warnings`, nothing in `unparseable`,
which is this package's own rule: *refuse rather than silently drop*, the same rule HV-016-02 and
HV-016-05 were about.

The phantom speaker is not confined to the parse. `coverageReport` takes a scene's speakers straight
from `shot.dialogue` (`coverage.ts:49-55`) and raises a finding naming each one:

```
No single or over-shoulder coverage is declared for MAYA. Review whether this scene needs it.
No single or over-shoulder coverage is declared for THE DOOR SLAMS BEHIND HIM.. Review whether this scene needs it.
```

That same `character` field is what casting reads (`casting.ts:200`, `casting.ts:246`), what the
character sheets are built from, and what the voice paths match a performance against. A writer who
writes a sound cue in capitals — which is how a sound cue is written — gets it deleted from their
film and gets a coverage warning about a door.

This was recorded as an unbuilt finding at the end of HV-016-05, alongside the parenthetical
three-way conflict, which remains a decision for Kevin and is not this.

## What this increment does

Fountain's rule is one sentence and the parser had half of it: **a Character is a line in uppercase
with an empty line *before* it and no empty line *after* it.** Both halves are enforced now.

- **`speechAfter`** — a cue must be followed by a line carrying text. That is what a lone all-caps
  action line fails.
- **`openBefore`** — a cue must not sit inside a run of action. That is what an all-caps line in the
  middle of action fails, and it is why `He waits. / SHE SLAMS THE DOOR. / He flinches.` is now
  three action lines rather than one action line, a cue, and its speech.
- **A scene heading counts as the boundary a blank line is.** The parse loop already treats a
  heading that way — it clears `pendingCharacter` on one — and this repo's own fixtures
  (`living-script-document.test.ts`) write a cue directly under a heading. `isHeading` is now one
  function used by the loop and by the rule, so the two cannot come to disagree about what a heading
  is.
- **Both are decided in two passes over the lines**, not a search per capitalised line. HV-016-04
  made this parser linear in the document and proved it; asking "is there a blank line after me" by
  scanning forward would be quadratic on a document of capitalised lines, which is exactly the
  document an attacker sends.
- **The passes read the lines the loop acts on.** A line that was nothing but a note is skipped by
  the loop (HV-016-05) and is skipped here, so a note between a cue and its speech is still not the
  blank line that separates them.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **An all-caps action line stays in the film**: in `action`, as an action beat, with no dialogue
   and nothing in `warnings` or `unparseable` — alone, after a real speech, and at the end of a
   scene where nothing follows it at all.
2. **An all-caps line inside a run of action is action**, and the line under it is not its speech.
3. **A real cue is still a cue**, in every form the parser already accepted: under a blank line,
   directly under a heading, directly under a forced heading, with a parenthetical extension, with a
   speech of several lines, and two speakers in a row.
4. **A note or a boneyard between a cue and its speech still does not separate them**, and a
   genuinely blank line after a caps line still makes it action — HV-016-05's behaviour, which the
   new rule is exactly the change that could have undone.
5. **The studio no longer asks for coverage of a door slamming**: `coverageReport` over the parsed
   scene returns the one real speaker and no other.
6. **The parse is still linear in the document** — eight times the document, at most twenty-four
   times the work, over three shapes built from capitalised lines.
7. Reverting `index.ts` fails criteria 1, 2, 4 and 5 (measured: 4 of the 6 tests fail).

- Test: `packages/parser/test/character-cue-rule.test.ts`.

## Known gaps this leaves

- **A transition line is not a boundary.** `CUT TO:` followed directly by an all-caps line, with no
  blank between, still reads as a cue. The loop does not clear `pendingCharacter` on a transition
  either, so making a transition a boundary is a change to the loop as well as to this rule, and it
  is not made on the way past.
- **A cue is still whatever is capitalised.** `THE DOOR SLAMS.` followed by a line of text is still
  a character named `THE DOOR SLAMS.`, because a cue followed by a speech is what a cue looks like.
  Fountain's own answer to this is the forced-cue marker `@`, which this parser does not read; a
  writer who wants a capitalised sound cue immediately above a line of action has no way to say so.
- **The parenthetical is still unresolved.** `(whispering)` inside a speech is still pushed into
  `d.lines`, spoken by the voice vendor and burned into the caption, while the Final Draft importer
  refuses the same construct by name. Three consumers disagree about it and cannot all be satisfied;
  it is decision #7 in the handoff and is not touched here.
- **This is Fountain.** The Final Draft importer has its own reader and its own cue rule, and was
  not touched.
