# HV-024-11 — The ElevenLabs Music contract and adapter, admitted against the $10 line

epic: HV-024
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: none new (G3, approved by Kevin 2026-09-30 and recorded as G15 in docs/loop/HUMAN-GATES.md: ElevenLabs Music on the existing ElevenLabs account, its own $10 line with alerts at $3 and $7)
spend_usd: 0

## Why

HV-024-10 gave generated music its own line: $10, with alerts at $3 and $7, and a cue's hold at
$0.15 a minute. It said so itself: nothing called the line, and generated music had no admission
path. The Composer still wrote only its own deterministic score.

This increment builds what the line was waiting for: the contract, the adapter, and the admission.
It makes no live call. The vendor stays off unless the operator turns it on.

## What this increment does

- **The contract** (`packages/generator/src/music-provider.ts`):
  - A cue request is a prompt, a length of 10–300 whole seconds, and a seed. It is always
    instrumental: the studio never asks a vendor to sing creator text.
  - A delivery is never trusted as it arrives. It is probed with ffprobe and must be one audio
    stream, in the provider's declared format, within 2 s of the length asked for. It is then decoded
    once to 48 kHz stereo 16-bit WAV by a fixed recipe, and the copy is probed again.
  - **The cost is the hold**: the length asked for at the declared rate (`musicCueCostUsd`). The
    vendor bills `music_length_ms`, so a cue that came back short costs no less, and nothing costs
    more. A vendor's own figure is not an input.
  - Every failure says whether the vendor may have been asked (`MusicProviderError.dispatched`).
- **The ElevenLabs Music adapter** (`packages/generator/src/elevenlabs-music.ts`):
  - One `POST /v1/music?output_format=mp3_44100_128` with `prompt`, `music_length_ms`,
    `model_id: "music_v1"` and `force_instrumental: true`, and the existing `HV_ELEVENLABS_API_KEY`
    in its own header.
  - No retry. The remote body is never repeated into an error. The seed is not sent.
  - **Live only when the operator names it:** `HV_MUSIC_PROVIDER=elevenlabs` and the key. The key
    alone is not enough: it is already on the staging host, for voices. With nothing set there is no
    music provider. Any other value stops the API at startup.
- **The mock music adapter** (`MockMusicProvider`) makes the same cue from the same request, and
  answers through the same checks. Tests hand it to the studio; nothing in the environment selects it.
- **The music line's ledger** (`packages/operator/src/music-ledger.ts`, and
  `packages/storage/src/music-ledger.ts` with the additive migration `0020_music_line`):
  - **The line is lifetime, as the voice line and the crew's line are.** A cue counts its hold until
    it ends, then its recorded cost. A cue never sent counts nothing. One that may have been sent is
    recorded at its hold, unreconciled.
  - **The hold is also a generation hold.** It is checked against the line, the film's limit and the
    month's generation cap in one critical section, each refused in its house wording:
    - the JSON store: the music ledger's file lock, around the cost ledger's own;
    - PostgreSQL: one transaction under the cost ledger's budget-row lock. The hold is an
      `hv_reservations` row with stage `music-cue` and the film and vendor on it.
  - The cost becomes an ordinary cost event, so the film's spend, the month-to-date rollup and the
    diagnostics count music.
    - In PostgreSQL the API cannot write cost events or end a hold (HV-040-03, unchanged). The
      worker's reconcile posts an ended cue's cost and ends its hold (`postMusicCuesWithin`). Until
      then the hold, never less than the cost, still counts.
    - Neither store's reconcile sweeps a cue's hold as a finished job's.
  - The $3 and $7 alerts are decided from the same figures, raised **once each, ever**, by the cue
    that crosses them, and handed to `onAlert` only after the hold is committed.
  - The API reads every film's cues and writes only its own. It cannot delete one. The migration
    changes no privilege on any other table.
- **The studio** (`packages/api/src/music-cues.ts`, `POST /api/projects/<id>/music-cues`):
  1. The prompt meets the safety gate. A refusal is 422, and nothing is stored or reserved.
  2. Rights, library size and the import slot are checked.
  3. The hold is reserved.
  4. The vendor is asked once.
  5. The cue goes into the sound library with a rights record naming who made it.
  6. It is settled at its hold.

  A retried request key returns the same cue. If that cue is still being made, the answer is a plain
  409. A cue still held more than ten minutes after it was reserved (`MUSIC_CUE_STALE_MS`; the live
  adapter gives up at four) is recorded as unreconciled when retried, and the retry is told so. Neither
  answer is tagged as a budget refusal. The studio logs `music.budget_alert`. `GET /sounds` says
  honestly whether there is a music vendor.
- **The Composer** (`packages/frontend/src/studio.js`) asks for one cue, keyed by the cut, only when
  the studio says it has a music vendor. Anything that stops the cue keeps the Composer's own score,
  with a note saying why. A generated cue is credited as **Music**, not as an original score.
- **Docs:** `docs/SOUND-SESSIONS.md` (generated music cues and the line), `docs/CREW.md`.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **The adapter's request and delivery.**
   - It sends one request of the contract's shape: endpoint, key header, body, and no seed.
   - It returns a probed 48 kHz stereo WAV and the provider's request id.
2. **Every failure is honest.**
   - Six kinds of failure after the request are each marked as possibly charged: a refusal, a rate
     limit, the wrong type, a body that isn't audio, a cue too long and a cue too short. None
     repeats the body or the key.
   - A request outside the contract is refused before anything is sent.
3. **The cost.** It is the length asked for at $0.15 a minute, rounded up to the cent: the hold, and
   never above it.
4. **The mock.** It is deterministic, and it is checked by the contract's own door.
5. **Live music is unreachable unless named.**
   - With no setting, the key alone, or `none`, there is no provider.
   - A nonsense value, or `elevenlabs` without the key, fails at startup.
6. **The line, in the file store.**
   - It admits up to $10 and refuses past it, in the house wording, writing nothing in either ledger.
   - Each ended, possibly-charged cue becomes exactly one `music-cue` cost event, and its hold goes.
7. **The line is lifetime.**
   - $9.50 unreconciled in September still counts on 1 October and a year later.
   - $3 and $7 are raised once each, ever, by the cue that crosses them, after the hold is written.
   - A replay, a refusal, or crossing again after a release (a year on) raises nothing.
8. **Generation spend.**
   - A cue is refused by the film's limit and by the month's cap, writing nothing.
   - The film's spend and the month's rollup show it once it ends.
   - The worker's reconcile never sweeps a cue's hold.
9. **Concurrency.**
   - Ten separate processes reserving $1.50 at once against the same files: exactly six are admitted,
     each alert is raised once, and the cost ledger holds $9.
   - Twelve concurrent reservations over separate PostgreSQL connections: exactly eight $1.25 cues
     are admitted, each alert is raised once, and `hv_reservations` holds $10.
10. **PostgreSQL answers what the file store answers**, step for step, including a year later.
    - After the worker posts, it holds the same `music-cue` costs and holds as the file store.
    - The film's limit and the month's cap refuse there too.
    - Posting is once.
    - The API role reads every film's cues and cannot write or delete another's.
    - The migration names no other table.
11. **The studio route.**
    - With no vendor it answers 409 with the honest note, and reserves nothing.
    - A prompt naming a real person or a brand is refused (422) before the ledger: nothing is
      reserved, asked or kept.
    - A cue that crosses $3 is kept in the library and settled at $0.15. **The `music.budget_alert`
      line is written to the log**: read, not just computed. The film's `/spend` and the month's
      rollup show the $0.15.
    - A retry is the same cue, with no second request.
    - An unsent failure releases its hold; a possibly-sent one records its cost at the hold.
    - A cue still being made is a plain 409.
    - Past the line, the film's limit or the month's cap, the refusal is 429 in that limit's house
      wording, and nothing is asked of the vendor.
12. **A stuck cue.** With an injected clock, a held cue is still "being made" a minute later. Past
    ten minutes, a retry records it as unreconciled at its hold, says so plainly, and asks nothing
    of the vendor.
13. **The Composer.**
    - With a vendor, it mixes one cue keyed `crew-music-<cut id>` and credits it as generated.
    - A refused cue keeps its own score, with a note.
    - Without a vendor, it never asks.
14. **Something reads the alerts.** Both stores raise what they decide, the studio logs
    `music.budget_alert`, and the log's closed set admits it.
15. Reverting `server.ts`, `logs.ts`, `studio.js` and `titles.js` fails criteria 11, 13 and 14.
    Reverting every source file fails all of the new tests.

## Known gaps this leaves

- **The endpoint's shape is unverified until the live proof.** The repository documents no part of
  ElevenLabs Music's API, and no live cue has been made. The request, the MP3 answer and the
  10–300 s bounds are written against the public description. The live proof increment confirms or
  changes them with the operator's evidence, inside the $10 line. The price is still HV-024-10's
  declared constant, not captured evidence.
- **Unreconciled cues have no reconciliation path.** A request that may have been charged is
  recorded at its hold until an operator settles it otherwise. There is no operator route for that
  yet.
- **A stuck cue is only noticed when it is asked for again.** Until then its hold counts against the
  line, the film and the month. That errs toward not spending.
- **In PostgreSQL, a cue's cost reaches `hv_cost_events` on the worker's next reconcile.** With no
  worker running, the hold keeps counting in its place: never less than the cost.
- **Music cues are not in state snapshots or project archives.** Their ended costs are, as ordinary
  cost events, so a restore keeps the money and forgets which cues it paid for. A held cue's
  reservation, like any other reservation, must end before a snapshot migration accepts the ledger.
- **A request key is not bound to its prompt.** A retry with the same key and a different prompt
  returns the first cue. The Composer's key is fixed by the cut and its prompt by the tone, so it
  does not do this.
- **The cue is made while the API request is open.** It holds the one sound-import slot for that
  time. A long live cue could outlast a proxy's timeout; the hold and the key make a retry safe, but
  a queued job would be better.
