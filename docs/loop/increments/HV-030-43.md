# HV-030-43 — A feature's lines are spoken on camera, on their speaker's shot

epic: HV-030
release: 3 "Features"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

Release 3's approved feature, "The Tide Clock" (`docs/evidence/release-3/release-run.json`,
`feature.review.comments`), was reviewed with:

- "Character speaking does not show them talking as i expected. Its like a voice over." (sequence 2)
- "None of the characters are actually speaking. A missed opportunity to show movie quality character
  acting. lipsync" (sequence 2)

Measured on its script (`docs/evidence/release-3/scripts/feature.fountain`), before this change:

- `featureShots` planned each scene with `sourcePlan` (`planShots`), which puts a scene's whole dialogue
  on its first shot. 27 of the 202 shots carried all 46 lines; 16 of those held two or three speakers.
- Shot prompts carried the scene action only. MARISOL's dialogue shot said "coils a rope" and nothing
  about her speaking.
- Every line sat on a scene's first shot, which the stand-in crew frames as a wide establishing shot
  (`standInPlan`), so "close-ups for dialogue" never framed a speaker.
- The final picture is silent (Kling is asked for no audio), and the rough cut's voices are laid under it
  (`final-dialogue.ts`). Nobody on screen was asked to talk, and a later lip-sync pass would have no
  speaking face to work on.

## What this increment does

**1. Each line on its speaker's shot** (`packages/planner/src/dialogue-placement.ts`, `sequences.ts`).
The Director's stand-in coverage rule for a feature. It is not a creator setting (G13).

- Each speech goes on the shot that holds the action beat it follows in the screenplay, or later when
  that shot already carries a speech. Speeches go in order on distinct shots, one speech a shot, while
  the scene has the shots.
- When a scene has more speeches than shots, one speaker's consecutive speeches share a shot. Only when
  it has fewer shots than changes of speaker do two speakers share one (balanced, in order).
- With a shot to spare, the scene's first shot (the crew's wide establishing shot) keeps no line. So each
  speech lands on a shot the stand-in crew already frames as a close-up, paced, static, with a
  performance note.
- No shot is added or removed. Shot ids, seeds, per-scene counts and therefore the sequence plan
  (`sceneShotCounts`, `stalePlanReason`) are unchanged. A speech is never split.

**2. Speaking direction in the prompt** (`dialogue-placement.ts`, `prompt-fit.ts`).

- A shot with lines opens its action with one compact sentence: `MARISOL speaks on camera: face visible,
  mouth moving, framed for dialogue.` For a shared shot: `ADA and BEN speak on camera: faces visible,
  mouths moving, framed for dialogue.` At most 80 characters on "The Tide Clock".
- The prompt fit treats it as part of the never-cut prefix after the scene heading. The style bible, the
  shot direction, the performance and cast parts and the action are all cut before it; the action
  still keeps its own first 240 characters after it.
- A shot with no lines is byte for byte as before.

**3. What a creator saved keeps working** (`placedDialogueScene`).

- A scene with accepted coverage (`sceneCuts`) keeps its cut's shots, lines and prompts exactly: the
  creator's coverage wins, and no speaking line is added to it.
- A scene whose saved shot direction was made against the old placement (an entry whose source hash is
  a shot of that scene as planned before, and not as placed now) keeps the old placement. So a feature
  planned before this change, its approved rough cuts and its finals plan exactly as they did, and
  `directShots` raises no new `DirectionConflict`. A project planned after this change directs the
  placed shots and keeps them.
- Deterministic: the same script and direction give the same plan. A sequence's rough cut and its final
  read the same `featureShots`, so they carry the same lines on the same shots, at the same paced length,
  and `final-dialogue` still reuses the rough cut's speech receipts.

A short or a reel is planned exactly as before: a scene's lines on its first shot, no speaking line.

## What it does not do

- No lip sync and no re-render of anything already made. The picture is still silent; this only gives the
  picture model a speaking face to draw and a later lip-sync pass a face to work on (docs/LIP-SYNC.md).
- No new shot. A scene with more speeches than shots still doubles speeches up; the creator can add
  coverage (`sceneCuts`) for that.
- A long single speech is never split across shots.
- A saved feature project keeps the old placement until its directions are removed or it is planned anew.
  Nothing migrates it.
- No live provider call; no change to providers, budgets or the crew's model prompt.

## Measured on "The Tide Clock"

Planned by `featureShots`, paced by the Editor on the `live-film-referenced` final pool
(`billedShotTiming`: floor 5 s, steps 3–15 s), with no locks:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| shots | 202 | 202 |
| shots carrying dialogue | 27 | 46 |
| lines | 46 | 46 |
| shots with two or more speakers | 16 | 0 |
| billed seconds (sum of paced clips) | 1,071 | 1,025 |
| read-through final estimate (no locks, profile lane) | $79.45 | $74.55 |

Before, 15 dialogue shots ran past the 5 s floor, up to 14 s, to hold their scene's speeches (187 at 5 s).
After, every shot is 5–7 s (191 at 5 s, 7 at 6 s, 4 at 7 s). Spreading the lines bills 46 s less, and no
shot is added.

## Acceptance criteria

1. On "The Tide Clock", `featureShots` gives 202 shots with the same ids, scenes, seeds and planned
   durations, 46 dialogue shots carrying one line each, no shot with two speakers, every scene's lines in
   screenplay order, and no line on a scene's first shot. The live pool's pacing bills 1,071 s before and
   1,025 s after.
2. Every dialogue shot's prompt is the old prompt with the speaking sentence after the heading; every
   other prompt is unchanged. The plan is the same on every run, and the scene shot counts are unchanged.
3. Placement follows the action a speech comes after. A one-shot scene of two speakers gets both lines and
   "ADA and BEN speak on camera". More speeches than shots keeps one speaker's consecutive speeches
   together. A short is planned as before.
4. An accepted scene cut keeps its own shots and lines with no speaking line. A direction saved against
   the old placement keeps it for its scene (and `directShots` accepts it). A direction saved against the
   new placement keeps the new one.
5. With a style bible and shot direction over the limit, the fit cuts style before direction before
   action, and the speaking sentence survives every cut.

  live run, planned as before), and the API, queue, frontend and root suites that plan a feature, read its
  sequences or its dialogue.

## Known gaps

- The stand-in crew's direction is unchanged. A shot whose speech was placed on a scene's first shot
  (a scene of one shot, or with no shot to spare) is still framed wide.
- A live crew model's plan may frame a dialogue shot otherwise. The speaking sentence still says to frame
  it for dialogue, but the model's shot size is not overridden.
- Measured on the plan and the pacing, not on a render. Whether Kling draws a speaking mouth from the
  sentence is for the next live run's review.
