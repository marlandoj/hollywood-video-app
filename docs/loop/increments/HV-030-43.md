# HV-030-43 — A feature's lines are spoken on camera, near their action and preferably on their speaker's shot

epic: HV-030
release: 3 "Features"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

Release 3's approved feature, "The Tide Clock" (`docs/evidence/release-3/release-run.json`,
`feature.review.comments`), was reviewed with:

- "Character speaking does not show them talking as i expected. Its like a voice over." (sequence 2)
- "None of the characters are actually speaking. A missed opportunity to show movie quality character
  acting. lipsync" (sequence 2)

Measured on its script (`docs/evidence/release-3/scripts/feature.fountain`), before this change:

- `featureShots` planned each scene with `sourcePlan` (`planShots`), which puts a scene's whole dialogue
  on its first shot. 27 of the 202 shots carried all 46 lines; 16 of those held two or three speakers.
- Shot prompts carried the scene action only. MARISOL's dialogue shot said "coils a rope" and nothing
  about her speaking.
- Every line sat on a scene's first shot, which the stand-in crew frames as a wide establishing shot
  (`standInPlan`), so "close-ups for dialogue" never framed a speaker.
- The final picture is silent (Kling is asked for no audio), and the rough cut's voices are laid under it
  (`final-dialogue.ts`). Nobody on screen was asked to talk, and a later lip-sync pass would have no
  speaking face to work on.

## What this increment does

**1. Each speech on a shot near its action, preferably its speaker's** (`packages/planner/src/dialogue-placement.ts`,
`sequences.ts`). The Director's stand-in coverage rule for a feature. It is not a creator setting (G13).

- The units placed are the scene's speeches, one a shot, while the scene has the shots. Otherwise each
  speaker's run of consecutive speeches is one unit. Only when the scene has fewer shots than runs do runs
  share a shot, in balanced contiguous groups.
- Units go in screenplay order on distinct shots. Each unit's window starts at its anchor (the shot holding
  the action beat it follows), moved later past the previous unit's shot and, when the scene has a shot to
  spare, past the scene's first (wide, establishing) shot. The window ends at the last shot that still leaves
  one shot for every later unit. When the anchor is past that end, the unit goes on the window's end, which
  can be *earlier* than its anchor (four actions then three speeches: the first speech lands on the second
  shot).
- In its window the unit takes the first shot whose action names its speaker: the cue's name, or the name
  without a leading title (DR. OSWIN is found as "Oswin"), matched as whole words, without case. When no
  shot in the window names the speaker, it takes the window's first shot. A speech is never split.
- No shot is added or removed. Shot ids, seeds, per-scene counts and therefore the sequence plan's shots
  (`sceneShotCounts`, `stalePlanReason`) are unchanged.
- The stand-in crew frames a scene's first shot wide and any other shot with lines as a static close-up
  (`standInPlan`), so a speech that avoids the first shot is framed for dialogue.

**2. Speaking direction in the prompt** (`dialogue-placement.ts`, `prompt-fit.ts`).

- A shot with lines opens its action with one sentence: `MARISOL speaks on camera: face visible, mouth
  moving, framed for dialogue.` For a shared shot: `ADA and BEN speak on camera: faces visible, mouths
  moving, framed for dialogue.` At most three names; past three, `ADA, BEN, CAL and others speak on
  camera: ...`. Names are compared as casting compares them (upper-cased), so `@Ada` beside `ADA` is one
  name, shown as first written. On "The Tide Clock" the longest is 74 characters.
- A speech whose speaker is not seen names nobody (`cueVoice` and `Scene.offCameraDialogue` in the parser):
  - V.O., O.S. or O.C. anywhere in the extension, always, whatever the action says. Spellings such as VO,
    V/O, O/S, OFF, OOV, OOF, voice-over, off screen and out of frame count, alone or beside (CONT'D).
  - An extension that is wholly a voice through a device or a door (on the radio, ON PHONE, on the phone,
    over the intercom, ON TV, ON SPEAKER, HEADSET, FILTERED, on the walkie-talkie, through the door), only
    when the scene's action never names the speaker (the cue's name, or the name without a title). Scripts
    also write (ON PHONE) for the character we see making the call ("Wren lifts the phone and paces."),
    and that speaker is on camera. A mention only of the speaker's voice ("Hans's voice crackles over the
    radio", "the voice of Hans") does not count as naming them.
  - A bare (CONT'D) is as the same speaker's previous speech in the scene was, so a voice-over continued
    across another speaker's line stays off camera.
  - Anything else is on camera, as a cue with no extension is: "(into phone)", "(on the phone, pacing)" and
    "(offering the radio)" included.

  The flags sit beside the scene's speeches, present only when a scene has an off-camera speech; the
  speech, its beats and their hashes are unchanged. Such a speech is still placed; a unit whose speeches are
  all off camera stays on its window's first shot.
- A speech whose lines are all parentheticals ("(laughs)") speaks no line (`lineSources`) and names nobody.
- The prompt fit treats the sentence as part of the never-cut prefix after the scene heading. The style
  bible, the shot direction, the performance and cast parts and the action are all cut before it; the
  action still keeps its first 240 characters. With the three-name cap the sentence is at most three cue
  names plus about 90 characters, so a one-shot scene of sixty 38-character speakers still fits Kling's
  2,500 characters (`FAL_KLING_MAX_PROMPT_CHARS`). When a prompt can't be fitted, the refusal names the
  sentence it kept.
- A shot with no lines is byte for byte as before.

**3. The plan says how it placed its lines** (`sequences.ts`, the plan step in `server.ts`).

- A sequence plan and each render's sequence may carry `dialoguePlacement: "speaker"`. It is part of the
  plan's revision. Absent, a feature plans every line on its scene's first shot, exactly as before, so
  every plan, rough cut and final saved before this change validates, hashes and renders as it did. A render,
  the desk and the takes read the marker; only the plan step reads saved directions to choose it.
- The plan step chooses the marker (`nextDialoguePlacement`):
  - A feature whose current plan is already stamped stays stamped when planned again, after a script edit
    or not. Its directions were saved against the placed shots, so an edit makes stale only the shots it
    changes (adding one speech to a four-shot scene: the shot that now carries it).
  - Any other feature (a new one, a feature planned before this change, or a short or reel planned again
    as a feature) is stamped unless saved directions hold it back (`dialoguePlacementHolds`): an entry that
    is current with every line on its scene's first shot and would be stale with the lines placed. Then the
    plan keeps the old placement, and the Director's crew note says so. It names the first direction that
    held it back, and lists every direction that holds it back once the crew has directed the plan's
    shots: clearing those and planning again places the lines.
  - So a project the crew directed before this change, or one with a desk edit saved before its first
    feature plan, keeps its placement and its directions, and is told why and how to change it. A project
    with no directions, or whose directions are the same either way, is placed.
- Every route that plans a project's shots reads the plan's placement (`projectShotPlan`, `projectShots`):
  the direction desk (GET and PUT /direction, coverage review), frame anchors, subject motion and motion
  studies, take groups, adopting a take, and the crew's changes. A take group keeps the plan it read
  (`ShotTakePlan.shotPlan`: `feature` or `feature-speaker`, absent for a short, as before). So a desk or
  take edit is saved against the shot the sequence renders, and the render raises no `DirectionConflict`.
- The read-through quotes a feature as the plan step plans it: `featureShots` with the placement the plan
  would stamp (a stamped feature stays stamped), where it used to plan the whole script at 240 shots
  (`sourcePlan`).
- A living-script candidate or shot plan refuses a feature sequence's film as its source, as it refuses
  other derived films: it plans the whole screenplay at the tier, which is not how a sequence is planned.
- Adopting a take whose shot is no longer planned as it was is refused with "This take's shot is no longer
  planned as it was when the takes were made" (it blamed accepted coverage, which may not be the cause).
- Screenplay navigation for an edit source (`edit-script-source.ts`) binds each placed line to its own
  screenplay line and beats: a shot's dialogue index is offset by the speeches its scene's earlier shots
  carry. A source rendered without the stamp is indexed exactly as before.
- Deterministic: the same script, direction and stamp give the same plan, so a sequence's rough cut and its
  final, which read the same sequence and direction, carry the same lines on the same shots at the same
  paced length, and `final-dialogue` still reuses the rough cut's speech receipts.
- A scene with accepted coverage (`sceneCuts`) keeps its cut's shots, lines and prompts exactly.

A short or a reel is planned exactly as before: a scene's lines on its first shot, no speaking line.

## What it does not do

- No lip sync and no re-render of anything already made. The picture is still silent; this only gives the
  picture model a speaking face to draw and a later lip-sync pass a face to work on (docs/LIP-SYNC.md).
- No new shot. A scene with more speeches than shots still doubles speeches up; the creator can add
  coverage (`sceneCuts`) for that.
- A long single speech is never split across shots.
- A feature planned before this change keeps the old placement until it is planned again with no saved
  direction that the new placement would make stale; the Director's note says which to clear. Nothing
  migrates it. A feature once stamped stays stamped on every later plan step as a feature.
- The speaker match reads names only. A shot that calls the speaker "she" or "the captain" is not found,
  and the speech stays on its window's first shot.
- No live provider call; no change to providers, budgets or the crew's model prompt.

## Measured on "The Tide Clock"

Planned by `featureShots` without the stamp (before) and with it (after), paced by the Editor on the
`live-film-referenced` final pool (`billedShotTiming`: floor 5 s, steps 3–15 s), with no locks or cast.
Asserted in `packages/planner/test/dialogue-placement.test.ts`, "The Tide Clock":

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| shots | 202 | 202 |
| shots carrying dialogue | 27 | 46 |
| lines | 46 | 46 |
| shots with two or more speakers | 16 | 0 |
| dialogue shots whose action names a speaker of the shot | 10 | 33 |
| … names only another character | 3 | 4 |
| … names no character | 14 | 9 |
| billed seconds (sum of paced clips) | 1,071 | 1,025 |
| read-through final video estimate | $79.45 | $74.55 |

Before, 15 shots ran past the 5 s floor, up to 14 s, to hold their scene's speeches (187 at 5 s). After,
every shot is 5–7 s (191 at 5 s, 7 at 6 s, 4 at 7 s). The estimate depends on the profile: on `live-film`
(Kling 2.5) it is $74.20 before and $74.55 after; on `live-film-anchored` $89.96 and $86.10 (computed, not
asserted). Before this change the read-through planned the script at 240 shots; on this script that is the
same 202 shots, so its quote then was the "before" column.

## Acceptance criteria

1. On "The Tide Clock", `featureShots` without the stamp is the old plan; with it, 202 shots with the same
   ids, scenes, seeds and planned durations, 46 dialogue shots of one line each, no shot with two speakers,
   every scene's lines in screenplay order, no line on a scene's first shot, 33 of the 46 dialogue shots
   naming their speaker in the action. The live pool's pacing bills 1,071 s and 1,025 s; the read-through
   quotes $79.45 and $74.55.
2. Every dialogue shot's prompt is the old prompt with the speaking sentence after the heading; every other
   prompt is unchanged. The plan is the same on every run, and the scene shot counts are unchanged.
3. Placement: the speech follows its action; moves to the first shot in its window naming its speaker
   (also without a title); never past the shot a later speech needs; can move earlier than its action when
   later speeches need the later shots; exact fit fills every shot; a scene grouped past 24 beats places each
   speech on the shot holding its beat; a one-shot scene shares; consecutive speeches of one speaker stay
   together. A short is planned as before.
4. Off camera: each V.O./O.S./O.C. spelling listed in the test, (V/O), (O/S), (OOV), (OOF) and (out of frame)
   included, names nobody, even when the action names the speaker; each device or door extension listed,
   (through the door) included, names nobody unless the scene's action names the speaker, and then names
   them; each listed on-camera extension names the speaker; a bare (CONT'D) follows the same speaker's
   previous speech, a (V.O.) across another speaker's line included. The parser flags them without
   changing the speech or its beats.
   A parenthetical-only cue and a case-different name are handled as casting handles them; the cap gives
   three names and "and others".
5. Prompt fit: with a style bible and shot direction over the limit, style is cut before direction before
   action, and the sentence survives; sixty 38-character speakers fit 2,500 characters; the refusal names the
   sentence.
6. Saved records: an unstamped plan and ref validate and hash as before and plan the old placement; a
   stamped one validates and its stamp can't be added or removed; a partial legacy direction keeps the old
   placement, and its rough cut and final plan the same shots; `nextDialoguePlacement` keeps a stamped
   feature stamped, and otherwise keeps the old placement only when the new one would make a saved
   direction stale (`dialoguePlacementHolds` names those, in shot order, and the Director's note lists
   them); a take group keeps its plan and an older group validates as before.
7. Routes: a feature planned through `/crew/plan` is stamped; GET /direction lists the placed shots; a desk
   edit after the crew directs every shot, and sequence 1's render is admitted with the same placement and the edit
   applied; a take quote on a placed shot reads the placed shot; a short is unchanged; the read-through's
   quote is `readThroughFacts` over the plan step's shots. The second review's re-plan: a stamped feature,
   crew-directed, given one more speech and planned again, stays stamped, lists [[], [ADA], [BEN], []] and
   has only shot-1-3 stale. A desk edit before the first plan keeps the old placement with the Director's
   note naming shot-1-1 and listing four directions; clearing those four and planning again places the lines.
8. A living-script candidate and a living-script shot plan refuse a feature sequence's film.

## Known gaps

- The stand-in crew's direction is unchanged. A speech placed on a scene's first shot (a scene of one shot,
  or with no shot to spare) is still framed wide.
- A live crew model's plan may frame a dialogue shot otherwise. The speaking sentence still says to frame
  it for dialogue, but the model's shot size is not overridden.
- For an unstamped feature sequence, screenplay navigation still finds each shot's action beats as one
  whole-script plan at the tier's shot limit, as before this change; for a feature with a scene past 24
  beats that can differ from the shots rendered. Left as it was so existing indexes keep their revision.
- An off-camera speaker is still cast for the shot: the shot's cast direction and reference images read
  every cue name in the scene (`charactersForScene`), and the stand-in crew frames any shot with lines,
  a V.O. shot included, as a static close-up (`standInPlan`). Both predate this increment.
- `validateSequenceRef` accepts a marker on a ref without checking that its plan had one. Only an edited
  state file could make such a ref; the plan's own revision still refuses an edited plan.
- Measured on the plan and the pacing, not on a render. Whether Kling draws a speaking mouth from the
  sentence is for the next live run's review.
