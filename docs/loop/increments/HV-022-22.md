# HV-022-22 — ElevenLabs lines are read in conversation

epic: HV-022
release: 3 "Features"
gate: none new (cites G14-202609210000)
spend_usd: 0

## Why

Kevin reviewed "The Tide Clock" (`docs/evidence/release-3/release-run.json`, `feature.review.comments`)
and heard the voices as a robot:

- "Marisol audio sounds like a robot." (sequence 2, 00:02:45:22)
- "Robot voice here is not good" (sequence 1, 00:01:25:17)
- "Robot voice here again. It sounds bad and not natural" (sequence 3, 00:04:01:19)

The feature's 46 lines were recorded as ElevenLabs takes. The feature holds $0.102636 on the voice
line, which is 1,847 characters at the line's own rate (HV-022-21). Each take was built the same way:

- The request had no conversational context. `elevenLabsLineRequest` sent `text` and nothing about
  the lines around it, so every line was a cold read.
- The speaker boost was off (`use_speaker_boost: false`).
- The controls were the crew's casting defaults: stability 0.5, similarity 0.75, style 0, speed 1.1.

The endpoint already takes `previous_text` and `next_text` for this purpose, and a speaker boost. This
increment uses them, at $0 and with no live call.

## What this increment does

- **A new, additive contract.** `ELEVENLABS_CONTEXT_CAPABILITY` (`hv-audio-capability/5`,
  `packages/generator/src/elevenlabs-capability.ts`) is the HV-022-05 contract with two additions:
  `context` and `speakerBoost: true`. The vendor, model, endpoint, output, controls and their bands
  are the same. It is listed in `AUDIO_CAPABILITIES` beside `ELEVENLABS_AUDIO_CAPABILITY`, which keeps
  its revision (`21655e9e…`). The new revision (`7f7d4cae…`) is pinned in
  `docs/evidence/hv019-performance/capability-revision-pins.json`.
- **A new line plan schema.** `hv-audio-line/7` (`packages/planner/src/audio-performances.ts`) is
  `hv-audio-line/6` plus `context: {previousText?, nextText?}`. `compileAudioLine` produces it only
  when the direction carries `context`, even an empty one. Without `context` an ElevenLabs line still
  compiles to `hv-audio-line/6`, so every recorded plan re-derives exactly as before.
  `elevenLabsContext` validates each side like `spokenText`:
  - at most 1,000 characters (`ELEVENLABS_MAX_CONTEXT_CHARACTERS`);
  - valid text, trimmed and non-empty;
  - no `<` or `>`;
  - it passes the safety gate.
  Only an ElevenLabs line takes the field.
- **The request.** For a plan on the new contract, `elevenLabsLineRequest` sends `previous_text` and
  `next_text` (each only when present) and `use_speaker_boost: true`. A plan on the old contract
  builds exactly the request it was admitted with, so its dispatch receipt's `requestSha256` still
  matches. The request also refuses a plan whose schema and capability disagree. The adapter accepts
  both ElevenLabs revisions (`supportedCapabilities`).
- **The take route fills the context** (`packages/api/src/server.ts`). Every new ElevenLabs take
  compiles once to learn its spoken length, then again with `elevenLabsLineContext(...)`. That
  function (`packages/planner/src/elevenlabs-performance.ts`) takes the scene's dialogue line just
  before and just after this one, whoever speaks it, from `lineSources(scene.dialogue)`. Parentheticals
  are cues, not lines, so they are never context.
  - A neighbour is left out, never shortened, when it is longer than the limit, has tags, or fails
    the gate.
  - It is also left out when it would take the take's characters past the policy's ceiling
    (`maxCharacters`). The previous line is fitted first, then the next.
  - A narration read has no neighbours and gets `{}`.
  - So no line that was admitted before is refused for its context.
- **Admission checks the context.** `validateAudioTake` derives the context again from the job's own
  screenplay, line index, spoken length and policy, and refuses an `hv-audio-line/7` take whose
  context is anything else ("The audition's conversation context no longer matches its screenplay
  scene."). A take cannot carry arbitrary text as context.
- **Holds count the context (HV-022-21's rule, extended).** Whether ElevenLabs bills `previous_text`
  and `next_text` is not confirmed, so a take holds them as if it does.
  - `elevenLabsBilledCharacters(line)` is the spoken text plus the context, in UTF-16 units.
    `audioTakePlan` uses it both for the policy's `maxCharacters` check and for
    `elevenLabsLineHoldUsd`. That keeps HV-022-21's integer micro-dollar arithmetic, rounded up, never
    down.
  - The hold never passes the policy's price, because the route fits the context under the ceiling.
  - Every reader of a take's hold still reads `audioTakeHoldUsd`. That covers admission, the film
    limit, the vendor's line, the job's cap and reservation, the route's answer, the billing views
    and snapshots.
  - Example: "Then we wait." after "Not for an hour." holds 29 characters. That is $0.001612 on the
    operator's plan, against $0.000723 for the line alone. A typical 40-character line between two
    40-character neighbours holds 120 characters, three times what it did: $0.006667 rather than
    $0.002223. 250 such lines hold about $1.67 of the $25 line.
- **The review panel's wording** (`packages/frontend/src/audio-studio.js`) now says an ElevenLabs take
  holds "this line's own characters, and the neighbouring lines sent with it as context".
- **Saved casts get the new read without being recast.** The context and the boost come from the
  route, not the cast, so the next take of any ElevenLabs line uses them. That includes The Tide
  Clock's.

### The pace stays at 1.1 (a decision, not an omission)

The brief asked for the crew's ElevenLabs pace to go from 1.1 to 1.0. That is a real timing risk, so
the safe option is kept and `CREW_VOICE_SPEED` is unchanged for both vendors:

- **A take that does not fit is not laid in.** Dialogue replacement keeps each line's start, and a
  take must end before the next line's start (`assertAudioTimelineWindow`). If it runs longer, the
  take is not laid in, and the line keeps the temporary eSpeak voice. That voice is the robotic one.
  Nothing is cut: the take is unused, and still paid for.
- **Within a shot, the window is the temporary read.** The next line starts after the temporary
  read plus its pauses. A take's trailing pause is its own `afterMs`, and the film line's is the
  line direction's `afterMs`. Both default to 200 ms (`audio-performances.ts`, `performances.ts`),
  so with the defaults the take carries the same trailing pause, and a take fits only if its speech
  is no longer than the eSpeak read. When the two differ, the window moves by the difference. How
  long the eSpeak read is depends on the line. eSpeak runs at 175 wpm nominal. HV-030-05 measured a
  long plain sentence 25% faster than that, and a short punctuated line ("Stop, wait, listen: did you
  hear that?…") 19% slower, because each pause is close to a fixed cost. Dialogue is often short and
  punctuated, so many lines have more room than the long-sentence figure suggests, and long plain
  lines have the least. The last line of a shot has more room still, because the Editor's pacing
  (`dialogueSeconds`) pads the shot.
- **A slower read fits fewer lines.** A 1.0 read is about a tenth longer than a 1.1 read, so some
  lines that fit at 1.1 would stop fitting and fall back to the robot voice. No measurement of the
  vendor's read lengths against these windows exists to bound how many.
- **Azure keeps 1.1** for the same reason. Its windows are the same temporary reads.
- **A saved cast keeps its saved speed until it is recast.** The speed is stored in the character's
  `audioVoice` profile, and the route reads it from there. A change to `CREW_VOICE_SPEED` would only
  reach characters cast afterwards.

## What this increment does not do

- **No emotion control for ElevenLabs.** Its contract refuses one on purpose
  (`controls.emotion: false`), and the new contract keeps that. A line asking for an emotion is still
  refused. Parentheticals stay direction metadata and are never sent as context.
- **No pace change** (see above), and no retake at another pace.
- **No live call, key, vendor or account.** Every test stubs the transport. `spend_usd` is 0.
- **No change to limits.** The $25 voice line, its alerts, the film limits, the monthly cap and
  the 10,000-character ceiling are unchanged.
- **Azure and Cartesia are unchanged**, and so are old ElevenLabs plans, receipts and takes.

### Proposal, not built: parentheticals as stability and style

A screenplay cue could set the existing numbers when the creator has not directed the line. Nothing
new would reach the vendor:

- an intense cue ("(shouting)", "(furious)", "(sobbing)") lowers stability to 0.35 and raises style
  to 0.2;
- a quiet or flat cue ("(quietly)", "(flatly)", "(whispers)") raises stability to 0.65 and keeps
  style 0.

How it would work:

- It would be a fixed table in `elevenlabs-performance.ts`, applied in `compileAudioLine` before the
  creator's own direction. The plan already records the effective controls, so it would be auditable
  and deterministic.
- An unknown cue changes nothing.
- It needs a listening check before it ships. ElevenLabs' own guidance is that low stability can
  make a read erratic and that style adds latency.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **A line in conversation.** An ElevenLabs line given context compiles to `hv-audio-line/7` on
   `ELEVENLABS_CONTEXT_CAPABILITY`, re-validates, and builds a request with `previous_text`,
   `next_text` and `use_speaker_boost: true`. With empty context, the request has neither field and
   the boost is on. Emotion is still refused.
2. **Old plans are unchanged.** A line without context compiles to `hv-audio-line/6` on the original
   revision, which is still `21655e9e…`. Its request is byte-for-byte the HV-022-05 request, with the
   boost off. A plan cannot be moved between the two contracts, given context, or have its context
   changed without being refused.
3. **Context is bounded and plain.** 1,000 characters are accepted. These are refused: 1,001
   characters, tags, untrimmed or empty text, control characters, unknown fields, and context on
   another vendor's line.
4. **The route's context is the scene's neighbours.** That means the line before and after, whoever
   speaks them, never a parenthetical. A neighbour that is too long, tagged or refused, or that would
   pass the policy's ceiling, is left out rather than shortened, previous first.
5. **Holds count context.** A 13-character line with 16 characters of context holds 29 characters:
   $0.00145 at the fixture's rate, and $0.001612 on the operator's plan (1,611.1124 micro-dollars,
   rounded up). The same line on the old contract still holds $0.00065. Through the PostgreSQL take
   route, the same figure appears in the route's answer, the job's cap and reservation, and the
   `hv_reservations` row. A ceiling-length line drops its context and holds no more than the policy's
   price. Forcing context onto it is refused by the price policy.
6. **Admission refuses forged context.** These are refused: empty context where the scene has
   neighbours, other text, or a side the scene doesn't have. A take on the old contract validates as
   it always did.
7. **The adapter dispatches once.** A contextual plan is dispatched once with its neighbours and the
   boost. Its receipt pins the new revision, and its request hash validates against the plan. The
   words aligned are the line's own. An old plan is dispatched with exactly its old body.
8. The existing ElevenLabs, voice-line, vendor-budget, audio-take, retained-audition and
   performance-conformance suites pass.

## Known gaps this leaves

- **Some robot lines may not be ElevenLabs at all.** The evidence records 46 takes held. It does not
  record how many were laid into the cut. A take that didn't fit its window kept the temporary eSpeak
  voice, and that is a robot. Counting the ElevenLabs auditions against the temporary lines in each
  sequence's voiced final costs nothing. That would show whether the fix that matters most is the
  read or the windows. The latter would be Editor pacing sized for production voices, or a retake at
  a faster pace for lines that don't fit.
- **The read is not verified by ear.** Nothing here proves the contextual read sounds better. That
  takes one live take on the operator's account, which is paid and needs its own increment.
- **Billing of context is unconfirmed.** It is held as billed, so it may over-hold. Settlement
  records the invoice allocation in full either way.
- **No golden `hv-audio-line/7` record.** The conformance suite pins the new revision. It has no
  golden ElevenLabs plan for either contract, because refreshing the golden file regenerates every
  record in it.
- **The pace stays at 1.1 for now.** 1.0 needs a measurement of read lengths against windows, or a
  retake-on-misfit path.
- **Whether context is billed is not read back.** ElevenLabs documents a character-cost response
  header. The adapter reads only `request-id` (`packages/generator/src/elevenlabs-audio.ts`), so no
  receipt records what a take was charged in characters. The first live contextual take should read
  that header to confirm whether `previous_text`/`next_text` are billed. Receipts and the adapter are
  unchanged here.
- **A vendor limit on context length is unconfirmed.** The route allows 1,000 characters a side
  (`ELEVENLABS_MAX_CONTEXT_CHARACTERS`). One unsourced mention puts a limit on `previous_text` at 100
  characters. If the vendor enforces a lower limit, a long neighbour would be rejected by the vendor
  rather than left out by the route. Check it on the first live take. The adapter is unchanged here.
- **The safety gate re-reads the neighbours.** A contextual take's context is gated again whenever
  the plan is re-validated (snapshots, retained auditions, admission). A later, stricter safety rule
  would refuse an existing take for a neighbouring line, as it would for its own line. That is
  intended and recorded in `docs/AUDIO-PROVIDERS.md`.

## Review follow-ups

The workshop review approved this increment with small follow-ups, all done here:

- S1: `elevenlabs-context.test.ts` has a two-scene script. A scene's last line gets no `nextText`
  and the next scene's first line no `previousText`, through `elevenLabsLineContext` and
  `validateAudioTake`. A take that borrows the other scene's line is refused.
- S2: a narration take on `hv-audio-line/7` carries `{}` and is admitted. One with forged
  `previousText` is refused ("conversation context").
- S3: the `hv-audio-line/6` request body is pinned as a literal JSON string, key order included, with
  the plan's revision and the request's hash (the dispatch receipt's `requestSha256`). The values
  were computed on main (367827d) before this increment, and match.
- S4: the pace section above now gives both halves of HV-030-05's measurement and says when the
  trailing pauses match. The decision is unchanged.
- S5: `packages/storage/test/elevenlabs-take-holds.test.ts` posts `context` in the take request. The
  route answers 400, and no job or hold is created.
- Optional: the two vendor facts and the gate's reach are listed above as known gaps. Stale wording
  is corrected in `audio-jobs.ts`, `performance-registry.ts`, `docs/AUDIO-PROVIDERS.md` and
  `docs/PROVIDER-ROUTING.md`. The review panel no longer names neighbouring lines for a narration
  read (`reservationText(policy, narration)`).
