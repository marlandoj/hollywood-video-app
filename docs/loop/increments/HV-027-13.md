# HV-027-13 — A finished film couldn't be exported once the month was a cent past its budget

epic: HV-027
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

HV-027-11 made capacity admit a job that holds nothing, whatever the month has spent. It gave the
deliverable, sound-mix, editorial, assembly and graphic routes `requestedUsd:0`. Each of those
routes then reserves its $0 with the ledger, and the ledger still ran the month's check:

    if (this.spend(now) + held + remainingUsd > monthlyCapUsd + 1e-9) throw new BudgetError(...)

With nothing added, that check is true exactly when the month is already past the cap. That does
happen: a provider's actual cost can exceed its hold, and the operator can lower the cap.
HV-027-11's own test put the spend exactly on the budget, so it never hit this case. A cent past,
exporting a film the creator already had returned 429 "generation capacity is reserved". That is
the outcome HV-027-11 was written to prevent. The PostgreSQL ledger's `reserveWithin` ran the same
check.

## What this increment does

- **A reservation that holds nothing is no longer refused by the month's spend.** This applies in
  both `CostLedger.reserve` and `PostgresCostLedger.reserveWithin`.
- **A reservation that holds money is checked as before.**

## Acceptance criteria

1. **JSON ledger.** With the month a cent past its cap, the ledger reserves $0 for a job and still
   refuses a $0.50 hold.
2. **The deliverable route.** A deliverable of a finished film is admitted (202) when the month's
   spend is a cent past the budget.
3. **PostgreSQL ledger.** With the cap under what is already spent, a $0 reservation is made and a
   $0.50 one is refused.
4. Reverting `packages/operator/src/index.ts` and `packages/storage/src/ledger.ts` fails criteria
   1–3.

- Tests: `packages/api/test/zero-cost-over-cap.test.ts` and
  `packages/storage/test/zero-hold-over-cap.test.ts`. The second is PostgreSQL-gated and was run
  against a local PostgreSQL 16.

## Known gaps this leaves

- **The film's own limit is unaffected.** Its checks (`assertFilmBudget`, and the one inside
  `admit`) already compare against what a job would add.
