release: 3 "Features"
gate: none new (cites G20-202610031349: Release 3's build order is agreed, and this is its step 11)

## Why

Release 3's build order, step 11, "Hero-render chain": "Upscale, frame rate and denoise on a chosen
shot, each stage with its own provenance; any paid stage is declared". The parts table's
`HV-019.hero-chain` (surface `desk-api`) wants "a hero shot [to go] through the hero-render chain, and
each stage records its own provenance".

Until now nothing in the studio upscaled or interpolated anything (HV-030-27's inventory). The P5 gap
register listed the hero-render chain as absent.

## What

**1. The chain, on local ffmpeg at $0** (`packages/planner/src/hero-chain.ts`,
`packages/generator/src/hero-chain.ts`). A creator chooses one shot of a finished final render. It
goes through three stages, in this order:

1. denoise (`hqdn3d`, light, medium or strong);
2. frame-rate conversion (`minterpolate`, motion-compensated, to 24, 25, 30, 48, 50 or 60 fps);
3. upscale (`scale` with Lanczos, to 720, 1080, 1440 or 2160 lines, keeping the aspect ratio with an
   even width).

Each stage writes a new H.264 file. The defaults are medium, 60 fps and 2160 lines.

**2. Each stage's own provenance** (`hv-hero-stage/1`). A stage records:

- the digest of the file it read;
- the stage, engine, provider and spend;
- the exact filter and the whole ffmpeg command;
- the ffmpeg version and a digest of its whole banner;
- the digest of the file it wrote;
- an ffprobe reading of that file.

The chain's record (`hv-hero-chain/1`) leads back to the shot: the film, the shot id, the shot's
render-record revision, and the clip's digest and probe. It is written as `hero/provenance.json`.
Every validation re-derives it link by link.

**3. The ffprobe gate.** Each stage's file is probed, with frames counted by decoding, and checked
against what the stage was asked to make from the file it read. The next stage starts only after the
check passes.

**4. Limits.**

- Source: at most 1920×1080, 60 fps and 10 s.
- Output: at most 3840×2160 at 60 fps.
- An upscale must enlarge the shot.
- A frame-rate stage must change the rate.

The shot's duration is checked at admission. Its size and rate are checked from the file before the
first stage runs.

**5. A stage interface a paid stage must declare itself through.** An engine names its stage,
provider and whether it is paid. A paid engine is refused unless the stage declares that provider
and a spend of more than $0 and at most $50. A chain with any declared spend is still refused at
planning, because a hero render is a deliverable and is admitted only at zero cost. No paid engine
ships.

**6. A deliverable, exported, never spliced into the cut.** A hero render is a delivery job beside
the film (`DeliveryPlan = DeliveryJobPlan | HeroJobPlan`). It is bound to:

- the film's sealed output revision;
- the shot's render record;
- the clip's digest.

It reuses the delivery stage's machinery: zero-cost admission, idempotency, the permission re-read
at dispatch on both backends, the worker, the s3 checkpoint, the snapshot validation and the artifact
route. No new job stage, migration or state schema is needed.

The smaller of the two designs the task allowed was chosen. The result is exported and does not
replace the shot in any cut. That would be an editorial operation with its own review.

**7. Content credentials.** The result is signed with `exportCredentials`, the same way as every
export. With the host's key, the sidecar's `hv.provenance` assertion carries `spec: "hv-hero-chain/1"`
and a new optional `derivedFrom` block. That block names the film, the shot, the render record, the
clip's digest and the digest of the stage records.

**8. Routes** (owner-only, inside `DeliveryApi`):

- `GET /api/projects/:id/deliveries/hero/:filmJobId`: the film's shots, each available or not with
  the reason, plus the options, engines and limits.
- `POST` the same path admits a hero render.

`GET …/deliveries` lists a made hero render with each stage's record and a link to every file it
retains. The artifact route serves those files and nothing else.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **A real mock clip produces three artifacts with the correct size and frame rate.** The
   deterministic mock provider's clip (320×180, 24 fps, 1 s) goes through strong denoise, 30 fps and
   720 lines. An independent ffprobe reads the outputs as 320×180@24 (24 frames), 320×180@30 and
   1280×720@30, all h264. Through the API, a mock final render's shot reads back at the source size,
   the source size at the new rate, and the upscaled size at the new rate.
   *Test:* `packages/generator/test/hero-chain.test.ts` → "a real mock clip goes through denoise…";
   `packages/api/test/hero-chain-route.test.ts`.
2. **Each stage's provenance links input to output digests, back to the shot.**
   - Stage 1's input is the clip's sha256.
   - Each later input is the previous output.
   - Each output digest is the file's.
   - The record names the filter, the full command and the ffmpeg version the host actually runs.

   A link edited under fresh revisions is refused by name: input, source, filter, probe size or
   spend. A file changed on disk fails verification.
   *Test:* the same generator case, the route test, and `packages/storage/test/hero-chain-recovery.test.ts`.
3. **A paid stage without a declaration is refused.**
   - A test-only paid engine with no declaration is refused with "must declare its provider and its
     spend".
   - A declaration naming the wrong provider, $0 or more than $50 is refused.
   - A local engine with a declaration is refused.
   - A declared paid stage is planned and carries its provider and spend.
   - A job plan with any declared spend is refused: "admitted only at zero cost".
   - An unknown engine is refused.
   - A stored plan edited to claim spend fails re-derivation.

   *Test:* `packages/planner/test/hero-chain.test.ts` → "a paid stage that does not declare…".
4. **An unauthorised caller is refused.**
   - No token: 401 on GET and on POST.
   - Another project's token on this project's route: 401.
   - That other project naming this film: 404.
   - A rough cut instead of a final: 404.

   No job is queued.
   *Test:* `hero-chain-route.test.ts`.
5. **Limits are enforced.**
   - A shot longer than 10 s is refused at planning.
   - A source over 1920×1080, over 60 fps, over 10 s, or not the length its record says is refused.
   - An upscale that does not enlarge, or one over 3840×2160, is refused.
   - Choices outside the lists (120 fps, 4320 lines, "heavy", unknown fields) are refused, by the API
     with 400 before anything is queued.
   - A stage file of the wrong size, rate, frame count or codec is refused.

   *Test:* `packages/planner/test/hero-chain.test.ts` → "limits…"; `hero-chain-route.test.ts`.
6. **C2PA: the result's provenance leads back to the source shot and is signed like an export.** With
   a throwaway signing identity, the result's sidecar verifies as `Trusted` against its root. Its
   assertion names `hv-hero-chain/1`, the result's sha256, and a `derivedFrom` equal to the film,
   shot, render record, clip digest and stage-records digest. Without a key the record says unsigned.
   *Test:* `hero-chain.test.ts` → "with a key on the host…".
7. **It is a deliverable at zero cost, beside the film.**
   - Admission is idempotent per request key and per chain, shot and sealed film.
   - A different chain under the same key is refused.
   - The job reserves and spends $0, and `totalFrames` is the shot at the converted rate.
   - The film's output and the shot's clip are unchanged.
   - Every retained file is served under the job's token, and an unretained name is a 404.

   The finished job checkpoints and completes once, loads at `hv-state/15`, and is refused at 13.
   *Test:* `hero-chain-route.test.ts`, `planner/test/hero-chain.test.ts` → "a hero render is a
   delivery job…", `hero-chain-recovery.test.ts`.
8. **Existing deliverables are unchanged.** Their plans, outputs and keys are the same. A mixed film
   is still refused with its own reason. The existing delivery suites pass unchanged.
   *Test:* the delivery suites listed under Evidence.

## Known gaps

- **No paid stage exists.** A vendor upscaler or interpolator needs that vendor approved (G3) and a
  reservation path of its own (G1). The interface refuses one until then, and a deliverable never
  reserves. Wiring a declared paid stage into admission is a later increment's decision.
- **Local filters, not model-based ones.** `hqdn3d`, `minterpolate` and Lanczos are what ffmpeg has.
  They are not Real-ESRGAN, RIFE or FILM, and nothing here measures the picture quality they produce
  on real footage. The tests prove the chain's shape, sizes, rates, frames and provenance, not that a
  hero shot looks better.
- **Export only.** The result does not replace the shot in a cut. If the creator wants that, it is an
  editorial operation with its own review, not built here.
- **Picture only, with no colour management.** The shot's sound is not carried, because a hero shot's
  sound is the cut's. The output is H.264 yuv420p with no colour-managed stage, which FULL-SCOPE's
  chain ends in.
- **Three lossy generations.** Each stage encodes at CRF 14. Lossless intermediates would cost
  gigabytes per 4K stage file. The generation loss is small and is stated rather than measured.
- **The deadline is a bound, not a measurement.** A hero job uses the mezzanine's per-frame allowance
  (30 minutes minimum). Single-threaded `minterpolate` at 1080p and a UHD60 encode have not been
  timed on the staging host.
- **Not exercised on PostgreSQL and RustFS here.** The s3 checkpoint now uploads every retained file,
  and the PostgreSQL admission and dispatch fences dispatch to the hero rules. Both run only in CI's
  full suite and on staging. This increment's tests use the local backend.
- **Verification is digest, record and probe, not reproduction.** A resumed or archived hero render
  re-digests every file, re-reads its record and re-probes its result. It does not re-run the chain.
- **No frontend panel.** The routes answer JSON.
- **`HV-019.hero-chain`'s front-door exercise** waits for the Release 3 run (step 16), on a live final
  shot.
