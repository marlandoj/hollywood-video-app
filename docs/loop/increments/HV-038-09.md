# HV-038-09 — The one series that took the whole operator console down

epic: HV-038
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

A critic pass over `packages/observability`.

`explorer.ts` reasons carefully about a row limit overflowing, and says exactly what it costs:

> a `rate(...[5m])` window spanning a deploy sees the union of the old and new label values, so every
> member of a retired set must remain a member of the current one or a complete result overflows its
> limit — and `metrics()` fails closed as a bundle, taking the latency and failure readings down
> with it until the next redeploy.

That is about **Prometheus-side** row limits. The same thing happens one layer upstream, and nobody
looked there.

Both metric views carried `aggregationCardinalityLimit: 256` over eight label keys. The label sets
are all declared, closed and right there in the same file — and they multiply far past 256:
**8 methods × 18 routes × 5 status classes is 720 for the `http.request` operations alone**, before
an outcome or a failure code. Past the 256th series the SDK stops distinguishing and collapses
everything into one series carrying `otel.metric.overflow` and **no other attributes at all**.

Measured, driving only label values the allow list itself admits:

```
operations emitted:              364
hv.operations data points kept:  256
overflow points:                 1  [{ attrs: {"otel.metric.overflow": true}, value: 109 }]
```

**109 operations — 30% — lost `hv_operation`, `hv_provider`, `hv_outcome` and `hv_failure_code`
entirely.** Every per-operation error ratio the console shows was understated by whatever fell in.

And then that one series took the console with it. `http_route!~"/api/operator/.*"` **matches an
absent label**, so every query returns the overflow row; `operationOf(undefined)` calls `invalid()`;
and the whole reliability bundle reads `unavailable`:

```
without the overflow series -> state: available | failures: [{"operation":"job.process",…}] | latency: 1 | providers: 1
with    the overflow series -> state: unavailable | value: null
```

On an operator console whose job is to say what is wrong.

## What this increment does

Three things, in the order they matter.

- **A dropped row is a dropped row.** `latencyRows`, `failureRows` and `providerRows` skip a series
  carrying `otel_metric_overflow` and count it, and the reading carries `droppedSeries`, so the
  console shows what it has and says what it is missing instead of showing nothing and saying
  nothing. Whatever the limit is, exceeding it must cost a row rather than the console — which is
  the part that would still have been true if the number below were wrong again.
- **The two labels nothing reads leave the metric.** `OPERATIONS_QUERY`, `FAILURES_QUERY`,
  `PROVIDER_ATTEMPTS_QUERY` and the latency quantiles group by `hv_operation`, `hv_outcome`,
  `hv_failure_code` and `hv_provider`, and match on `http_route` and `job`. `http.request.method` and
  `http.response.status_class` are grouped by nothing and matched on nothing, and together they
  multiplied the series count by **forty**. They stay on the span and in the log, where a reader
  looks at one request — the log carries `http.response.status_code` exactly, not the class.
  `hv.stage` is also read by no query and is kept anyway, at 9×, because it is the slice an operator
  asks Prometheus for by hand; that is a judgement, so it is written down in the test's table beside
  the five that are read.
- **The limit is a number with its arithmetic beside it.** With the sets as declared, and outcome
  and failure code on all of them: the http family is 18 × 2 × 9 = 324, the two provider operations
  are 2 × 7 × 9 × 2 × 9 = 2,268, and the remaining seven are 7 × 9 × 2 × 9 = 1,134 — about 3,700 if
  every combination occurred, which they do not. 4,096 clears that, is an order of magnitude above
  the 364 this studio was measured emitting, and is still small enough to be a limit.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **A cardinality overflow costs the console the series it collapsed, not the console**: with an
   overflow row in all three queries the reading is `available`, every real row is byte-identical to
   the reading without it, and `droppedSeries` is 3.
2. **A row that is merely malformed is still refused** — no operation and no marker, an operation
   outside the closed set, an outcome outside it. The tolerance is for the series the SDK writes on
   purpose, identified by the label it writes, and nothing else.
3. **The metric carries only the labels something reads, or names why it carries them anyway**: the
   key set is compared whole against a table, the five that claim to be read are checked against the
   query strings themselves, and the two that were dropped are checked to be still on the span and
   in the log.
4. **The limit clears each family it declares**, asserted as arithmetic over the declared sets
   rather than against a magic number, and is bounded above so a limit stays a limit.
5. Reverting the two source files makes the test file fail to load, because the constant it asserts
   about does not exist.

- Tests: `packages/observability/test/metric-overflow.test.ts`.

## Known gaps this leaves

- **Nothing measures the live cardinality.** The limit is arithmetic over declared sets plus one
  measurement of a synthetic drive; what the studio actually emits under real traffic is not
  observed, and the first sign of the limit being wrong again would be `droppedSeries` going
  non-zero — which is now visible, and is the whole point, but is still after the fact. A gauge of
  the SDK's own series count would be the direct answer.
- **`droppedSeries` is in the reading and not yet on the page.** The operator console renders the
  bundle; nothing draws this number. It is a field and a view, and the view is not here.
- **The overflow row is still counted into no total.** Its `value` is the sum of everything it
  collapsed — 109 operations in the measurement above — and this increment throws that number away
  rather than adding it to an "unattributed" line. Adding it would mean deciding whether an
  unattributed rate belongs in an error ratio, which it does not without knowing its outcome.
- **The same limit is on `hv.operation.duration`.** A histogram series is far heavier than a counter
  one, and 4,096 of them with sixteen buckets each is a different memory question from 4,096
  counters. Nothing here measured that.
- **This is the SDK's overflow, not Prometheus's.** The row limits `explorer.ts` already carries
  (`LATENCY_LIMIT`, `FAILURES_LIMIT`, `PROVIDER_LIMIT`) still fail the bundle closed when a *result*
  exceeds them, which is the case that file was written about and is unchanged.
