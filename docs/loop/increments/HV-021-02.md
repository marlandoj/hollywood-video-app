# HV-021-02 — The one continuity repair the studio can honestly make

epic: HV-021
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0 — no provider is called.

## Why

HV-021-01 made the studio able to see declared continuity drift. Seeing it is half the epic's Release
2 slice; the other half is "one-click repair", and the interesting question is not how to apply an
edit — it is **which drift a studio may fix on the creator's behalf at all**.

Only one kind qualifies. When a scene declares two different key lights, there is a fact of the
matter about what the creator did first, and making the scene agree with it is a mechanical edit that
cannot be wrong about intent. When a scene headed `- DAY` contains a shot directed "night", the
studio can see the contradiction and cannot resolve it: either the heading is wrong or the direction
is. A repair that picked one would be right about half the time and would silently overwrite a
screenplay decision the rest.

So this increment fixes the first and refuses the second **out loud**, which is the part worth
building carefully.

## What this increment does

- **`packages/planner/src/continuityRepair`** (`packages/planner/src/continuity-repair.ts`) turns a
  continuity report into a proposal: the edits that would hold each scene's look, bound to the
  report's revision and to the same casting revision, direction revision and source plan hash that
  report was computed from. The value a scene holds is the one its **first declaring shot** states.
- **The notes say what it will not touch and why** — the heading contradiction, wardrobe (a cast
  record, not a shot direction), a missing reference image (nothing a direction edit can make), the
  absent frame handoff (a rendering choice that is the creator's), and a stale source (review that
  shot first). A repair that stayed silent about the rest would read as though the rest were fine.
- **`POST /api/projects/:id/direction/continuity/repair`** reads it back with a one-line summary, and
  changes nothing. **`.../repair/accept`** applies it under the direction and screenplay versions the
  creator held.
- **The edits are recomputed on the server and must match the caller's copy exactly**, the way an
  accepted scene cut must echo the directions it will destroy. A repair can only ever apply what the
  creator was actually shown.
- **Each edit moves a setting and nothing else.** The entry keeps the source and the hash it was
  saved against, so a repair can never re-bind a shot to a source the creator did not review.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **The value a scene holds is its first declaring shot's.** Every later shot that disagrees gets
   one edit per field; the first shot is never edited; a shot that agrees is left alone; and case and
   spacing are not drift.
2. **The proposal is bound to the report it was read from** — its revision and that report's casting
   revision, direction revision and source plan hash.
3. **What it will not repair is stated**, one note per kind, deduplicated, including the heading
   contradiction it could have guessed at and deliberately does not.
4. **A stale shot is out of the repair as it is out of the report**, and the note says to review it.
5. **Applying a repair holds the scene's look**: the drifted shot takes the held value, the shot that
   stated it is untouched, its source binding is unchanged, no shot becomes stale, the desk's own
   report no longer finds the drift, and a second review finds nothing to repair.
6. **A repair applies only what the creator was shown**: an invented edit, an empty set, a stale
   direction version and a stale screenplay version are each refused with nothing applied, and the
   routes are owner-only. A film with no drift has nothing to accept, and says so.

- Tests: `packages/planner/test/continuity-repair.test.ts` (1–4),
  `packages/api/test/continuity-repair-route.test.ts` (5–6).

## Known gaps this leaves, stated rather than implied

- **There is still no Continuity Supervisor persona.** The repair exists; the crew member who offers
  it in the studio's conversation, asks its three questions and appears in the credits is the next
  increment. Adding a seventh persona touches the crew tables, the front door and the titles, and it
  deserves its own increment rather than riding along with the repair engine.
- **No frontend control ships here.** The Director's desk serves the report already; the button that
  reads the proposal and accepts it comes with the persona.
- **Still nothing visual.** This repairs declarations against each other. The drift FULL-SCOPE P6
  describes — similarity to identity locks, wardrobe and palette match — needs an image model, and
  `docs/CONTINUITY.md` still says so.
- **A repair is one scene-look at a time, up to 240 edits.** A film with more drift than that is told
  to fix a scene at a time rather than handed a change it cannot read.
