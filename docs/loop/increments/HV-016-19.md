# HV-016-19 — A cue with a lowercase extension, a curly apostrophe, an accent, a `^` or an `@` was read as action

epic: HV-016
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

The parser decided a character cue with one pattern over the whole line,
`/^[A-Z][A-Z0-9 '().-]*$/`. Fountain cues that fail it are common:

- `HANS (on the radio)` — the Fountain spec's own example; an extension is written in any case;
- `MAYA (CONT’D)`, `O’BRIEN` — the typographic apostrophe word processors type;
- `JOSÉ` — a capital outside A to Z;
- `STEEL ^` — the second speaker of dual dialogue;
- `@McCLANE` — Fountain's forced cue, for a name that is not all capitals.

Each became an action line, and so did every line spoken under it, with no warning. The speech was not
voiced, not captioned and not in the coverage asks, and it became an extra shot whose prompt read the
dialogue as scene description (`STEEL ^` put a caret in the prompt).

## What this increment does

- **The cue's name and its extension are tested separately** (`packages/parser/src/index.ts`). The
  name is capitals in any script, with digits, spaces and `'’().-`; the trailing `(...)` extension is
  any case. Every line the old pattern accepted is still accepted and names the same speaker.
- **A trailing `^` is dropped** and the line is the second speaker's cue.
- **A leading `@` forces a cue** whatever its case, and is dropped from the name.
- **`cueCharacter` is the one place a cue's name is derived**, exported for the living script
  document, whose evidence check compared the cue line with its own copy of the extension strip and
  would otherwise have refused every document holding a `^` or `@` cue.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Charset.** `HANS (on the radio)`, `MAYA (CONT’D)`, `O’BRIEN`, `JOSÉ` and `ZOË (V.O.)` are cues
   and keep their speech; the scene has no action.
2. **Same speaker.** `MAYA (CONT’D)` and `MAYA (CONT'D)` both name MAYA, and the scene's cast finds
   MAYA under either.
3. **Dual dialogue.** `STEEL ^` is STEEL's cue; the scene has no action and no shot prompt holds a `^`.
4. **Forced cue.** `@McCLANE` speaks as McCLANE, with or without an extension and caret; `McCLANE`
   and `Maya (quietly)` without the `@` are still action.
5. **Nothing correct changes.** Every sample the old pattern accepted is a cue with the same name, and
   the neighbour rule (HV-016-06) still makes a lone `JOSÉ` action.
6. **Living script.** A document holding `^`, `@` and `’` cues binds each speech to its cue line.
7. Reverting the source files fails criteria 1–6; reverting only the living script's check fails 6.

- Tests: `packages/parser/test/character-cue-marks.test.ts`.

## Known gaps this leaves

- **The Final Draft importer keeps its own cue pattern** and still refuses `JOSÉ` or `O’BRIEN` with a
  message naming the cue. It refuses loudly rather than losing the speech; relaxing it is a separate
  change to what an import accepts.
- **Dual dialogue is two speeches in sequence,** not two side by side. The film has no simultaneous
  speech.
- **A name in a script without capitals** (CJK, for instance) still needs the `@`.
- **`O’BRIEN` and `O'BRIEN` are two names** to casting, as `O'BRIEN` and `OBRIEN` always were.
