# HV-016-09 — A reopened project link took the creator to the Director's desk, not to their film

epic: HV-016
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

`docs/CREW.md` lists this under "Not yet", in its own words:

> **Resuming inside the studio.** A reopened project link opens the Director's desk, because the
> studio does not yet rebuild its step from the project.

`index.html` did exactly that:

```js
showDesk(deskPreferred || current?.kind === "p");
```

A creator who closed the tab and came back to their own link landed in every detailed panel of the
application — the surface HV-030-03 put behind the Advanced switch *precisely because it is not the
front door* — and the studio itself, if they switched back to it, was at the pitch with an empty
box. So the one path the studio offered a returning creator was **to pitch the script again, which
renders, and charges, again**, for a film that was already rendered and already paid for.

This is the front door's half of a promise the rest of the application already keeps: `resumeProject`
rebuilds the desk from the project in some detail. The studio had nothing.

## What this increment does

`flow.resume()` rebuilds the studio's step from `GET /api/projects/:id`, which holds what was
**made** — the script, and every job with its stage and status.

It does not hold what the crew **said**: the read-through is the model's answer and is not stored.
Nor what the creator told the crew: the format, the tone, and the replies to the questions. So the
furthest step this rebuilds is the furthest one whose evidence is in the project, and each one says
what it could not bring back rather than inventing it:

| In the project | Resumes to | What it says it lost |
|---|---|---|
| A finished `final` | the film, and the share step | the read-through, so the first pass's questions are not shown. Nothing further is generated from the tone or the answers, so this step resumes whole |
| A finished `animatic` | the rough cut, and the approval | the tone and the answers, so the final will be scored and titled with the Composer's own direction — said **before** they approve |
| A saved script, nothing rendered | the pitch, with the script in the box | nothing was rendered, so the crew reads it again and that costs nothing |
| Nothing | the pitch, empty | nothing, because there is nothing to explain |

- **Only a job that is `done` resumes a step.** A queued, running, failed or cancelled final is not
  a film to show or share, and a project holding one resumes to the rough cut instead.
- **The latest finished job of a stage is the one**, because a film may be rendered more than once.
- **`requestChanges` refuses on a resumed rough cut, by name.** Sending the crew back means asking
  for a fresh read-through, and that needs the format and the tone this film was pitched with.
  Reading the script back with a format nobody chose would be the studio inventing the creator's
  own answer, so it says what is missing and offers the two things that are possible.
- **`index.html` opens the studio on a project link** rather than forcing the desk. The desk is
  still rebuilt behind the Advanced switch and the switch's own remembered preference still wins, so
  a creator who prefers the desk keeps it.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **A project with a finished film resumes to the film**, with its spend, its script and the share
   step — not to the pitch.
2. **A project with a rough cut resumes to the approval**, so the cut already paid for is not
   rendered again, and the note says so and names what the final will be missing.
3. **A project with a script and nothing rendered resumes to the pitch with the script in the box**,
   and a project with nothing resumes to the pitch with nothing to explain.
4. **An unfinished render is not a film**: queued, running, failed and cancelled each resume to the
   step below, and the latest finished job of a stage is the one chosen.
5. **A resumed rough cut can be approved** — straight through to the final — **but not sent back**,
   which is refused by name. The fake throws if the crew is asked for a read-through, so a missing
   guard would fail differently.
6. **Resuming asks the project once and the crew not at all**: two reads, the project and the spend.
7. Reverting `studio.js` makes criteria 1–6 fail, because `flow.resume` does not exist.

- Test: `packages/frontend/test/studio-resume.test.js`.

## Known gaps this leaves

- **The `look` step cannot be resumed at all.** It is the crew's read-through and plan, and the
  read-through is the model's answer to a script — not stored anywhere. A project that got as far as
  the crew's questions and no further comes back to the pitch. Retaining the crew's own side of the
  conversation would let that step come back whole, and is a storage change rather than a front-door
  one.
- **A resumed final is scored and titled by whatever was already rendered**, and a resumed rough cut
  that is approved is scored with the Composer's default direction. The note says so; it does not
  offer to ask for the tone again, which would be the better answer and is more UI than this change.
- **`index.html` is not tested here.** The boot decision — open the studio on a project link, unless
  this browser asked for the desk — is three lines in a page this repo has no DOM harness for. The
  flow it calls is tested; the call is not.
- **Nothing resumes a render in flight.** A project whose final is still running resumes to the
  rough cut rather than waiting on that job, even though HV-030-08 now gives the studio a bounded
  way to wait. Picking a running job back up is the natural next increment and would close the gap
  HV-030-08 named for itself.
