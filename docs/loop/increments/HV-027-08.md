# HV-027-08 — A reframe copied the film's whole lossless conform to read its master

epic: HV-027
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

`deliveryBindingFor` names every file of the sealed conform — the master, `index.ffconcat`,
`final.wav` and every `part-NNNNN.mkv` — for all three kinds. That is right, and its own comment says
why: nothing enumerates another job's artifacts under `s3`, and the binding is made before a kind is
chosen. It is what the job is **bound to**.

It was also what the job **copied**:

```ts
const wanted=binding.files.reduce((total,file)=>total+file.bytes,0);
assertEditFreeSpace(root,wanted*2);
await withEditSourceAccess(access,signal,active=>
  copyDialogueFiles({…},binding.files,root,sources,active,reader));
```

Only the mezzanine branch touches the conform directory. The reframe branch opens `master` and
nothing else. So a creator asking for a 9:16 or 1:1 crop of a finished film pulled the whole lossless
FFV1 picture master through the artifact reader, re-digested it, and reserved twice its size before
starting.

Measured on this epic's own test fixture — an HD film of 900 frames, 15 parts of 60 MB, a 4 MB
master:

```
mezzanine reads   912,640,556 bytes
reframe reads       4,000,000 bytes      — 228× less
```

`EDIT_STORAGE_LIMITS.outputBytes/2` caps the conform at 24 GiB, so a real feature-length film means
up to 24 GiB copied and ~48 GiB reserved. On a host without that headroom the crop is refused
outright — with the *editorial worker's* message, *"The editorial worker needs more free workspace…
use a smaller assembly"* — for bytes the job never opens.

Nothing caught it because the end-to-end delivery test renders a 640×360 three-second fixture, where
the whole conform is a few megabytes, and `delivery-reframe.test.ts` calls the renderer directly and
never goes through `renderDeliveryJob`.

## What this increment does

- **`deliveryReadFiles(plan)`** answers what a delivery of this kind actually opens, out of the
  inventory it is bound to: the whole conform for a mezzanine, the master alone for a reframe.
- **`renderDeliveryJob` copies that and reserves from that**, because a reservation is for the copy
  it is reserving for. The binding is unchanged: what a job is bound to is not what it opens.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **A mezzanine reads the whole conform and a reframe reads the master alone**, with the master in
   both, and the binding itself untouched.
2. **The difference is the whole picture master**, measured on this epic's own fixture: 228 times,
   and a reframe's doubled reservation is still less than a mezzanine's single read.
3. **The renderer copies what it reads rather than what it is bound to**, and reserves from the same
   list — the mezzanine still reaching into the conform directory it copied.
4. Reverting `packages/planner/src/delivery-jobs.ts` fails all three.

- Tests: `packages/planner/test/delivery-read-files.test.ts`. Verified: all three fail on the
  unchanged source.

## Known gaps this leaves

- **The reservation is still `wanted * 2`.** That doubling was for a copy plus a render writing
  beside it; for a reframe the render's output is a crop of the master and smaller than it, so the
  multiplier is now conservative in a different direction. Nobody has measured what it should be.
- **No test renders a reframe through `renderDeliveryJob`.** The selection is asserted and the call
  site is guarded, but the end-to-end path is still only exercised by the 640×360 fixture, where the
  difference this fixes is invisible.
- **The binding is still the same for every kind.** A reframe's job record names 18 files it will
  never open, and `assertDeliverySourceAvailable` checks all of them at admission — which is more
  checking than the job needs, and is not obviously wrong.
- **Nothing tells the creator what a deliverable will cost in disk.** The offer says whether a kind
  is available and why not; it does not say that a mezzanine needs 48 GiB of workspace and a crop
  needs 8 MB.
