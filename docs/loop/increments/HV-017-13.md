# HV-017-13 — Some stills were pinned, and the studio said none had been

epic: HV-017
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

HV-017-06 pins each storyboard still of the rough cut as its shot's first frame and then re-cuts the
rough cut from them, so that what the creator approves at approval 2 is what the final renders. Each
pin is saved as it is made — an anchor upload, then a direction save — and nothing undoes it.

`pinStills` threw on the first refusal, and the caller caught it with one sentence:

> Cinematographer: the storyboard stills could not be pinned as the final's first frames (…); the
> final begins from the script.

If shot 3 of 5 was refused — the project's 96-image reference limit, *"Image processing is busy"*, a
direction version that moved — shots 1 and 2 were already anchored. The creator was told none were
and that the final would begin from the script. The final began from those two stills. And the rough
cut was not re-cut from them, so the cut approved at approval 2 was not the cut approval 3 rendered:
the exact mismatch HV-017-06 exists to close ("NORA changed between the storyboard and the final").

The same catch also covered a failure of the **re-cut**, after every pin had been saved, with the
same untrue sentence.

`studio.test.js` covers pinning only with a stub in which every upload and every save succeeds.

## What this increment does

- **`pinStills` answers how many it pinned and what stopped it**, instead of throwing and losing the
  count. It stops at the first refusal, because a full library, a busy image slot or a moved
  direction version refuses the next shot for the same reason.
- **The note follows what was saved.** None pinned: the old sentence, which is then true. Some
  pinned: how many, why the rest were not, and that those shots begin from their still. Any pinned:
  the rough cut is re-cut from them, so approval 2 shows what the final will render.
- **A failed re-cut says what is actually the case**: the stills were pinned, the final will begin
  from them, and the rough cut on screen does not show them.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **A pin that stopped part-way says how many it made**, names the refusal, does not say the final
   begins from the script, and the rough cut is re-cut from the pins that were made.
2. **A pin that made nothing** keeps the original sentence exactly, and nothing is re-cut.
3. **A re-cut that failed after every pin was saved** says the stills were pinned and the rough cut
   does not show them, and the creator still reaches approval 2.
4. **When everything works there is nothing to say.**
5. Reverting `packages/frontend/src/studio.js` fails criteria 1 and 3.

- Tests: `packages/frontend/test/studio-partial-pin.test.js`.

## Known gaps this leaves

- **The pins that were made are not undone**, and the creator is not offered a way to undo them from
  the front door; they are visible and removable in the Director's desk.
- **The shots that were not pinned are not retried.** A busy image slot clears on its own; asking
  again means asking for a new look, which re-plans.
- **A failed re-cut leaves the creator approving a rough cut the note says is stale**, with no button
  to ask for the re-cut again. Approving it anyway is safe — the final is made from the direction,
  not from the rough cut — but it is not what HV-017-06 set out to guarantee.
