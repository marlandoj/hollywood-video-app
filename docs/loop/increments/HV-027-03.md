# HV-027-03 — What a finished cut can be delivered as

release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0 — pure planner work. No provider, no file, no clock is read.

## Why

HV-027-01 and HV-027-02 each built a deliverable and each ended the same way: *nothing calls this*.
HV-026-03 ended the same way about the quality check. All three name the same blocker — the job
stage that carries a deliverable — and HV-027-01 sized it: "a plan field, an output kind, a
validator, a worker, a checkpoint, an artifact-permission entry and signed URLs."

Before any of that is worth writing, one question has to be answered: **what does a delivery job take
as its input?** That is a design decision, not a wiring exercise, and getting it wrong means the
stage has to be rebuilt. This increment answers it and nothing else, so the stage that follows has
something settled to implement against.

## What this increment decides

- **A deliverable is a new job that names the old one.** A finished job's artifact set is sealed
  three ways, so a deliverable cannot be added to a completed job — and should not be: the film
  someone delivered is the film they approved.
- **It is bound to the sealed output's revision, not to the job id.** A job id says which render; the
  revision says which *bytes*, over the whole retained file list. Render the film again and the
  revision moves, so a deliverable of the old one is visibly a deliverable of a different film rather
  than a stale file with the right name.
- **The master's digest and size come from the sealed inventory**, not from a fresh look at the disk.
  The inventory is what the revision is computed over and what verification reproduces. A sealed
  output that names a master its own inventory does not contain is refused here rather than delivered
  from.
- **The idempotency key is the output revision and the kind, and nothing else** — not the job id,
  which would make an identical deliverable of a re-render a different one, and not a clock.
- **The conform directory is derived from the master's own path**, because two fields that must agree
  are two fields that can disagree. Every conform writes its export to `<conform>/export.mp4`; a
  master elsewhere is not one this studio made.
- **The binding names every file the renderer will read, with its digest** — the master, the
  conform's ffconcat index, its picture parts in order, and the final mix — and records the storage
  backend, as every other plan does. Named rather than discovered, because **nothing in this studio
  enumerates another job's artifacts under `s3`**: every cross-job read goes one declared file at a
  time through the artifact reader, which checks each file's digest and length as it streams it. A
  plan that said "the conform directory" would work on a local disk and have nothing to ask for on
  staging. The list is selected from the sealed inventory, so the inventory's recorded size of the
  final mix is checked against the arithmetic the conform cannot disagree with, and its picture parts
  are checked against the conform's own count and recorded bytes.
- **Every kind is answered, including the ones that cannot be made**, each with the reason the
  planner that refused it gave. An offer list showing only what is possible reads as though the rest
  had never been considered — the same reason the quality check carries `notChecked` and the
  continuity report carries comparison counters.

### One thing this increment found and fixed

`deliveryMezzaninePlan` was doing two jobs: checking that a conform's record describes a film, and
checking that a *lossless master of that film* fits beside it. Binding to a conform needs the first
and not the second, and because they were one function, a film too long for a mezzanine could not be
bound to at all — so its reframes, which cost a fraction of the size, were refused with it. The first
half is now `assertMezzanineSource`, and the offers test is what caught it.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **A binding round-trips and re-derives**: `validateDeliveryBinding` rebuilds it from its own parts
   and refuses one whose master's size was changed under its revision.
2. **The same deliverable of the same sealed output is the same job**, from a different job id and a
   different source stage; a different output revision is a different job; each kind is its own.
3. **Every kind is answered.** An HD master offers all three with plans; a 640×360 master offers the
   square and refuses the vertical, naming `202 by 360` and the `256-pixel minimum`; a film too long
   for a lossless master of itself loses only the mezzanine.
4. **A binding is refused when it does not describe a film this studio made**: a bad project or job
   id, a stage that makes no conform, a malformed output revision, a master with no digest or no
   bytes, a master that is not at `conform/export.mp4`, and a conform whose own record does not add
   up — the last of these whichever deliverable is being asked for.
5. **`deliveryBindingFor` reads the binding off what the job sealed**: the master, the index, the
   parts and the mix from the inventory; the revision from the right key for the stage; and refusals
   when the inventory does not contain the master the output names, holds a different number of
   picture parts than the conform's own record claims, or is missing a file the renderer needs. A
   file the renderer does not read — the provenance manifest — is not in the reading list.
6. **A plan re-derives from its own binding** and refuses one whose kind or idempotency key was
   edited under it.
7. **Nothing else changes**: the reframe, mezzanine and picture-quality suites pass unchanged.

## Known gaps this leaves, stated rather than implied

- **There is still no `delivery` job stage**, so nothing can create one of these. That is the next
  increment and it is now the only thing between three built deliverables and a creator: the stage in
  `render-stage.ts`, the `hv_jobs` CHECK and its migration, the queue's plan/checkpoint/output fields
  and their exclusions, a snapshot tier and its allowed-stage list, the ledger's zero-cost rule and a
  permission re-read, a worker, an `artifactPermission` case, and the routes.
- **No signed URL, no creator route, no frontend.** An offer list is computable; nothing serves it.
- **The offers are computed, not cached.** Each one builds a full plan, which for three kinds is
  three `contentHash` calls over small objects. That is cheap now and is stated because a per-shot
  or per-job cache is the kind of thing that gets added without measuring.
- **A reframe's placement is still centred.** `deliveryReframePlan` takes an anchor and the offers do
  not expose it, because nothing yet decides where the frame should sit. A crew persona choosing a
  placement is later work and the plan already carries the field it would set.
- **Burned subtitles and SDH are not here**, and the decision HV-027-01 recorded about them — that a
  burned deliverable must be terminal and never admissible as a source — is still waiting on a human.
