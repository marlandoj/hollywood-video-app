# HV-022-21 — Voice holds at feature scale: a take holds its own line

epic: HV-022
release: 3 "Features"
gate: none new (cites G14-202609210000 and G20-202610031349)
spend_usd: 0

## Why

Release 3's build order, step 6 (`docs/ROADMAP.md`, agreed at G20), and constraint 5 in HV-030-27:
every ElevenLabs take held the price of 10,000 characters, whatever the line said.

The catalogue (HV-022-07) prices a voice as the plan's rate times the line ceiling:
`elevenLabsTakeHoldUsd(planUsdPerPeriod, characterLimit)` times `ELEVENLABS_MAX_LINE_CHARACTERS`.
Admission then reserved that `policy.heldUsd` for every take. On the operator's plan ($5 for
90,000 characters, `docs/evidence/release-2/elevenlabs-voiced.json`) that is $0.555556 a take. G14's
$25 voice line therefore fitted 44 takes in all, and the line already holds $14.44. A 15–20 minute
feature has about 150–250 lines. The same hold also counts toward the film limit: 200 takes would
hold $111, past a short's $40 limit and most of a feature's $150 (HV-030-28).

## What this increment does

- **A take holds its own line.** `elevenLabsLineHoldUsd(policy, characters)` in
  `packages/planner/src/audio-jobs.ts` returns the policy's authorized rate (`heldUsd` over
  `maxCharacters`) times the characters the request sends. `elevenLabsLineRequest` sends
  `spokenText`, and its UTF-16 length is never less than the character count the vendor bills.
- **Margin.** It keeps the margin the existing hold policy has. An audio take is one dispatch with
  no retry (`maxRetries: 0`), so one line's worth is held, rounded up to the micro-dollar the ledger
  stores, never down. The arithmetic uses integer micro-dollars (BigInt), so a 10,000-character line
  holds exactly the policy's price and no line holds more.
- **The hold is recorded on the take.** `AudioTakePlan.heldUsd` is set by `audioTakePlan` for
  ElevenLabs takes. It is inside the take's revision and is recomputed by `validateAudioTake`, so a
  forged hold is refused. `audioTakeHoldUsd(take)` is the single reader. Every place that read
  `take.policy.heldUsd` as a take's hold now uses it:
  - admission (film limit, vendor line, alerts, reservation) and the dispatch journal in
    `audio-ledger.ts`
  - the job's `costCapUsd`/`budgetReservedUsd` checks
  - the take route and its 202 answer, and the job and applied-audition billing views in `server.ts`
  - retained auditions
  - the snapshot validator's liability checks
- **Takes already admitted keep their hold.** An ElevenLabs take admitted before this has no
  `heldUsd`. It still validates at the ceiling it reserved, so stored jobs, retained auditions,
  snapshots and restores read exactly as before.
- **The review panel** (`audio-studio.js`) no longer shows the ceiling as an ElevenLabs take's
  reservation. It says the take holds this line's own characters at the voice's rate.
- **Nothing is raised.** The $25 vendor line, its $5/$15 alerts, the film limit, the monthly cap and
  the 10,000-character line ceiling are unchanged. An over-the-line take is refused exactly as
  before.
- **Overruns are handled as they always were.** `settleAudioInvoice` records the invoice allocation
  in full: it is not clamped to the hold, and it counts on the vendor's line and the film. It
  releases the hold and cancels a take still queued or running whose allocation exceeds its job
  cap. Smaller holds make that path more likely, and it is unchanged and now tested with an
  ElevenLabs take. `scripts/reconcile-audio.ts` calls the same `settleAudioInvoice`.

On the operator's plan, a 40-character line holds $0.002223 and a 120-character line $0.006667. A
250-line feature of long lines holds under $2 of the $25 line.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **A 40-character line holds proportionally.** At $0.555556 per 10,000 characters it holds
   exactly $0.002223 (2,222.224 micro-dollars, rounded up), and $0.002 on the fixture's $0.50 rate.
   This holds in the take, the job's cap and reservation, the `hv_reservations` row, the route's
   answer, the owner's billing view and the film's held total.
2. **A long line holds more.** 400 characters hold $0.022223. No line holds more than the policy's
   price, and 10,000 characters hold exactly that price.
3. **About 200 typical lines fit the $25 line.** In the arithmetic, 250 lines of 120 characters are
   admitted under $25, against 44 at the ceiling hold. On PostgreSQL, 200 takes are admitted through
   `admitAudio` with $25 of room on the vendor's line and the $40 film limit, for $1.33.
4. **An over-the-line take is still refused.** The next take with one micro-dollar too little room
   on the vendor's line, or on the film's limit, is refused and holds nothing. With exactly enough
   room it is admitted.
5. **An overrun is settled as before.** An ElevenLabs take dispatches with its line's hold, and its
   answer never arrives. It is then allocated $0.01 against a $0.002223 hold. The cost event and
   the vendor's line carry the full $0.01, the hold is released, and the running take is cancelled
   with "The audio invoice allocation exceeded its job cap." The settled take still exports through
   the snapshot validator.
6. **Takes already admitted and other vendors are unchanged.** An ElevenLabs take without its own
   hold validates at the ceiling and only there. Azure and Cartesia takes carry no line hold and
   hold their policy's amount, and a line hold added to them is refused. A job reserving anything
   other than its take's hold is refused.
7. **Each take's hold counts against its own film's limit (HV-030-28).** Through the take route,
   a feature and a short each admit 200 typical takes, $1.3334 held against $150 and $40. At the
   ceiling hold a short was refused its 72nd take and a feature its 270th. With both limits scaled
   to cents, a short at $0.02 is refused its third take and a feature at $0.05 its eighth. Each is
   refused by the film's own spending limit.
8. Existing voice-line, vendor-budget, ElevenLabs and audio-take suites pass unchanged, and so do
   HV-030-28's `packages/operator/test/feature-film-budget.test.ts` and
   `packages/api/test/feature-film-limit-route.test.ts`.

## Known gaps this leaves

- **Holds already on the line stay at the ceiling.** The $14.44 the line holds is in takes admitted
  before this. It is freed only when the operator allocates the invoice with
  `scripts/reconcile-audio.ts`, as HV-030-27 notes.
- **The rate is the plan's allowance rate.** The vendor may bill characters beyond the allowance
  (overage) at a different rate, or count normalized text differently. Such a take is an overrun
  that settlement records in full. It is not prevented at admission, and neither was it under the
  ceiling hold, since that ceiling used the same rate.
- **Azure has the same shape, and it does not bind.** Azure holds a fixed $0.03 for up to 1,500
  characters (`AZURE_TAKE_HOLD_USD`). That is 833 takes on a $25 line and $6 of film limit for 200
  takes. Cartesia's fixture holds are per take too. Both are unchanged here. If either vendor
  carries a feature, the same line-sized hold can be applied.
- **The review panel states the rate, not the cents.** The client doesn't compile the spoken text
  (pronunciations can change it), so the exact hold appears once the take is admitted, in the
  route's answer and the take's billing view.
