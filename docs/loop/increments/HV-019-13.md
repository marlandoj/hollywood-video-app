# HV-019-13 — The lease-loss test still failed CI: another claim's sweep took its job

epic: HV-019
release: 2 "Voice and crew depth"
gate: none new
spend_usd: 0

## Why

HV-019-12 stopped the old worker's own lease timer from seeing its lease expire. The test still
failed on 2026-09-29, on #295, with the same symptom:

```
expect((await processing)!.claimedBy).toBe("new-worker");
Expected: "new-worker"
Received: null
```

HV-019-12 had found only one of two causes. `PostgresJobStore.claimNext(now)` first recovers every
abandoned job whose lease has expired at the `now` it is given. The rest of the suite claims at
`Date.now()`, and so does any worker loop a test left running. This test's clock started at
`Date.now()` and its lease is 150 ms long, so on the wall clock the lease expired 150 ms into the
test. Any claim anywhere in the suite after that put the job back in the queue, unclaimed. The old
worker, finding its lease gone, read the job at that moment and returned it with `claimedBy: null`.
The replacement then claimed it. HV-019-12's 120 ms pause made this more likely, not less.

## What this increment does

- **The test's clock runs a minute ahead of the wall clock.** A sweep at wall-clock time finds the
  lease live and leaves the job alone.
- **The test now performs such a sweep itself**, 400 ms on the wall clock, during the pause. It
  checks that the job is still the old worker's.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **With the clock ahead.** A wall-clock sweep during the test leaves the job with the old worker.
   The replacement then claims it, and the old worker's provider is aborted after one call. The old
   worker returns the job as claimed by `new-worker`. This passed on all three local runs against
   PostgreSQL 16.
2. **With the old clock.** Starting the test's clock at `Date.now()` fails the test
   deterministically with CI's own failure, `Received: null`, on both of two local runs.

- Tests: `packages/storage/test/pipeline.test.ts` (PostgreSQL-gated).

## Known gaps this leaves

- **Test-only.** Recovering a job whose lease has expired is correct behaviour. What made the test
  flaky was its own clock, not the product.
- **Other tests may share the pattern.** Other PostgreSQL tests that claim with a short lease on a
  clock near the wall clock could be exposed to the same sweep. None has failed in CI so far.
