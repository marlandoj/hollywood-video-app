# Scene coverage proposals

The owner can propose, edit, discard and explicitly accept an ordered scene cut in the private shot editor. The proposal creates actual master/action shots, speaker angles and optional silent reaction views. It displays the film's old/new shot count and duration before automatic speech timing and assembly transitions. Roles, subjects, timing, beat allocation and scene/shot notes are editable. Notes enter generation prompts and remain in direction history and provenance for replay.

The parser retains ordered visible action, dialogue and transitions with original one-based source lines. Existing `action`, `dialogue` and `transitions` arrays and legacy allocation remain unchanged. Protected notes/boneyard lines remain excluded under the existing parser rules. Coverage uses recognized Fountain content; this is not a complete new Fountain parser. Semantic scene bindings exclude source line numbers so an inserted blank line or protected comment does not invalidate a cut. Screenplay, cast and direction revisions still bind each acceptance.

Each narrative action/dialogue beat must occur exactly once, in order. Silent views reference the immediately preceding beat (or opening), have no dialogue, and cannot duplicate narrative ownership. Transition beats remain in the source review/history; they do not change the assembler's transition implementation. Default proposals use unspecified geometry; roles and subjects are creative intent, not observed camera placement. The existing declared coverage checker identifies missing or unknown relationships. Native geometry and cinematic quality are not established by this feature.

Large scenes group adjacent beats into at most 60 proposed shots without omitting content. The owner sees the grouping and can edit it. Accepted cuts never silently truncate at a tier boundary: film admission rejects plans exceeding the selected 24/60-shot limit. A proposal over the selected limit can be reviewed/edited but cannot be accepted. Duration values are 30–900 frames at 30 fps or automatic; automatic speech timing and the provider's actual capability/cost gates still apply.

## Persistence and source compilation

Accepted cuts are an optional `sceneCuts` array in `hv-direction/1`. Their normalized content is included in the direction revision. Legacy snapshots without the array keep their original hash. Ordinary direction changes preserve the cuts; restore creates a new revision containing the selected history's cut and direction entries. The existing 100-version retention applies.

Coverage IDs use `shot-S-10001` and higher. Unaffected base shots keep their existing source and seed when a scene cut is accepted; coverage seeds are deterministic from their stable ID and the job's base seed. Shot edits preserve IDs. The source compiler supplies quotes/admission, workers, takes, source image and movement tools, reuse keys and snapshot validation. Source text/notes and dialogue are bound in the normal per-shot input hash. Existing selective rendering reuses only unchanged effective shots and verifies their independent copied bytes.

Changed or removed screenplay scenes make accepted cuts stale. The editor exposes that state and permits replacement/removal; film generation refuses until resolved. Replacing sources lists the affected saved directions and requires explicit acknowledgement before removing those entries from the current version. Earlier directions, anchors and movement plans remain retained under their existing rules; they are not rebound to new shots. Source-bound movement exports become stale and require review. History restoration can recover the earlier cut and directions.

Owner routes, relative to `/api/projects/:id/direction`:

- `POST /scene-cuts`: review `{sceneIndex,maxShots,includeReactions?}`; removal uses `remove:true`. Edits use the returned `binding` and `{edits:{shots,notes}}`. Returns the hash-bound proposal, ordered source lines and impact, without writing history.
- `POST /scene-cuts/accept`: `{proposal,removeDirectionIds}`. Rechecks current owner, retention, screenplay, cast, direction and full source, shot limit and exact direction-removal acknowledgement. PostgreSQL runs the mutation under the existing project row lock.

Preview approval remains bound to the new direction revision. A prior approval cannot authorize the changed final. Full output provenance, completed media records and portable archives retain cuts, notes and origins. No SQL migration is needed.
