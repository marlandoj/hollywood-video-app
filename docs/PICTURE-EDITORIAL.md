# Picture editorial implementation in progress

The timeline, bounded conform, source preparation, project histories and provider-free render jobs are implemented locally. The owner browser editor now connects source inspection, sequences, exact frame controls, saved branches and reviewed export admission. Completed edits support comparison playback, selected export and owner downloads. The timeline overview displays saved timing; playback uses completed exports. Realtime proxy preview and the remaining P8 requirements are still open.

The model retains up to 16 immutable source descriptions, 256 clips and 256 markers at 30 fps. Audio addresses 1600 stereo 48 kHz samples per picture frame. Picture has four layers; source mix, dialogue, narration, music, ambience, effects and captions have independent lanes. Source mix can preserve a previously mastered nonlinear soundtrack; separate pre-master stems do not reconstruct that master automatically.

Linked clips share source and timing. Split, move, trim, ripple removal/insertion/reorder, slip, roll and slide operate on linked ranges together. An explicit unlink allows independent audio movement for L/J cuts. Ripple boundaries that cross unrelated clips are refused with a correction message. Split clips retain source-relative fade envelopes, preserving their samples and picture output. Deliberate trim/settings edits reanchor envelopes. A slip can move the virtual envelope origin before source zero; the renderer computes alpha directly from that phase without decoding a padded prefix. Markers move with reordered/rippled ranges.

Edit history records immutable edit nodes and separate cursor events. Undo, redo and branch selection append events; they do not erase abandoned edits. Every node replays its operation from its recorded parent. Expected history revisions detect concurrent writes. The current limit is 1000 events per history. Project services now persist the library and reload it across local consoles. PostgreSQL mutations use the existing project row lock; sequence creation also rechecks current source jobs and artifact metadata. The project library permits 64 receipts, 32 sequences and 64 MiB of metadata. Each sequence binds exact source receipts, so later retention or review records can coexist without rewriting old branches. Replay shares the validated immutable source catalog between nodes.

The conform engine verifies input hashes and decoded picture dimensions/frame counts, independently seeks and verifies selected source frames, renders layers over black, retains FFV1 parts of at most 60 frames and per-frame hashes, and produces H.264/AAC MP4 plus HLS. Audio is mixed in bounded chunks with Q20 gains and source-relative linear fades. It retains each lane and the summed final WAV, rejecting lane or final overload. Plain WebVTT captions move with their source ranges. Delivery timestamps floor starts and ceil ends to milliseconds so even a very short retained cue has positive duration; original sample positions remain in the timeline. Unsupported styled/region/markup source captions are refused rather than discarded.

Measured dialogue/narration windows are separate from caption coverage. Cutting a voice window yields an explicit review item even if captions are removed. Trimmed audio whose speech timing is unknown is separately identified. Server-side inspection derives these facts from retained job receipts. The browser groups these warnings by clip and identifies measured lane/source times. Render requests must accept their exact revisions, timeline, source bindings and runtime.

## Browser workflow and qualification boundaries

- The owner editor inspects retained originals, creates named sequences with standard/custom dimensions, and submits append-only timing/settings/history changes. Pending requests retain their original identity in local browser storage. Recovery checks the event chain, keeping its revision distinct from the revision of the whole history. A failed recovery read preserves the request for another retry.
- Unsaved fields stay in their active group until applied or discarded. Stale saves preserve those fields. Split clips keep inherited fades; changing their phase requires valid new lengths and explicit reanchoring. The source bin has a separate discard action. Render completion updates the version picker without allowing an older playback request to replace a newly opened view.
- A local browser fixture with synthetic speech and mock picture exercised source creation, custom dimensions, splits/trims, undo/redo, picture-only insertion, fade safeguards, retained-source alternate creation, speech review, export selection and reopening. Deliberately lost edit/create/render responses recovered without duplicate history events, sequences or jobs. A stale save from another tab retained its draft. Both MP4 players loaded and played, and A/B audio switching worked; this is playback plumbing evidence, not production listening or exact synchronized-comparison qualification.
- Desktop and 390-pixel mobile views were inspected. Mobile comparison stacks vertically, the timeline scrolls within its own viewport, and measured controls met the 44-pixel target. The saved timing overview is read-only; realtime proxies and frame-accurate comparison remain required P8 work.
- Qualify long-duration storage and runtime. Admission now estimates retained originals, canonical lanes, lossless parts, delivery and verification workspace. It bounds an export to 48 GiB and 80,000 files, estimates up to 128 GiB of workspace, checks free disk before processing and monitors working files/free space. - Run full Linux CI and existing benchmark rules for the browser increment, then verify the integrated tree. No Zo rollout has occurred for editorial.

The complete P8 scope also requires proxies, freeze frames and retiming/speed ramps, full track/transition operations, take swaps and synchronized comparisons, proposed alternate assemblies, titles/overlays/adjustment layers, script-linked editing, and OTIO/EDL/FCPXML/AAF interchange. ## Source preparation and recovery

Inspection retains completed film, dialogue, accepted lip-sync and sound receipts as portable JSON values. It derives actual picture dimensions and decoded frame count, plain captions, language, audio lanes and measured speech. Sources declaring burned captions require clean picture before editing.

Preparation keeps owned original files and converts only audio requiring conversion. Existing 48 kHz stereo 24-bit masters and stems are copied exactly. Original measured speech is reconstructed from retained 22.05 kHz PCM without synthesis. Decoding records tail padding/discard, allowing at most 2048 AAC samples or one rounding sample for other formats. Unknown native audio remains identified as unmeasured.

Recovery checks the manifest and every file, then independently reproduces canonical conversions from owned originals using the recorded runtime. A local test removes the original job directory before recovery and verifies that a forged conversion is rejected even when its metadata has been resealed. Permission and cancellation callbacks cover copies, verifiers and subprocess work.

State schema 4 preserves sequences, branches, source receipts and editorial jobs. Older schema declarations containing editorial data are rejected. Portable archive packaging accepts original jobs or verified original copies in a retained editorial export. Both source-scope tests pass locally. ## Bounded picture measurements

A synthetic 30-second, 1920x1080 H.264 source with B-frames was used for 8 and 64 independently sought two-frame clips. Every selected range matched the original decoded frames. Sampled combined Bun/media-process working-set maxima were 174,428,160 and 181,633,024 bytes (about 166 and 173 MiB), observed every 100 ms. Rendering took about 14.5 and 39.3 seconds. These are sampled observations, not hard memory limits or qualification of a one-hour timeline.

## Preview media foundation

The internal preview source index authenticates original decoded frames and canonical 48 kHz stereo 24-bit audio. It creates independently requested pages of up to 60 frames, with JPEG picture bounded to 480 by 270 and only the requested sound lanes. One source decode supplies both the original frame hashes and resized JPEGs, and the selected hashes must match the full source index. Audio-only requests skip picture decoding. The final partial page retains its exact original frame and sample count. Original frame hashes are stored as packed 32-byte values; audio indexing yields periodically and checks current access throughout preparation.

The browser wire format binds source, runtime, recipe, selection, frame addresses and payload hashes. It rejects unknown fields, malformed JPEG envelopes/dimensions, incomplete or reordered lanes, truncated samples and trailing bytes. Each encoded page is limited to 16 MiB, including a header of at most 64 KiB. These hashes establish media integrity; the eventual owner API must separately enforce project ownership and current source permission.

The process-owned disk cache defaults to 512 MiB and 512 ready pages, two page builders, 32 pending pages and 32 active readers. It shares page preparation between consumers while keeping their cancellation and access checks independent. Reads and builds have a 60-second deadline. Unused pages are removed in least-recently-used order for capacity, with 15-minute idle eviction checked on subsequent reads. Cache hits check permission and file identity/hash again. Corrupt entries are removed so the next request can rebuild them. Closing stops queued/running work, waits for producers to stop writing, and removes the owned cache directory. Returned byte buffers belong to callers; HTTP delivery must separately bound outstanding responses. Source preparation/index lifecycle, crash-orphan cleanup and owner routing remain integration work.

The pure preview mixer and full conform share Q20 gain and source-relative fade arithmetic. Preview rounds each lane before summing, rejects lane and final overload, and emits silence without advancing when required pages are missing. It uses reusable bounded scratch buffers and supports the actual caller-supplied block length. Picture demand retains source frame addresses and the export's quantized layer opacity. The reduced-resolution preview does not establish pixel-identical browser composition or display/audio-device synchronization.

Local media tests compare B-frame seeks, original frame hashes and exact stereo samples through page boundaries and the final partial page. A full-conform comparison covers gain, inherited fades, split/slip edits and an independent effects overlap. Cache tests cover shared cancellation, withdrawn access on warm hits, bounded queues/readers, deadlines, least-recently-used eviction, changed/missing files, error preservation and cleanup after producers stop. An isolated browser bundle test omits TextEncoder and structuredClone, matching the restricted AudioWorklet global used during qualification.

A private Chromium 152 fixture decoded all 127 JPEG frames and ran the shared mixer in an OfflineAudioContext at 48 kHz. Its 203,200 samples per channel matched the full-conform PCM checksum exactly after fades, a split and a slip. This verifies browser decoding and offline worklet sample arithmetic. Realtime buffering, seek/stop transport, canvas composition, performance under maximum overlap, owner authorization and browser editor controls are still required before the editor can offer instant preview. No preview endpoint or playback control is exposed by this increment.
