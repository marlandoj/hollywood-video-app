# Picture editorial implementation in progress

The timeline, bounded conform, source preparation, project histories and provider-free render jobs are implemented locally. The owner browser editor connects source inspection, sequences, exact frame controls, saved branches and reviewed export admission. Completed edits support comparison playback, selected export and owner downloads. Saved cuts also support reduced-resolution picture and soundtrack playback with explicit buffering. Sustained realtime performance and the remaining P8 requirements are still open.

The model retains up to 16 immutable source descriptions, 256 clips and 256 markers at 30 fps. Audio addresses 1600 stereo 48 kHz samples per picture frame. Picture has four layers; source mix, dialogue, narration, music, ambience, effects and captions have independent lanes. Source mix can preserve a previously mastered nonlinear soundtrack; separate pre-master stems do not reconstruct that master automatically.

Linked clips share source and timing. Split, move, trim, ripple removal/insertion/reorder, slip, roll and slide operate on linked ranges together. An explicit unlink allows independent audio movement for L/J cuts. Ripple boundaries that cross unrelated clips are refused with a correction message. Split clips retain source-relative fade envelopes, preserving their samples and picture output. Deliberate trim/settings edits reanchor envelopes. A slip can move the virtual envelope origin before source zero; the renderer computes alpha directly from that phase without decoding a padded prefix. Markers move with reordered/rippled ranges.

Edit history records immutable edit nodes and separate cursor events. Undo, redo and branch selection append events; they do not erase abandoned edits. Every node replays its operation from its recorded parent. Expected history revisions detect concurrent writes. The current limit is 1000 events per history. Project services now persist the library and reload it across local consoles. PostgreSQL mutations use the existing project row lock; sequence creation also rechecks current source jobs and artifact metadata. The project library permits 64 receipts, 32 sequences and 64 MiB of metadata. Each sequence binds exact source receipts, so later retention or review records can coexist without rewriting old branches. Replay shares the validated immutable source catalog between nodes.

The conform engine verifies input hashes and decoded picture dimensions/frame counts, independently seeks and verifies selected source frames, renders layers over black, retains FFV1 parts of at most 60 frames and per-frame hashes, and produces H.264/AAC MP4 plus HLS. Audio is mixed in bounded chunks with Q20 gains and source-relative linear fades. It retains each lane and the summed final WAV, rejecting lane or final overload. Plain WebVTT captions move with their source ranges. Delivery timestamps floor starts and ceil ends to milliseconds so even a very short retained cue has positive duration; original sample positions remain in the timeline. Unsupported styled/region/markup source captions are refused rather than discarded.

Measured dialogue/narration windows are separate from caption coverage. Cutting a voice window yields an explicit review item even if captions are removed. Trimmed audio whose speech timing is unknown is separately identified. Server-side inspection derives these facts from retained job receipts. The browser groups these warnings by clip and identifies measured lane/source times. Render requests must accept their exact revisions, timeline, source bindings and runtime.

## Browser workflow and qualification boundaries

- The owner editor inspects retained originals, creates named sequences with standard/custom dimensions, and submits append-only timing/settings/history changes. Pending requests retain their original identity in local browser storage. Recovery checks the event chain, keeping its revision distinct from the revision of the whole history. A failed recovery read preserves the request for another retry.
- Unsaved fields stay in their active group until applied or discarded. Stale saves preserve those fields. Split clips keep inherited fades; changing their phase requires valid new lengths and explicit reanchoring. The source bin has a separate discard action. Render completion updates the version picker without allowing an older playback request to replace a newly opened view.
- A local browser fixture with synthetic speech and mock picture exercised source creation, custom dimensions, splits/trims, undo/redo, picture-only insertion, fade safeguards, retained-source alternate creation, speech review, export selection and reopening. Deliberately lost edit/create/render responses recovered without duplicate history events, sequences or jobs. A stale save from another tab retained its draft. Two exports (158 and 30 frames) completed with zero provider cost. Both MP4 players loaded and played, and A/B audio switching worked; this is playback plumbing evidence, not production listening or exact synchronized-comparison qualification.
- Desktop and 390-pixel mobile views were inspected. Mobile comparison stacks vertically, the timeline scrolls within its own viewport, and measured controls met the 44-pixel target. The saved timing overview is read-only; realtime proxies and frame-accurate comparison remain required P8 work.
- Qualify long-duration storage and runtime. Admission now estimates retained originals, canonical lanes, lossless parts, delivery and verification workspace. It bounds an export to 48 GiB and 80,000 files, estimates up to 128 GiB of workspace, checks free disk before processing and monitors working files/free space. These conservative estimates can refuse a large assembly while preserving its sequence. Sequential parts bound decoded buffering; the full one-hour, high-entropy delivery requirement remains open.
- Run full Linux CI and existing benchmark rules for the browser increment, then verify the integrated tree. No Zo rollout has occurred for editorial.

The complete P8 scope also requires proxies, freeze frames and retiming/speed ramps, full track/transition operations, take swaps and synchronized comparisons, proposed alternate assemblies, titles/overlays/adjustment layers, script-linked editing, and OTIO/EDL/FCPXML/AAF interchange. These remain explicit requirements. P7/P9 production voice, sound and licensing qualification and all other studio waves remain open.

Filter references: [FFmpeg 8.0.1 documentation](https://github.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/blob/n8.0.1/doc/filters.texi), including frame-index trims, alpha fades, overlay end-of-file handling and frame synchronization. The concrete generated filter and runtime fingerprint accompany each local conform.

## Source preparation and recovery

Inspection retains completed film, dialogue, accepted lip-sync and sound receipts as portable JSON values. It derives actual picture dimensions and decoded frame count, plain captions, language, audio lanes and measured speech. Sources declaring burned captions require clean picture before editing.

Preparation keeps owned original files and converts only audio requiring conversion. Existing 48 kHz stereo 24-bit masters and stems are copied exactly. Original measured speech is reconstructed from retained 22.05 kHz PCM without synthesis. Decoding records tail padding/discard, allowing at most 2048 AAC samples or one rounding sample for other formats. Unknown native audio remains identified as unmeasured.

Recovery checks the manifest and every file, then independently reproduces canonical conversions from owned originals using the recorded runtime. A local test removes the original job directory before recovery and verifies that a forged conversion is rejected even when its metadata has been resealed. Permission and cancellation callbacks cover copies, verifiers and subprocess work.

State schema 4 preserves sequences, branches, source receipts and editorial jobs. Older schema declarations containing editorial data are rejected. Portable archive packaging accepts original jobs or verified original copies in a retained editorial export. Both source-scope tests pass locally. Source/history commit `103c0596ff75096eaaa54bf0b0ff015dea833994` passed all Linux checks (561 tests, PostgreSQL/S3 archive recovery, telemetry and benchmark gates). Owner/job commit `18a33ebec18b128e4894176cb891e075f93a64a7` subsequently passed all Linux checks with 567 tests, including independent editorial PostgreSQL/S3 recovery and 19 portable-archive Python tests. Atomic snapshot directory sync is Linux-only.

The local worker/recovery test passes 46 assertions: independent continued rendering after original deletion; revalidation of an interrupted checkpoint without either earlier carrier; zero provider cost; lease ownership; cancellation; current permission denial; selected playback; owner master/timeline/report downloads; and schema 4 snapshot validation. Recovery hashes every retained artifact, reproduces source conversions and the full conform, and checks decoded FFV1 part frames. Matroska container bytes may vary, so decoded-frame equality is authoritative for reproduced parts. No production listening or long-duration qualification is claimed.

Owner API and workspace tests cover required voice-cut review, stale writes and idempotency, rendering, undo, retained-source sequence creation, capacity estimation and actual workspace limits. The expanded owner API test passes 64 assertions, including the browser's timeline/branch response and unaccepted review receipt. ## Bounded picture measurements

A synthetic 30-second, 1920x1080 H.264 source with B-frames was used for 8 and 64 independently sought two-frame clips. Every selected range matched the original decoded frames. Sampled combined Bun/media-process working-set maxima were 174,428,160 and 181,633,024 bytes (about 166 and 173 MiB), observed every 100 ms. Rendering took about 14.5 and 39.3 seconds. These are sampled observations, not hard memory limits or qualification of a one-hour timeline.

## Preview media foundation

The internal preview source index authenticates original decoded frames and canonical 48 kHz stereo 24-bit audio. It creates independently requested pages of up to 60 frames, with JPEG picture bounded to 480 by 270 and only the requested sound lanes. One source decode supplies both the original frame hashes and resized JPEGs, and the selected hashes must match the full source index. Audio-only requests skip picture decoding. The final partial page retains its exact original frame and sample count. Original frame hashes are stored as packed 32-byte values; audio indexing yields periodically and checks current access throughout preparation. Index scratch is capped at 32 MiB/four files, page scratch at 64 MiB/70 files, and the minimal source probe at 64 KiB before JSON parsing.

The browser wire format binds source, runtime, recipe, selection, frame addresses and payload hashes. It rejects unknown fields, malformed JPEG envelopes/dimensions, incomplete or reordered lanes, truncated samples and trailing bytes. Each encoded page is limited to 16 MiB, including a header of at most 64 KiB. Optional ordered `pictureFrames` selects exact frames within a page; older complete-picture pages remain valid. The owner API separately enforces project ownership and current source permission.

The process-owned disk cache defaults to 512 MiB and 512 ready pages, two page builders, 32 pending pages and 32 active readers. It shares page preparation between consumers while keeping their cancellation and access checks independent. Reads and builds have a 60-second deadline. Unused pages are removed in least-recently-used order for capacity, with 15-minute idle eviction checked on subsequent reads. Cache hits check permission and file identity/hash again. Corrupt entries are removed so the next request can rebuild them. Closing stops queued/running work, waits for producers to stop writing, and removes the owned cache directory. The owner API narrows this cache to eight readers and adds the separate delivery bound described below. Crash-orphan cleanup remains required.

The pure preview mixer and full conform share Q20 gain and source-relative fade arithmetic. Preview rounds each lane before summing, rejects lane and final overload, and emits silence without advancing when required pages are missing. It uses reusable bounded scratch buffers and supports the actual caller-supplied block length. Picture demand retains source frame addresses and the export's quantized layer opacity. The reduced-resolution preview does not establish pixel-identical browser composition or display/audio-device synchronization.

Local media tests compare B-frame seeks, original frame hashes and exact stereo samples through page boundaries and the final partial page. A full-conform comparison covers gain, inherited fades, split/slip edits and an independent effects overlap. Cache tests cover shared cancellation, withdrawn access on warm hits, bounded queues/readers, deadlines, least-recently-used eviction, changed/missing files, error preservation and cleanup after producers stop. An isolated browser bundle test omits TextEncoder and structuredClone, matching the restricted AudioWorklet global used during qualification.

A private Chromium 152 fixture decoded all 127 JPEG frames and ran the shared mixer in an OfflineAudioContext at 48 kHz. Its 203,200 samples per channel matched the full-conform PCM checksum exactly after fades, a split and a slip. This verifies browser decoding and offline worklet sample arithmetic. The owner transport described below adds buffering, seeking and composition; maximum-scale realtime performance remains unqualified.

## Owner preview preparation and delivery

The API prepares an ephemeral window of the saved sequence without admitting a render job, creating a review receipt or calling a generation provider. Under `/api/projects/:projectId/editorial/sequences/:sequenceId/preview`, `POST /` accepts `{id, historyRevision, from, frames}` and returns 202 with preparation status. Windows begin on a 60-frame page boundary, extend up to 300 timeline frames and include only originals needed for visible picture or audible lanes. `GET /:id?historyRevision=...` checks access, reports per-source preparation and renews a 60-second session lease. `DELETE` at that path stops the session; owners may also stop a session after changing the saved cut. Failed preparation reports an actionable error, while expired sessions require preparation again.

Ready status supplies authenticated picture source keys and one mixed-soundtrack key. `GET /:id/picture/:sourceId/:from?historyRevision=...&sourceKey=...` returns only visible frames required by that saved window. It omits hidden lower layers and unused frames within the requested source page. Distinct frame selections have distinct cache identities. `GET /:id/audio/:from` with the same identity parameters returns one stereo PCM page mixed on the server. The server uses the full conform's clip order, Q20 gain, inherited fades, lane rounding and overload rejection. Its scratch contains six two-second lane buffers and one source audio page; it never loads all originals' sound pages together or renders the whole timeline for a page request. Empty timeline ranges produce black-picture demand and exact silence.

Every status, cache hit, source read and response delivery checks current ownership, saved history, character permission and retained-carrier availability. Restored original copies remain usable after the original job has expired or been removed. A changed cut or expired/revoked carrier cannot reuse a warm page. Responses are private and uncached, with the packet SHA-256 in `x-hv-preview-sha256`. Delivery uses at most eight active response slots, 64 KiB copied chunks and a 60-second deadline. Slow or unread bodies hold their slots until consumption, cancellation, source expiry or deadline. Media requests and their CORS preflights use the existing artifact rate bucket; status requests retain the API bucket. Clients must bound and validate streamed bodies even when a network response omits Content-Length.

Preparation defaults to eight sessions, two per project, 32 sources, 32 pending sources and two active preparations. It retains at most 32 GiB of ready originals with 128 MiB of binding metadata; admission reserves estimated working bytes against a 96 GiB workspace allowance and checks available disk. A source has a 20-minute preparation deadline, abandoned leases cancel unshared work, and unused ready originals expire after 15 minutes or are evicted for capacity. Source directories left by previous processes remain counted by the workspace guard. These are process bounds plus a shared-directory usage check, not an atomic reservation across a cluster. Crash-orphan reclamation, maximum-duration/high-entropy capacity and a fleet-wide resource policy remain open. Closing drains active preparation and readers before removing only the process's own directories.

Local tests verify retained-copy delivery, stale histories, owner isolation, permission withdrawal on warm pages, cancelled polling, shared preparation, abandoned leases, preparation deadlines and orderly shutdown. A paused TCP client exercises real Bun HTTP backpressure. Media tests compare server PCM byte for byte with full conform, reject overload/missing originals/cancellation, and authenticate sparse B-frame selections. PostgreSQL/S3 coverage also requests picture and mixed sound from retained original copies after original deletion; its qualification requires Linux CI. These checks do not establish production listening, physical A/V synchronization, maximum-scale realtime performance or Zo deployment.

A synthetic two-second mix with 16 indexed originals and 256 overlapping, faded clips exposed a repeated FFmpeg version subprocess in the PCM-read loop. Canonical PCM reads now validate access and their indexed hash; the mix/session boundaries still verify the runtime. Direct signed-24-bit sample reads preserve exact conform arithmetic. On the local Windows host, observed page times for 1/16/64/256 clips were 167/320/600/1891 ms, with a 576,650-byte packet at every size and sampled process RSS below 107 MB. The 256-clip PCM matched full conform exactly. These single-run observations exclude owner/database round trips and network delivery; they do not establish sustained realtime capacity or latency bounds.

## Saved-cut browser playback

The owner editor offers Play/Pause, Stop, a playhead slider, previous/next frame and an exact timeline-frame field. Playback uses the current saved history. Saving an edit, changing sequence/project, leaving the page or starting retained-line/native media playback stops the old preview. Stop remains available during editor requests. Natural completion releases media and its audio context while keeping the final frame visible; Replay begins at frame zero. Unsupported browsers receive an export-playback alternative.

One 48 kHz AudioWorklet consumes stereo PCM24 pages and reports source-sample/context-time anchors. Its clock holds whenever a complete output block lacks sound or composed picture coverage, including a block crossing a page boundary. Device timestamps select historical anchors across buffering, pause and resume. Seeking changes the transport generation, aborts old media requests and rejects late acknowledgements. Each command has its own acknowledgement identity. The browser builds complete reduced-resolution frames by drawing source layers in export order, with the same crop and quantized opacity; padding and picture receive alpha together. Source images may be released after their layer is painted, including a frame with 256 overlapping clips.

The browser admits at most two ten-second source windows, renews ready leases every 20 seconds, retains at most twelve encoded packets/32 MiB of allocated packet storage, 96 decoded source images and 24 completed frames, and gives the worklet at most three two-second audio pages. One picture pump and one independent audio-prefetch task prevent future audio requests from blocking picture coverage. Active fetch/decode buffers are additional bounded working storage, not included in cache counters. Packet bodies are capped at 16 MiB, authenticated, and checked against the complete requested identity even without Content-Length. Metadata requests have a 30-second deadline, media requests 60 seconds and worklet acknowledgements ten seconds. Cleanup retains the captured owner client for lease cancellation after project changes. Cached browser media is reauthorized on the next server access or scheduled renewal; this is not instantaneous push revocation.

Component tests exercise exact PCM, final partial blocks, stale epochs, command identities, delayed-device anchors, bounded/malformed responses, dense layer composition, Stop during setup and rapid seeks with captured-owner cleanup. A private Chromium 152 owner-editor fixture exercised a 660-frame saved cut, silent gaps, two window changes, exact keyboard frame entry and frame stepping. After completion, source windows, packet/image/frame caches and the audio context were all released. Its final composition run sampled maxima of two windows, 9,206,317 encoded bytes, 13 source images and 14 completed frames; it observed about 22 seconds playing and five seconds buffering. Preview controls measured at least 44 pixels in each dimension. Synthetic audio was muted at the output, so this is transport evidence, not a listening or physical synchronization evaluation. JPEG/canvas output is not asserted pixel-identical to full-resolution conform. Sustained realtime playback, maximum-duration browser capacity and deployment qualification remain open.

## Preview request runtime checks

Each session read owns a runtime check that combines repeated FFmpeg version probes within a one-second interval. Every existing owner, saved-history, character-permission and retained-carrier check still executes; none of those authorization results are cached. Runtime validation occurs on the first access, at least once per second when subsequent accesses occur, and freshly after the read's final authorization check before its result is returned. Source and mix processing retain their own runtime checks. The same read-scoped validator remains attached to response-body access checks, so slow delivery still detects a changed runtime. A failed probe permanently fails that read; a new request starts with a new validator. There is no process-wide successful runtime cache.

Three interleaved local comparisons against PR56 measured complete authenticated HTTP responses. Median cold/warm audio request times fell from 1432/744 ms to 987/484 ms (31%/35%); cold/warm picture pages fell from 1467/701 ms to 1046/481 ms (29%/31%). Version probes per request fell from 14/7 to 5/3 for audio and 13/6 to 4/2 for picture. Extra periodic probes remain possible on slower requests. These are synthetic measurements on one Windows host, not latency guarantees or sustained realtime qualification. Tests verify uncached permission checks, changed runtime at result admission, independent subsequent requests and refusal of further bytes during a slow response.

## Preview cache lifetime and workspace checks

Ready packet entries release their producer and cleared timer references, so a retained audio packet no longer keeps its renderer's validated timeline alive. Each authorized playhead window reuses one soundtrack mix while its prepared source instances remain the same. Repeated admission preserves that window; replacing its sources rebuilds the mix. Every use still passes through current session, owner, saved-history, original-permission and runtime checks. Released windows can be collected even while their packets remain cached. Tests verify actual renderer collection, concurrent warm reads, packet equality, window turnover and revoked audio/picture access.

Page builders await a shared asynchronous workspace traversal. It checks the free-space reserve, canonical owned paths, junctions and actual file/byte counts without synchronously walking the entire cache on the API event loop. Concurrent builders share one in-flight scan; later checks reuse its result for at most one second from the scan's start. A failed result remains a failure until a new full scan succeeds. Files removed during ordinary cache eviction are skipped; other filesystem errors and ownership violations fail the check. Caller cancellation releases that caller independently. Closing the cache releases waiting callers and stops further scan I/O; an already-issued read-only filesystem operation can finish afterward. The scan keeps no persistent directory handle. Source preparation and conform retain their existing workspace checks.

These changes preserve storage and authorization requirements; they do not establish sustained realtime playback or maximum-duration, dense-overlap, fleet or production-listening qualification. Those remain separate checks in the full editorial program.
