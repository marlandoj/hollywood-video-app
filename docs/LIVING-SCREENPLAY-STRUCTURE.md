# Canonical screenplay structure and current shot plans

Timeline trims and scene reorders must offer reviewed screenplay changes, and an accepted change must still permit a later forward edit. FULL-SCOPE.md remains authoritative. These foundations establish physical correspondence and planning evidence; they do not yet deliver that owner workflow, screenplay branches or merge review.

`living-script-structure.ts` compiles explicit physical-line insert, replace, delete and move operations against exact screenplay bytes and authoritative protected ranges. It retains CRLF, Unicode, comments, complete inverse evidence and contiguous ancestry. Equal words never restore a replaced line's identity. A pure patch is a proposal, not a committed version or an owner-authorized revision.

`living-script-document.ts` separates stable line, scene and beat identities from current physical addresses and parser positions. Whole-scene moves retain identity. Replacements, role changes, splits, merges and unsupported constructs remain explicit. Original source entries bind through the validated receipt and the complete exact ancestry root; equal text from a different version cannot substitute for that root. Parser limitations remain visible and the full Fountain/editor expansion remains required.

`living-script-reverse-review.ts` derives evidence from full retained before/after libraries and their replayed cut histories. Measured speech, picture coverage, partial or unknown timing, holds, repeated occurrences and caption timing remain distinct. A saved trim does not establish a word boundary or automatically authorize deleting screenplay text.

`living-script-cast-rebind.ts` proposes wardrobe, scene-performance and scene-scoped permission correspondence through stable physical scenes. Original status, expiry, attestation and artistic settings remain intact. New or equal-text replacement scenes do not inherit a scene-scoped grant. Orphaned settings block the complete candidate pending an explicit decision. A legacy cast origin is a review proposal bound to the root document and complete cast snapshot; subsequent compact origin steps replay exact ancestry prefixes and derive their snapshots. The service must persist the accepted origin and select it from current project state, alongside the exact current screenplay and casting. Caller-created seals are not acceptance evidence. The current-access helper checks the pending original address or accepted current address and freshly evaluates expiry and permission; it does not replace reference, carrier, cost or other project checks. Explicit owner cast edits need corresponding durable binding events in the later service integration.

`living-script-shot-plan.ts` reconstructs the original default or authored coverage recipe from validated film receipts. It distinguishes the base planned seed and duration, effective directed inputs, and actual recorded repair seed and speech-adjusted duration. Physical dialogue membership preserves continuation blocks across transitions. Current base-shot materialization reads the canonical screenplay and preserves inherited shot IDs, grouping and seeds during supported whole-scene moves. New, removed, replaced, split, merged or reordered beat membership requires an explicit replanning decision. Missing clocks or render evidence stay visible; a materializable base plan is neither an effective directed plan nor media-reuse authorization. Global allocation must not silently regroup unrelated inherited shots.

`living-script-current-plan.ts` retains a separate current plan with complete ordered scene recipes, original or newly allocated provider-visible identities, seeds and requested durations. Its immutable original receipt remains unchanged. Compact evolution requests replay every accepted carry, revision, creation and retirement against exact document ancestry; the allocation registry retains retired identities. Bootstrap requires the original document with empty ancestry. Later same-text scenes cannot bypass that history. Routine dialogue changes receive deterministic local proposals; changed action/beat coverage requires explicit scene regrouping. Every complete candidate accounts for all material and tier capacity. Physical replacement relations retain many-to-many line ancestry without claiming word timing or performance transfer.

`living-script-current-direction.ts` binds explicit direction to the exact current plan and physical spoken lines, while preserving implicit coverage defaults. A retained physical line can carry its controls when its words, speaker and parenthetical cues agree; local line addresses may change. Replaced, removed or transferred lines require explicit targets, removal or a complete settings replacement. A single reviewed override can transfer to several new lines or another shot. Conflicting transfers, orphaned settings and retired-shot disposal remain explicit. Settings snapshots are correspondence records; the service must select their accepted versions from authoritative project state.

`living-script-current-render.ts` composes current base shots, physically rebound casting, picture controls and line direction. It checks pending or accepted current casting for every scene, preserving immediate revocation/expiry checks. Picture overrides whose scene-intent revision changed require an explicit rebind before this compilation succeeds. Existing `directCast` uses the same implementation and retains legacy behavior. New opaque shot IDs do not require fake legacy direction snapshots. Reference custody, admission, cost and worker publication remain separate required gates.

`shot-render-recipe.ts` extracts the actual worker dispatch fields into one sealed recipe and repair-attempt resolver. The worker supplies its real provider-class classification, stage and resolved dimensions. Reference and anchor metadata remain separate from privately materialized image bytes. Existing current-access, lease and cost checks still execute around dispatch. Provider-visible shot labels, positional repair seeds, requested duration rules, stage-resolved anchor modes and explicit optional dispatch fields retain their previous semantics. Legacy shot-input hashes and render records keep their existing definitions. A future versioned reuse receipt must link the new current input to immutable original execution and media evidence; relabeling a historical record or ignoring an input is insufficient.
