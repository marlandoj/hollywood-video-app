# Screenplay navigation in saved cuts

This local increment connects retained screenplay lines and scenes to their occurrences in a saved editorial timeline. It advances the P8 “Cut from the Script” requirement; bidirectional screenplay edits, screenplay revisions propagated into generated media, and reviewed alternate assemblies remain required program work.

The source index is derived from the original receipts retained by a sequence. It identifies the original screenplay version, physical source lines, scenes, actions, spoken lines and their performed text. Repeated words do not establish identity: line bindings use the original positional and performance evidence. Localized or replaced speech is displayed separately from its original screenplay text. Graphics have no screenplay source. An unavailable timing relationship is stated explicitly.

Measured speech and shot-level coverage are different kinds of evidence. A shot's coverage interval does not establish when an action happened inside its generated picture. Timeline occurrences describe retained source ranges; masks, occlusion, track roles, fades and mixing can change the visible or audible result. Saved-cut preview remains the place to inspect that result.

The projection uses the existing source clock through trims, splits, slips, moves, duplicates, speed ramps and held frames. It retains independent picture and sound ranges and borrowed transition handles. All sample intervals use 48 kHz, with half-open output bounds; navigation frame bounds enclose them. A held source interval may have equal source endpoints while its output interval continues. Held sound follows the existing conform's mute behavior.

Owners open **Screenplay in this cut** to inspect the saved sequence. Source-version labels keep different originals distinct. Selecting an entry outlines its retained timeline ranges and exposes individual occurrences for clip selection and seeking. Spoken entries list measured occurrences before broader coverage. Optional playback following prefers active measured speech, falling back to coverage when measured timing is unavailable; it does not change keyboard focus, screenplay text or timeline history. Unsaved editor fields must be resolved before changing the selected clip.

The owner API binds its response to sequence history, timeline and original receipts. It checks current project permission and retained media carriers before returning screenplay data. Changing the saved branch invalidates the old response. Navigation does not enqueue jobs, synthesize media, alter provider charges or rewrite historical render and archive identities.

The response is bounded to 16,384 entries per source, 100,000 timeline occurrences and 8 MiB. Exceeding a bound produces an error rather than a partial index. These bounds do not establish maximum-scale responsiveness.

Compiler checks cover physical lines with comments, parentheticals and transition-interrupted dialogue; repeated speech; accepted coverage; local dubbed dialogue, sound copies and accepted synthetic lip-sync/narration; graphics without screenplay bindings; and actual final-assembly overlap. Caption links require exact reproduction of the complete ordered retained caption cue list, including its rounding and chunking. Unmeasured block captions and unmatched historical recipes remain explicitly unbound.

Projection checks compare ramp boundaries against enumerated source-clock membership and cover holds, independent lanes, duplicate ranges, borrowed transition handles, two distinct source versions and capacity rejection. Actual owner HTTP checks exercise module delivery, stale history, current screenplay changes, cancellation, permission withdrawal, changing carriers and independently retained exports. Navigation creates no media job or provider charge.

The integrated owner browser loaded distinct repeated lines, sought their measured picture timing, followed playback, preserved unsaved fields, searched retained text, and refreshed correctly after duplication and undo. The 390-pixel layout had no horizontal overflow and visible controls met the 44-pixel target minimum. This used a six-second synthetic original and its duplicated cut. It does not qualify maximum-scale responsiveness, production listening, or every timing operation in the browser; those additional timing cases have automated projection coverage. Full CI remains required before merge. No deployment or completion of the full editorial or studio scope is claimed.
